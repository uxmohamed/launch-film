# Claude Cowork — feature film (162 frames ≈ 81 s)

## 1. One-line concept
Cowork is Claude's mode for scheduled, multi-tool background tasks. The spine is **one QBR, followed across a day**:
1. The user writes one long prompt the night before, and Claude plans and schedules two tasks.
2. The laptop closes and Claude works overnight.
3. At 8:14 the phone shows a brief, and she edits the drafted email at 8:19–8:21.
4. "Acme QBR ended", so Claude runs the recap.
5. Back at the laptop, she types "do this for every client meeting".
6. "Your thinking sets the direction. Claude keeps it moving."

The opening line "Claude keeps working, even when you're not" bookends with the closing "Claude keeps it moving."

## 2. Visual world
- **Three grounds, each a state of the user:**
  - **Cornflower blue** (#6b9bd1-ish) with a pattern of large overlapping hairline circles in lighter blue, at ~1.5 px and ~25% contrast: "user present" (#1–26, #51–55, #73–118 behind the phone, #133–135, #149).
  - **Warm off-white** (#f5f3ee) for Claude's in-app thinking (#27–50, #136–148).
  - **A dithered halftone cloud** (peach/salmon dots on blue or white, like a sunrise seen through a screen) for "Claude working while you're away" (#58–72, #119–131, #150–155).
- **Type:** Claude's serif (Tiempos-like) for the thesis lines (~2.2% of frame height on the blue, ~3.5% for "Got it." and the tagline), and a UI sans inside the product. In macro, input text is enlarged to **~5% of frame height** (#11–17).
- **UI staging:**
  - a floating frameless input card on the blue ground (#8–26, ~28% of frame width, then ~75%);
  - macro crops that follow the caret (#11–20);
  - in-app content full-bleed on the off-white ground with no window chrome (#27–50);
  - one orientation wide of the full window (#51–53) that keeps pulling back into a **flat illustrated laptop** (cream bezel, no shading, #54–57);
  - a **flat illustrated phone** (cream bezel with a thin orange rim, #73–118).

  The devices are flat vector, not photoreal, so they belong to the brand world.
- **Accent logic:**
  - salmon highlight = "task phrase" in the prompt and "needs attention" in the brief;
  - blue highlight = "a placeholder you must fill" and "text selected for edit";
  - orange = Claude (the spark, the send button, and the status pill when "Finishing up").
- **Atom:** the orange Claude spark (✳). It's the thinking indicator (#27–50), the status pill icon (#58–72, #119–131), the collapsed state (#71–72), a notification icon (#74), and the logo (#156).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Text → input card** (#6→#7): the thesis line is replaced by an empty card of the same centre, then chrome fills. A "prompt box as stage".
- **Caret-follow macro ↔ card pull-back** (#10→#11, #20→#21): scale ~3× in and back out.
- **Click → hard cut to the product's thinking ground** (#26→#27): the cause is the send click, and the ground colour flips from blue to off-white.
- **Scroll / drift continuity** inside the thread (#29–50).
- **Pull-back through three containers** (#51–55): content → window → laptop. The laptop is revealed *around* the window the viewer was already in.
- **Lid-close = time-skip** (#56–58): the device's physical action is the transition, and the screen's warm light survives as the halftone cloud. This one is new.
- **Halftone cloud as "away" state** (#58–72, #119–131): the ground itself says "you're not here".
- **Status pill collapses to the atom** (#71–72) → the phone rises (#73).
- **Phone rise / sink** (#73, #118–119) and **laptop rise** (#132) from the bottom edge: the devices enter and exit vertically like props lifted into frame.
- **Clock as time-lapse:** 8:14 (#73) → 8:19 (#97) → 8:21 (#105) on the status bar. Time passes without a single title card.
- **UI slides away to reveal the tagline** on the ground behind (#149), then a cross-grade to off-white.
- **Spark → wordmark** (#156→#157).

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The **ghost arrival state** is used both at the open and in the logo ("Claud" + ghost "e", #157). It's the same arrival grammar at both ends of the film.
- The input card appears **empty for exactly one frame** (#7) before its chrome fills.
- The **salmon highlight is semantic**: every highlighted phrase in the prompt becomes a scheduled task or deliverable later ("Build my prep doc" → the prep doc; "Draft the renewal email" → the email; "Run the prep at 8am" → the 8:14 notification; "After the meeting…" → the recap). The prompt is the film's table of contents.
- The laptop's **screen colour tracks the time of day**: blue (day), a sunset gradient as the lid closes (#55–57), warm → blue again when it reopens (#132–133).
- **Satellite tool chips use depth of field**: the active one is sharp, the others blurred ~6–10 px, so they read as parallel work.
- The **status pill turns orange** only at "Finishing up your task…" (#67): colour marks the state change.
- **Diagonal stagger** for the recap steps (#127–130), each row ~5% further right, then everything snaps flush-left at completion (#131). The diagonal is "in progress", the aligned list is "done".
- **Two kinds of highlight colour** in the phone section: blue for "needs you" (a placeholder or a selection), salmon for "needs attention".
- **The human edits the AI's draft on camera** twice (#101–103 fill the blank, #111–115 rewrite a sentence). It's a deliberate trust beat.
- The provenance footer ("Built from 4 email threads · 18 messages…") is left readable for ~2 s (#93–95).
- Real-feeling, specific data: names (Dana Whitfield, Marcus, Jordan, Helena Ortiz), 11:47 quote time, "tickets are down to 9", "Opus 4.8 High".

## 6. Pacing curve
- A calm 3 s type open, then an input-typing section with a steady, readable cadence (0–13 s).
- The click → "Got it." (13.5 s) → a medium-density in-app run of rows and cards every 0.5–1 s (14–25 s).
- A big breath with the pull-back and lid-close (25–29 s), then a slow overnight cloud (7.5 s).
- The phone act is the longest reading passage (10 s scroll plus the edits, 36–60 s).
- A second cloud (6 s) and the laptop return with the recap (66–75 s).
- The tagline (3 s) and the logo (3.5 s).
- The rhythm alternates "user present / dense UI" and "user away / sparse cloud" three times. The climax isn't a camera shout but the emotional payoff line "Let's do this for every client meeting" (#143–147).

## 7. What makes it premium
- **One real object followed for a whole day** (the Acme QBR), with honest intermediate states: integrations checked, tasks scheduled, drafts unsent, placeholders to confirm.
- **The environment changes with who is present** (blue/off-white/halftone), and devices are flat brand illustrations that open, close, rise and sink. It's time told through props, not captions.
- **Every highlight in the prompt pays off later.** The copy of the prompt is the storyboard.
- **Restraint:** no camera shout at all. The biggest move is a 1.5× pull-back into a laptop.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Prompt as table of contents.** Highlight each task clause in the prompt as it's typed (a marker box at ~40% tint of the accent, landing on the clause's last character). Pay off each highlighted clause, in order, later in the film.
2. **Lid-close time-skip.** Pull back from the in-app view into a flat laptop (1.5–2 s), warm the screen gradient, then close the lid over ~1 s (screen foreshortens to a line). Let the screen's glow persist as the next ground (a halftone cloud) for the "while you're away" act.
3. **Away-state cloud with status pill.** On a dithered halftone glow, one centred pill (atom icon + a verb-ing label that types with shimmer) cycles 3–4 labels at ~1.5–2 s each; satellite tool chips at 3 depths with 6–10 px blur on the inactive ones; the pill turns accent-coloured on the final state, then collapses to the atom tile.
4. **Status-bar clock as timeline.** Advance the phone clock between beats (8:14 → 8:19 → 8:21) instead of title cards.
5. **Diagonal-stagger → align.** Step rows enter each offset +5% x and 0.5 s later, then snap into a flush-left column when all are done (~0.3 s ease-in-out).
6. **Human edit beat.** Macro into the AI draft, select a placeholder or sentence (blue selection), replace it with typed human text at reading speed (~8 chars per 0.5 s). 2–3 s, twice per film.
7. **Devices as rising props.** Flat illustrated devices enter and exit by rising from or sinking below the bottom edge (~40% of frame in 0.5 s, ease-out in, ease-in out), never sliding sideways.
