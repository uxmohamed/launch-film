# Crosscut M — Perplexity Computer, OpenAI Images 2.0, Herman Miller Gaming (COYL), Figma Shaders, Affinity Creative Freedom

The group is mixed: one agentic-AI SaaS demo (Perplexity), two creative-tool launches (Figma, Affinity), one AI-model brand film (OpenAI) and one physical-product CGI film (Herman Miller). Runtimes are 50–69 s.

## Recurring patterns
1. **Same-subject, many-treatments riffle.** This is the dominant device in four of five films:
   - OpenAI: one chameleon across ~60 scenes;
   - Figma: one flower in three shaders (#2–#5), and the Figma logo in ~8 shaders (#86–#95);
   - Affinity: one chair, dog, mountain or word re-rendered 5–8 ways (#46–#74);
   - Herman Miller: one desktop in four finishes (#14–#19), and one desk in four configurations (#106–#112).

   The subject stays fixed in position and scale, and only the material, style or mode changes, at ~0.5 s per variant. **This is how creative and generative tools prove range**, replacing the manual's single-object *lineage* (which changes form over time) with **identity under variation**.
2. **The output is the film's texture.** Figma's title, UI edges, outro text and logo are all shaded. OpenAI's every word is a generated image. Affinity's closing marks are its own brushes. When the product makes pixels, the film has no neutral ground plate: the product *is* the ground.
3. **Logo run through the product (the ending).** Figma puts the logo through shaders, then clean (#86–#96). Affinity's strokes converge into the app icon (#85–#88). OpenAI's inline image collapses back into the lock-up (#114–#115). The brand mark is the final subject of the product's own verb.
4. **Inline image or icon as a word in the lock-up.** Perplexity's Finder icon between "Perplexity" and "Mac App" (#7, #127). OpenAI's "ChatGPT [image] Images 2.0" (#1, #115). Both reprise the icon in the CTA with the content swapped or re-born.
5. **One accent colour with a job.** Herman Miller red = the cable, the height indicator, the brand strap. Perplexity teal = active/selected, plus red only for PII. Affinity lime = "free". Figma blue = typed/new. OpenAI is the exception: the white ground is the only constant.
6. **A single persistent UI control as the continuity anchor.** Affinity's Vector/Pixel/Layout pill (#46–#62). Figma's floating shader panel or pill stack. Perplexity's prompt card. None of the three SaaS/tool films shows the full editor for more than 2 s (Figma #16–#19, Perplexity #27–#28 inside a laptop, Affinity only at the very end on a MacBook).
7. **Live parameter as transition.** Figma's slider drags distort the art on the same frames (#26–#28). Herman Miller's height readout ticks while the camera jump-cuts (#58–#62, #91–#94). Perplexity's meters tick (#99–#103). A changing number or value is the carrier when the camera isn't continuous.

## Contradictions
- **Type as narrator vs. no narrator.**
  - Perplexity uses a book-serif narrator caption over black between every proof (#81–#91, #116–#123), the classic manual approach.
  - OpenAI has **zero overlay type**: every word is diegetic inside the images.
  - Herman Miller uses a corner-flush chapter label plus a HUD margin (a spec sheet).
  - Affinity changes typeface every beat (slab, geometric, editorial serif, swash display, book serif).

  Rule of thumb: generative and creative tools let the output speak (diegetic or multi-voice type); agent/infra products need a narrator.
- **Tiny vs. huge type.** Perplexity's captions are ~4.5% of frame height with no shout. Affinity opens with "SUBSCRIPTION" at 95% of frame width and repeats 12–20% shouts. Herman Miller's labels are 6%. The manual's "tiny type + one shout" fits only Perplexity (and it has no shout at all).
- **Device frames.**
  - Perplexity *opens* on hardware (a rim-lit Mac mini and MacBook), because the platform (local compute on your Mac) is the point, and pushes through the screen to go frameless (#28→#29).
  - Affinity *ends* on a MacBook, as a reveal that the marks live in the app.
  - Figma and OpenAI never show a device.

  This is consistent with the manual's device rule: a device appears when the platform is the claim.
- **Ground stability.** OpenAI keeps pure white for 60 s. Affinity and Figma swap ground colour almost every shot. Perplexity uses exactly two grounds (streak world → flat charcoal) with one diegetic break.
- **Speed.** Perplexity is keynote-slow (an 8 s typing shot, 11 s of end cards). OpenAI, Affinity and Figma run at a 0.5 s-per-image metronome for 60–80% of the runtime. The fast films stay readable because nothing is to be read except one word per image (OpenAI) or the fixed pill/subject (Affinity, Figma).

## Numbers and ranges (≈0.5 s per sampled frame)
- **Riffle cadence:** 0.5 s per variant (all five). Holds on phrase ends or story beats run 1–2.5 s (OpenAI #27–#31: 2.5 s; "Then he met" 1.5 s).
- **Same-subject riffle length:** 4–10 variants (Figma logo 8–10, Affinity "Free" 7, dog 8, Herman Miller finishes 4, configurations 4).
- **Word-per-image cadence (OpenAI):** 0.5 s per word, with ~1 s on nouns and names ("Cam", "LUCY", "NOTICE"). About 30 words over 60 s.
- **Caption on-screen time (Perplexity):** 2.5–4 s per two-line caption. The ghost-word arrival state lasts ~0.5 s at ~25% opacity plus a slight drop.
- **Typing speed:** Perplexity's long prompt runs at ~8–10 words per 0.5 s once going, with the first word slower. Figma's prompt runs at ~12 chars per 0.5 s with a 5–6 char blue "hot" tail.
- **Full-UI orientation:** 1–2 s, once per film (Figma #16–#19, Perplexity #27–#28).
- **Shrink funnel (Affinity #11–#15):** 25–40% scale-down per 0.5 s cut, from 75% to 12% of frame width.
- **Pull-backs:** Figma output → website ~2.5× in 1.5 s (#59→#60). Herman Miller tray → full desk ~4× in 3 s (#36→#41). Affinity "Craft" ~4× over 2 s (#51→#54). Perplexity status → window ~0.75× (#103→#104).
- **Ticking values:** 4–5 uneven steps over 2–2.5 s (HM 23.8 → 25.3 → 27.9 → 29.7 → 31.3; Figma Offset 85 → 37 → 23 → 20; Perplexity Tokens 1555 → 1602 → 1629).
- **Pill stack offset (Figma):** each pill +3% x/y. The stack count ramps 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 7+ across ~25 s.
- **End holds:** Perplexity 3.5 s wordmark (plus 7 s of pre-logo cards); OpenAI 2 s logo only; Figma 3 s; Affinity 3.5 s; Herman Miller only ~1 s logo after a 1.5 s tagline.
- **Dim-to-exit (Perplexity):** the whole composition drops to 40–60% luminance for 1 frame after the final click (#58, #80, #86, #115).

## vs. the manual

### (a) Techniques NOT in the manual
1. **Same-subject treatment riffle (identity under variation).** Fix a subject's position and scale; swap its material, style or mode every 0.5 s for 4–10 variants; end on the canonical version. It's the creative-tool counterpart to the morph chain. (Affinity #55–#74, Figma #86–#96, HM #14–#19, OpenAI throughout.) The manual's "fixed-layout slideshow" swaps *content*; this swaps *rendering* of the same content.
2. **Diegetic word riffle.** Each word of the sentence is typeset inside a different photographed or generated surface (sign, mug, neon, fabric), one per 0.5 s, with no overlay captions. (OpenAI #6–#18, #40–#48, #103–#112.)
3. **Shape-shifting stage window.** On an empty ground, the only "camera" is the aspect ratio and size of the image window per cut (2.35:1 for words, a square for character beats, portrait for texture beats). (OpenAI #22–#26, #95–#97.)
4. **Inline thumbnail ↔ stage bookend.** The image in the "Brand [img] Product" lock-up stretches from the centre into the stage; at the end the last image collapses back into the slot with different content. (OpenAI #2–#4 / #114–#115.) Related: the pixel-to-icon inline word (Perplexity #126–#127).
5. **Shrinking-history funnel.** Successive artefacts, each 25–40% smaller, converge to the point where the product's primitive is born. (Affinity #11–#20.)
6. **Mode-switch pill as a fixed anchor over re-rendering art.** (Affinity #46–#62.) It's the UI equivalent of Harvey's centred dot, but *semantic*: the anchor is the cause of every change.
7. **Cascading effect-pill stack.** A countable depth visual for "combine N things", with the count escalating per act. (Figma #39–#43, #70–#78.)
8. **Hover-driven preview swap.** The cursor hovering picker thumbnails *is* the cut; the background changes on the same frame. (Figma #50–#56.)
9. **Rim-lit hardware silhouette.** The device body stays at ~5% luminance with only its bevels lit, and the highlight travels around the perimeter over 3 s in place of a push. (Perplexity #12–#26.)
10. **Push through the device screen as the chapter break.** (Perplexity #27→#29.) It's the manual's "flat layout → device" run in reverse, and it switches the ground plate diegetically.
11. **Corner-flush chapter label plus HUD margin.** Heavy caps at ~6% height, 1% from a corner, rotating corner per chapter, with a 4-block micro-text spec margin. (Herman Miller.)
12. **Perforation → bokeh → ribbon → coil tunnel.** A light-based object-lineage chain from a texture to the hero part, followed by an axis flip on the same object. (HM #24–#31.)
13. **Pain montage of retro dialogs.** Multiplying error windows (2 → 8 → 12 in 1.5 s) with joke titles, as the "before" state. (Affinity #27–#31.) It's a before/after that the manual would have forbidden as a cut, but here the before is an argument, not a product state.
14. **Door-swing plane type pivot.** A rotating plane carries statement A, then its inversion B, and becomes a vector path with anchors. (Affinity #32–#36.)
15. **Dim-to-exit.** A 1-frame luminance drop to ~50% after the final click, before the hard cut to type. (Perplexity #58, #80, #115.) The manual has "dim before chapter change" only in crosscut notes, not as a rule.
16. **Undersize-rise word.** A tagline word enters at ~60% size, raised, then drops to the baseline. (HM "DIAL IT UP" #113–#114.) It's the inverse of the manual's oversize-settle.
17. **Headline recolours on completion.** Words append in white, and the whole line turns brand colour when the last word lands. (Figma #11–#12.)
18. **Type processed by the product.** The closing tagline is smeared, halftoned and dot-matrixed into the logo sequence. (Figma #79–#85.)

### (b) Contradictions of the manual's rules or numbers
- **"Follow one object, which changes form."** OpenAI, Affinity and Figma follow a subject that *doesn't* change form: it changes *rendering*. And Affinity follows a new subject every 2–4 s (chair → "Craft" → dog → mountain → "Free"). For creative tools, lineage is replaced by the riffle, and continuity comes from the fixed subject and anchor pill.
- **"Pace for reading; staggers 0.4–0.5 s; beats 1.5–3 s."** OpenAI, Affinity and Figma run 60–80% of their runtime at 0.5 s per cut. The manual's "accelerate only where nothing is read" still holds, but these films make the riffle the *main body*, not a rapid section.
- **"Type is small (1.5–3.5%); one shout per film."** Affinity shouts 10+ times (#2–#3 at 20% of frame height, "Change them", "Free" ×5, "Forever", "Craft"). Herman Miller's labels are 6% on every chapter. Perplexity is ~4.5% with **no** shout at all. None matches the "tiny type + one shout" pattern.
- **"One type family plus mono."** Affinity uses 5+ faces deliberately (slab, rounded geometric, editorial serif, swash display, book serif). OpenAI uses as many faces as there are words. Multi-voice type is the claim for creative tools.
- **"One accent colour at a time."** Figma's and Affinity's grounds change hue almost every shot (orange, lime, magenta, olive, peach). Only the UI accent (Figma blue, Affinity lime) is constant.
- **"UI lives on a persistent ground plate; never full-bleed."** Figma's and Affinity's artwork is full-bleed, with the UI panel floating on top of the *output* (Figma #25–#36). The output is the ground.
- **"Never cut before → after."** Affinity hard-cuts from a brush-stroke heart to the error-window chaos (#26→#27) and from "Blame them" to "Change them". The before is rhetorical, and it works because it's framed as an argument.
- **"End by subtraction; hold 2–5 s."** Herman Miller holds the logo only ~1 s after a 1.5 s tagline (#113–#117). Perplexity's ending instead runs ~11 s across three cards (thesis → "Now available" → logo), which is 16% of the runtime.
- **"Arrival state: pick one per film."** Affinity and Herman Miller each use several (Affinity: hard cut, per-word append, type-on mid-word, late-period tail; HM: letter-by-letter, random-letter fill "I NA URE", space-snap "EFFORTLESSCONTROL", undersize-rise).

### (c) Manual claims strongly confirmed
- **Rebuild UI; show the full window 1–3 times for ≤1.5–5 s.** Figma shows the editor once for 2 s (#16–#19); Perplexity shows it once for 1 s (#27–#28), inside a laptop; Affinity shows it only at the end on a device. Everything else is a floating macro panel or pill at 25–45% of frame width.
- **Device-frame rule.** A device appears only when the platform is the claim (Perplexity's "local model on your Mac" opens on hardware; Affinity's MacBook reveal says "this is a real app"). Figma and OpenAI stay frameless.
- **Arrival state for the newest word.** Perplexity's ghost word at ~25% opacity plus a drop (#5, #14, #81, #116, #119, #124). Figma's blue hot tail on typed characters (#21–#23), which is exactly the "hot text" verb transition in the brand's UI colour.
- **Numbers tick in uneven, decelerating steps.** HM 23.8 → 31.3 in 5 steps; Perplexity tokens and meters; Figma slider values.
- **Real micro-states kept.** Perplexity's "Skip file → Submit" relabel (#77→#78), the model label persisting, the toggle flip; Affinity's "Sit Happen|s" caret; Figma's selection handles.
- **Shimmer instead of a spinner.** Perplexity status lines (#59–#64) and the Privacy Gate pill's teal band (#67–#69).
- **Prompt → pill → result.** Perplexity: prompt → status line on empty ground → pill → pill morphs into a dialog (#58–#70). Figma: prompt → effect panel over output (#24→#25).
- **Bookend the film.** OpenAI's lock-up with a swapped thumbnail; Affinity's strokes at the open and close; HM's "COYL GAMING DESK" typed at #5–#7 and again at #109–#110; Perplexity's inline Finder icon at #7 and #127.
- **Show the verb, not before/after.** Figma's live slider scrubbing, and Affinity's dog stretched and liquified inside transform boxes, both animate the transformation itself.
- **Pull-back from macro to context as payoff.** Figma #59→#60, HM #36→#41, Affinity #51→#54 and #83→#87.
