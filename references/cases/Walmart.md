# Walmart — analysis (125 frames, ~62 s) — brand identity film

## 1. One-line concept
A rebrand reveal for Walmart's refreshed identity (Spark, Everyday Sans, palette, illustration). It is not a product demo. The spine is a **comparative-adjective chant ("Bolder / Brighter / … / Happier / Smarter / Better") → "Welcome to your Walmart" → Spark**. It is punctuated by the Spark as a fixed-position anchor and by a history montage that ties the new identity to 1962–2025.

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** flat "True Blue" (#0053e2-ish) full-frame. Chapter cards swap to yellow (#ffc220) and navy (#001e60). Many of these cards are **inset rounded rectangles with a ~2% white margin** (#16–18, #33–34, #48, #58), so the brand colour sits *on* a white sheet like a card, not full-bleed.
- **Type:** Everyday Sans (a humanist grotesk), used in three roles:
  - chant words, bold, ~15% of frame height and centred ("Bolder", "Brighter", "Happier");
  - light-weight tagline "Save Money. Live Better." (#49, second line tinted light blue);
  - the wordmark.

  The scale shout is a giant crop "Bol" at ~80% of frame height (#13), before the pull-back to "Bolder" (#14).
- **Imagery:** a mix of four very different sources, all colour-graded to the palette:
  - brand photography (associates in blue and yellow, #3, #62);
  - OOH/environmental (murals, building signs, billboard, trucks, #4–5, #24–26, #91–95);
  - flat editorial illustration with blue shadows (#76–82);
  - 3D renders (tumbling cylindrical speakers, #44–47);
  - archival scans (Walton's storefront, the 1980 Wal-Mart World newsletter, the Automotive sign, 25th-anniversary artwork, #51–57);
  - product UI (a laptop "Customize your Spark" tool, #29–30; app dashboard, #71–75; phones, #64–68, #88–90, #96–97).
- **UI staging:** a flat front-on phone or laptop on a blue ground with a thin navy bezel. The dashboard is built as **flat, rounded cards on the blue ground with no window** (#71–75).
- **Motifs:** the Spark (six rounded petals) at the exact frame centre; rounded-rectangle cards with a consistent ~3% corner radius; the palette as vertical stripes (#38–40).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Fixed-position atom match cut** (#21–28, #42, #118–124): the Spark sits at the frame centre in every context, so the cuts read as one object moving through the world.
- **Scale-shout pull-back** (#13→14): a crop so tight it's abstract, then the whole word is revealed.
- **Inset card carousel push** (#18→19): cards slide sideways with a visible white gutter, which shows the card system itself.
- **Palette-stripe wipe** (#38–41): the brand palette *is* the wipe.
- **Card-rise cover** (#47→48): a navy rounded card rises from below to take over the frame.
- **Full-bleed → device scale-down** (#66→68).
- **Scene → grid tile → next tile expands** (#75→77→83). This nests the scenes (manual §7) and works in both directions.
- **Frame-kept history** (#31→33): an identical inset frame and "1980" label, with the photo swapped for the logotype.
- **Macro scale-out to the end card** (#118→119).

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The chant cards carry **different ground colours per word**: Bolder (blue), Brighter (yellow inset), Happier (blue), Smarter (navy), Better (yellow). The palette is shown as rhythm.
- In "Welcome to your Walmart", the final word is in *darker* navy on blue: an arrival state that de-emphasises instead of highlighting (#117).
- "Tech-" appears first with only its hyphen, and the "powered" slot is empty except for a single dot (#85). It's a word-in-progress state.
- The stacked "Walmart" reel uses four tints of blue so that each row reads as a colour step.
- The name badges show tenure numbers ("25 years") as proof, and every badge is framed identically.
- The "aaaaa" row shows the font's weight axis, a type-specimen beat (#36–37).
- Inset cards have ~2% white margins and ~3% corner radius. That same radius appears on the dashboard cards, the phone and the grid tiles.

## 6. Pacing curve
Relentless 0.5 s cutting (a brand-montage tempo) for most of the film. Breaths come only at the construction beats (#19–23), the tagline (#48–50), the dashboard build (#71–75) and the end. There's no single climax; the density peaks in the archive (#51–58) and the card wall (#98–102). The ending subtracts: chant words → one line of small type → the Spark alone for a 3 s hold → 0.5 s of black.

## 7. What makes it premium
- The identity system is demonstrated by the edit itself: palette as wipe, radius as card system, font weight as a specimen, Spark as a match-cut anchor.
- Real-world placements (watch, bag, billboard, drone) prove that the mark scales, which is the brand's argument.
- History is folded in with the same frame language (inset card + year label), so heritage and new feel continuous.
- Only one type family is used, across archival and new material.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Centred-atom placement riffle.** Put the mark at an identical centre and size across 5 real-world contexts, 0.5 s each.
2. **Palette-stripe wipe.** Five brand-colour bars (each ~20% of frame width) cross R→L in ~1 s. The last bar becomes the next ground.
3. **Adjective chant on alternating grounds.** One bold word per card (~15% of frame height). Rotate the ground among 3 brand colours. 0.5–1 s per word.
4. **Inset card system.** Show brand colour as a rounded card with a ~2% white margin. Transition by sliding cards sideways with the gutter visible.
5. **Crop-to-word pull-back.** Open on a letter crop at ~80% of frame height, then pull out ~5× in 1–2 frames to reveal the word.
6. **Scene ↔ grid tile.** The current scene shrinks into one cell of a 3×3 collage, the grid reflows, and another cell expands to full frame and becomes the next chapter card.
7. **Macro-mark scale-out ending.** Cut to the mark at ~8× (only petal tips visible), snap to its final size, and hold 3 s.
