# Burp — "Make anything move."

248 frames, about 124 s at 0.5 s per frame. (The in-film stopwatch in #214–227 ticks about 0.42 s per frame, and the timeline playhead in #200–203 about 0.53 s per frame, so the true runtime is roughly 105–125 s.) This is a launch film for an AI motion studio.

## 1. One-line concept
Burp is a browser studio where AI agents (Image Generator, Video Generator, Motion Designer, Director) turn a prompt, a URL or a set of files into animated ads and posters. The spine is:
- "For every post / Every advertisement / Every launch — The moving version is the one that works."
- Then the problem: pro motion tools and a Slack message saying "Nvm, let's just have it as an image."
- Then the rewind, burp.com, and the demo.
- Then the payoff: "One Studio. Nothing left in another tab → Make anything move."

The thesis is literally the verb: **static → moving**.

## 2. Visual world
- **Three registers.**
  1. The white clean-room for type and logo (#1–13, #37–49, #228–248).
  2. A dark cinematic "old way" register: macro, shallow-DOF live-action B-roll of hands on a mouse and keyboard, real After Effects-style editors with RGB-split footage, a graph editor, and macOS notifications over an orange wallpaper (#14–36, #43–44).
  3. The Burp product world: a white frameless app on **solid saturated grounds that change per project** (electric purple #153–183, pale green #189, dark green #195, orange #210, tan #212, green #214). Behind the prompts sits a pixel-mosaic gradient with a white grid (#93–103).
- **Type.** A rounded geometric grotesk. Captions are about 3–3.5% of frame height, in brand blue (#2–9) or black with a grey arrival state (#10–12). The final line uses a **multi-stop gradient fill across words** (orange → purple, "Make anything move.", #238–241).
- **Logo.** A rainbow semicircle (bowl or smile). It appears first as a line-art "◡" (#45), then fills with vertical rainbow bands (#46–49).
- **UI staging.** Four modes:
  - macro crops tilted about 5–10° in perspective (the prompt box #81–92, panels #142–152, #163–167, #184–188, #198–206);
  - a flat full app window at about 85% of frame width on a coloured ground (#153–162, #176–183, #189–197, #207–217);
  - a website screenshot at full bleed with the prompt card floating over it (#104–113);
  - outputs shown full frame as posters (#119–141).

  The RGB chromatic aberration (about 1% of frame width) on posters and edges is part of the look, not an accident (#50–55, #120–123).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Relayout slot** (#4→#5→#7). Text and collage swap sides on each new list word. It's a slot reel where the *layout* changes, not the word position.
- **Hard cut from white to dark** (#13→#14). It's a register change: "the old way".
- **Push-to-message** (#28→#29), followed by a pull back (#33→#34).
- **Rewind** (#37–39). The film's own opening plays backwards with smear. It means "start over, the right way".
- **Search-bar CTA** (#40–42). A URL is typed into Google as the bridge into the brand.
- **Pixel-fill logo build** (#45–49).
- **Prompt empties → split layout** (#60→#61). Sending the prompt re-lays out the frame as chat plus canvas.
- **Blur → site behind card** (#102–104). The URL in the prompt *becomes* the backdrop, and the card shrinks away to the corner.
- **Scan boxes** (#108–112). Outlines hop between elements. It's the "fetch" verb.
- **Still → animated** (#70→#71). The poster finishes generating and starts moving. This is the film's verb transition.
- **Flat ↔ tilted macro** (#152→#153, #162→#163, #197→#198). The alternation acts as punctuation between flat orientation shots and angled detail.
- **Ground-colour cuts** in the editor tour. The ground colour is taken from the current project's poster.
- **Mosaic → white bento → type → logo → URL.** This is the subtraction ending.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The prompt's rainbow border is the logo's palette, travelling around the box (a different colour at each corner, per frame, #83–92). The "AI is active" state is branded rather than generic glow.
- The generation percentage is **linear** (about +11% per 0.5 s), unlike eased counters. The image denoise follows it.
- In the old-way montage, the RGB-split footage *is* the content being edited (a FIFA logo in AE), so the glitch is diegetic.
- The notification stack is real-world time pressure. The joke lands on text you must read, so the camera gives it 2.5 s and a 2.5× push.
- The mosaic ground behind "+ Add Files" re-shuffles its colours every frame (#93–97), so the background stays alive during a static macro.
- The logo spinner is used as the agent's "planning" indicator (#115–118), so the brand mark does UI duty.
- The stopwatch tile carries its count from the flat app into the mosaic (#217 → #218), so continuity comes from a running number.
- The final tagline's gradient shifts as the last word lands: "Make any" goes orange and "thing move" purple (#240 → #241).

## 6. Pacing curve
- A 6.5 s opening thesis (type-led, calm, with a fast collage).
- An 11 s old-way montage (0.5–1 s cuts), then a 4.5 s breath on the joke.
- A 1.5 s rewind, and the burp.com/logo reveal at about 18–27% of runtime.
- The product demo from about 28 s to 108 s at a steady 1.5–5 s cadence, with 5 s "proof" holds on generated outputs. The densest passage is the editor tour (#153–217).
- The climax is the mosaic with the running stopwatch.
- Exhale: bento, tagline, logo, URL, about 10 s in all, ending on empty white.

## 7. What makes it premium
- Starting with a *human* problem (a real Slack cop-out) shot like a commercial, with shallow-DOF macro B-roll, before any product.
- Every output shown is a finished, art-directed piece: consistent poster systems (LEAP FORWARD / HANG TIME / SUSPENDED IN AIR). There are no generic AI images.
- The product's verb (move) is shown by making still assets animate on screen, not by saying so.
- Brand colour is used functionally: the rainbow marks agent activity and the logo; project grounds come from the artwork.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Rewind reset.** After the problem montage, play the first 3–4 s of the film backwards at 2–3× with horizontal motion blur (about 40 px), then cut to the solution. Total 1–1.5 s.
2. **Relayout slot reel.** A list of three nouns. With each new noun, the caption swaps half of the frame and the collage moves to the opposite corner. 0.5–1 s per item, and the last item ghosts out.
3. **Denoise with a big percentage.** The output tile starts black, then blurred, then sharp. A white percentage at about 10% of frame height ticks linearly (about 11% per 0.5 s). Title type types on at 60–80%.
4. **Still → alive handoff.** When generation finishes, change nothing but let the subject start moving (pose changes every frame) under a 10% push. Hold 4–5 s.
5. **URL becomes the backdrop.** The prompt contains a URL chip. Blur the ground, fade the site in behind it, and shrink the card to the corner. Then scan-boxes hop across 3–4 elements at 0.5 s each.
6. **Ground from the artwork.** In app tours, set the solid ground to the dominant colour of the project on the canvas, and cut the ground with the project.
7. **Flat/tilt alternation.** Alternate a flat full window (2–3 s) with tilted 5–10° macros of one panel (1–2 s). The tilt signals "detail".
8. **Running proof across the cut.** A live counter (a stopwatch) keeps ticking while the layout changes from the app to the mosaic.
