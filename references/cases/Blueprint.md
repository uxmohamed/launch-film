# Blueprint (Founders, Inc.) — program teaser (58 frames ≈ 29 s)

## 1. One-line concept
A teaser for "Blueprint", a Founders, Inc. program running **Oct 1 – Nov 7**. The spine is "every great invention started as a drawing": famous patent and engineering drawings flash by (the Golden Gate Bridge, the Porsche 911, the Macintosh, the Space Shuttle, the inflatable tube man, a circuit "Fig. 1", Einstein as the patent clerk), then a grid of squares (a day counter) fills over the drawings, and the blackletter "Blueprint" wordmark and dates arrive. There are no words except the patent text and the title. It's a brand/event teaser, analysed for its motion system.

## 2. Visual world
- **One-colour world: blueprint cyanotype.** Saturated electric blue (#1a2cff-ish) ground with drawings in light cyan line (~2 px at 1080p), heavy animated film grain / noise (visibly re-rolled every frame), a soft glow on the lines, and slight vignetting. Everything, including photos, is pushed into this duotone except the "reality" inserts.
- **Reality inserts:** full-colour photos of the real object (the Golden Gate at sunset #9–12, a red Porsche #15, a Macintosh #20, a shuttle launch #26–27, a tube man #31, Einstein in B/W #36), always combined with the blueprint through a **hard geometric split** (vertical #9–12, diagonal #20, #31) or an **irregular cut-out patch** (#26–27, a torn blue shape over the photo).
- **Type:**
  - the patent documents' own typography (serif patent headers, "Fig. 1." in copperplate script, stencil drawing labels);
  - the Founders, Inc. wordmark in a heavy rounded grotesk (#1, ~12% of frame height);
  - **a giant pair of blocky white quote marks** (#3–4, ~35% of frame height);
  - the blackletter "Blueprint" (~12% of frame height at rest, ~70% during the macro pan);
  - dates "OCT 1 / NOV 7" in a pixel/VCR mono (~3.5%), placed top-left and bottom-right of the wordmark like a diagonal bracket.
- **Graphic atom:** small outlined squares (pixel cells, ~1.2% of frame) that form a 10×4 grid (#37–47).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Hard cuts** everywhere (~40 in 29 s), on a steady 0.5–1 s beat. Continuity comes from the **world**: the same blue, grain and line style. A cut between two different drawings reads like a page turn.
- **Hard split (vertical/diagonal) = drawing → reality.** The line of the split *is* the reveal: half the frame stays blueprint and half becomes the photograph of the thing it produced. A static composite, used 4 times (#9, #15, #20, #31), each on a different axis.
- **Cut-out patch** (#26): the blueprint pasted as a torn patch onto the photo. The inverse of the split.
- **Duplication pull-back** (#16–19): the camera recedes and the single drawing tiles into a grid. It shows "many" (a series of Macs, or "many inventions").
- **Anchor grid through strobe cuts** (#37–47): the grid stays fixed at frame centre while the background changes every frame. The manual's "centred anchor" is scaled up to a whole cluster.
- **Macro type pan → pull-back reveal** (#50–55): the title is first seen as fragments too large to read, swept across by the camera, then the camera pulls back to the readable word.
- **Pop-in of the dates** (#56).

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The **grain is re-rolled every frame** and is heavy (a noise amplitude of maybe 8–12% luminance), which unifies scans of very different quality.
- The **real patent metadata** is kept legible for ~0.5–1 s: patent numbers, dates and inventor names (including Steven P. Jobs). It's real, specific content as proof of taste.
- The **joke rhythm:** grand (bridge, Porsche, Mac, shuttle) → absurd (tube man) → wink (Einstein). The humour lands on single 0.5 s frames.
- The **grid is probably a day counter**: 40 cells ≈ the 38-day program (Oct 1 – Nov 7). It fills solid, then resets to hollow with only cell #1 filled (#45–46), like "day 1 of N". Hedged: this reading is inferred.
- The **cell count accelerates** (1 → 6 → 12 → 30 → 40 across ~2.5 s), giving the film its rising energy without any camera move.
- The blackletter is **tilted during the pan** (#50–53) and straight at rest (#54). Rotation settles as the camera settles.
- **The dates bracket the wordmark diagonally** (top-left and bottom-right), echoing the diagonal splits used earlier.
- The giant **quote marks** (#3–4) open a "quotation" that is never closed or filled. They frame the whole montage as a citation. It's a graphic tease more than a device.

## 6. Pacing curve
It's metronomic at ~0.5–1 s per shot for the first 18 s, with micro-accents on the splits (each held 1–2 s so the photo reads). The montage accelerates into the grid section (strobe background cuts at 2/s, cells multiplying), breathes for 1 s on a clean drawing (#48–49), then gives the title a camera move (the pan) and a 1.5 s hold with the dates. The end hold is short (~1.5 s): a teaser, not a launch.

## 7. What makes it premium
- **A total world treatment.** Every archival source is forced into one cyanotype with live grain, so 15 unrelated scans feel like one designed object.
- **Curation as argument.** The choice and order of patents (infrastructure → icon → computer → spaceflight → a joke → a genius) carries the meaning without copy.
- **Reality-vs-drawing splits** turn a static composite into a reveal. No animation is needed.
- **Blackletter for a builder program** is an unexpected type choice that makes the title feel like a certificate or charter.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Monochrome world pass.** Map every source (scans, photos) to a 2-tone palette (ground + line), add per-frame re-rolled grain (~10% luminance) and line glow. Reserve full colour for the "reality" inserts only.
2. **Split reveal: plan vs. real.** A hard vertical or diagonal split with the drawing on one side and the photo of the finished object on the other, and the drawing's title block overlaid across both. Hold 1–2 s. Rotate the split axis each time.
3. **Anchor cluster over strobe.** Lock a small graphic cluster (a grid of cells) at frame centre and hard-cut the background every 0.5 s. Grow the cluster's count on an accelerating curve (×2 per beat), then invert it (solid → hollow) as the resolve.
4. **Duplication pull-back.** Pull back ×2 per 0.5 s while the subject tiles into a grid (1 → 3 → 12 → 30) to say "many" in 2 s.
5. **Macro title sweep.** Before the wordmark resolves, truck across it at ~6× scale with ~10° tilt for ~2 s (3–4 frames of letter fragments), then cut or pull back to the readable title at ~12% of frame height.
6. **Diagonal date bracket.** Start and end dates at opposite corners of the title (top-left and bottom-right) in a contrasting mono at ~30% of the title's cap height.
