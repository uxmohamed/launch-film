# Coca-Cola (brand identity refresh), analysis (183 frames, ~91 s)

## 1. One-line concept
An identity-system reveal film: "The world's most recognized brand enters a new chapter. Not just iconic. More iconic than ever." The spine is an **anaphora**: "More ___." (Unmissable / Uplifting / Distinctive / Authentic / Unified / timeless / timely), with each adjective proven by one component of the identity system:
- the square logo
- the Dynamic Ribbon
- photography
- the typeface
- localisation
- packaging
- out-of-home (OOH) applications

It ends with "Iconic everywhere." and the square logo.

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** three flat grounds only: Coke red (#f40009), pure white, and full-bleed photography. There is no texture, gradient or grid.
- **Type:** the brand's condensed high-contrast serif (the new display face) in white on red or red on white. There are **two size registers that alternate every word**:
  - whisper: 4–6% of frame height, for "The world's", "recognized brand", "enters a", "More", "timeless.", "Iconic";
  - super-shout: 35–60% of frame height, for "new chapter.", "iconic", "than", "ever.", "Unmissable", "Distinctive", "everywhere." These are often larger than the frame, so the camera trucks across them.
- **Imagery:**
  - studio packshots on white with hard contact shadows (cans and bottles, #19–28, #145–149);
  - macro condensation and bubbles (#9–13);
  - lifestyle photography cropped tight on faces and bottles (#36–38, #56–61);
  - an OOH/application catalogue: billboards, taxi tops, a truck, merch, a macOS dock icon (#82–86, #111–139, #157–172).
- **Motifs:** the **square logo** (red square with script and ribbon) and the **Dynamic Ribbon** (the white/red swoosh). Both serve as atoms. There's also the full stop: every adjective ends in a period, "Unmissable." and so on.
- **Depth:** photographic only. There's no UI and no blur, except one rack-focus (#151).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Hard cut on the word** is the default: >60% of the cuts land on a new word or image every 0.5 s.
- **Logo grows into ground** (#3→4): the square logo's red becomes the red ground.
- **Photo → tile → grid** (#28–29) and **photo → tile → conveyor** (#39–42). The scene becomes one asset among many.
- **Ribbon morph chain:** swoosh → bottle label → chain of bottles → overlay on photography → connector between words (#48–55, #56–61, #89–90). This is the film's object lineage.
- **Word truck:** an oversized word is revealed by the camera trucking across it, and the truck motion carries into the cut.
- **Outline → fill** (#74–76).
- **Type → applied in the world:** the localized line becomes a poster in a photo (#81→82).
- **Rack-focus into a word** (#150→151).
- **Pull-back grid finale** (#175–177): the film's own contents are revealed as a wall.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- **Size rhythm:** small and huge words alternate almost strictly: "The world's" S / "most" L / "recognized brand" S; "More" S / "iconic" XL; "More" XL / "timeless." S. The last one inverts the pattern so the small word lands as the emphasis.
- Oversized words are **cropped at the ascender and descender** (the g in "Uplifting", the y in "timely"). The frame is treated as smaller than the word.
- The flying bottle cap (#33) crosses the frame during a word truck: an object-level secondary motion.
- Brick-stacking logo rows get **smaller toward the bottom** (#66–68), giving a false-perspective floor.
- In the marquee rows, the square logo sits at cap height as punctuation, and adjacent rows scroll in opposite directions.
- The multilingual lines keep the logo in the same relative slot in every language (#78–81).
- The "A typeface in Regular Medium Compressed" lines each render in the named style. The copy is the specimen.
- The cursor clicking the dock icon (#136) is the only UI moment. The brand is treated as an app.
- The final grid is made from shots already seen, so the viewer recognises the film in miniature.

## 6. Pacing curve
- Metronomic 0.5 s beats for almost the whole film: word, image, word, image.
- Variety comes from the **register** (small type / huge type / packshot / lifestyle / OOH), not from the tempo.
- Breaths are the few 2–4 s constructions: the ribbon chain (#48–55), the logo bricks and marquee (#65–70), outline → fill (#74–77), and the bottle pair (#145–151).
- The densest passages are the OOH riffle (#157–172, 16 images in 8 s) and the grid pull-back.
- The ending subtracts hard: grid → a single square logo on white, held 2.5 s.

## 7. What makes it premium
- Each claim word is paired with exactly one identity component, so the film doubles as the brand-guidelines table of contents.
- The atoms (the square logo and the ribbon) are shown *transforming*: into the label, into a chain of bottles, into punctuation, into a patch or an earring. They are not just placed.
- Photography is consistently art-directed: red and white only, tight crops, hard contact shadows.
- Type is treated as an object with physical scale (bigger than the frame).

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Anaphora spine.** A fixed small lead word ("More") plus a huge variable word, held 0.5–1 s each and hard-cut. Repeat 6–8 times across the film, each followed by 3–8 s of proof.
2. **Word truck.** Set the word at 40–60% of frame height so it is wider than the frame. The camera trucks across it in ~1–1.5 s (ease-in-out), then scales out to fit (#34) or cuts.
3. **Atom → label → chain.** The brand swoosh draws on (0.5 s), wraps the product as its label, and duplicates into a chain that traces the swoosh path (#48–55).
4. **Photo → tile → wall.** The hero photo shrinks to a bordered tile (0.5 s ease-in-out). Neighbour tiles appear and the grid drifts. Reuse it as the finale, pulling back to show every asset.
5. **Outline → fill specimen.** A hairline outline with construction guides (1 s), then a solid fill (0.5 s). Use it on the one word about craft or authenticity.
6. **Self-describing type stack.** Each line names its style and is set in it, and the stack scrolls up one line per 0.5 s.
7. **Localized line swap → in-world.** Keep the layout identical and swap the language every 0.5 s (4×), then cut to the same line on a real billboard.
8. **Rack-focus into a tiny word.** Blur the macro product ~20 px while a tiny word (~2% height) sharpens at the centre. It bridges product to type.
