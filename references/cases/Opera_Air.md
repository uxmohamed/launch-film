# Opera Air — analysis (123 frames, ~61 s)

## 1. One-line concept
Opera Air, a "mindful" browser with focus music, binaural beats and guided exercises. A single iridescent **soap bubble** is on screen for almost the whole film. It works as ground, stage and logo holder. The spine is a slow meditative couplet repeated per feature: **"Create focus / with Binaural Beats" → "Stay in flow" → "with Guided Exercises" → "Breathe in / Breathe out" → "The mindful browser" → logo.**

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** soft full-frame gradients that change hue per act, like chapters. Act 1 and the end are blue-to-white with a light leak from the left (#1–27, #107–123). The Focus act is lilac-pink (#28–69). The Exercises act is teal-over-sand (#70–105), with a warm white wash at #106. The bubble refracts the ground, so each act's colour also tints the atom.
- **The atom:** a wobbling, soft-body soap bubble at 55–75% of frame height. It has a thin-film rainbow rim (yellow/green/cyan at the edges) and a slightly darker core. **Its outline deforms on every frame** (squash of ~3–6% on one axis, bulges travelling around the rim), so even locked-off holds are alive.
- **Type:** one light geometric grotesk in white, at only ~2.5% of frame height ("Opera Air", "Create focus", "with Binaural Beats"). It always sits inside the bubble, centred. There is no scale break anywhere. The logo is Opera's "O" in dark green (~5% height) with a tiny URL line under it.
- **UI staging:** UI appears as **physical frosted-glass objects** rather than screenshots. There are glass pills with debossed icons on a tilted glass plate (#14–27), and a vertical sidebar pill standing like hardware (#79–84). Windows are tilted in 3D perspective (~20–30° yaw) and semi-transparent, so the bubble shows *through* them (#51–56, #107–112). Only one flat UI card appears: the "Creativity Boost" mini-player (#57–60).
- **Depth:** real depth-of-field. Glass edges carry specular highlights, and the bubble sits both behind (#51) and in front of (#107) the window. No drop shadows at all; depth comes from refraction and transparency.
- **Motifs:** the bubble; concentric outline rings (sound ripples, #59–68, and breath rings, #95–104); frosted glass.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Persistent atom behind cuts** (#8→28, #50→51, #69→70, #93→94, #105→107): the bubble is the constant. Text, UI and ground change around it, so no scene boundary feels like a cut. The ground hue change *is* the chapter change.
- **Hard cut into a macro hardware shot** (#8→9, #13→14, #19→20, #27→28). This is the only place real cuts cluster: a fast "hardware" interlude at 2–4 s per shot.
- **Object as its own icon:** the magnifier ring pulls back to become the search glyph (#9–13).
- **Split morph:** a button detaches from its pill (#15→16).
- **Ripple rings dissolve the UI** (#59–61). The player card fades while the rings (standing for sound) take over the frame.
- **Ground-colour cross-fade through the bubble** (#69→70: lilac → teal).
- **White bloom / light-leak wash** (#105→107): overexposure from the left edge leads into the final act, which reprises the opening blue.
- **Bookend:** the first and last frames are both the bubble on blue with "Opera Air" inside (#5 vs #121).

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The bubble's wobble never stops, even in the end card (#119–123). Its silhouette changes about 2–4% per frame.
- **The text is refracted by the bubble**: it sits inside the film and gets slightly brighter where it crosses the rim.
- **Two arrival states per line.** The first word is typed per letter; the second word blurs in as a unit ("Create" vs "focus", #28–37). That fits "focus" coming into focus.
- The bubble is in front of the window in #107–112 but behind it in #51–56. It swaps depth layer.
- The "Creativity Boost" timer ticks in real time (1 s per 2 frames). It's proof of a live product without a cursor.
- There is **no cursor anywhere in the film.** Selection is shown by a highlight ring (#54, #81) or a white tile (#90).
- The sound ripple rings are drawn as imperfect ellipses, some with 2–3 inner rings (#60–66), so they look like water rings rather than UI.
- The breathe rings expand and contract in real time: "Breathe in/out" is literally paced for the viewer to breathe.
- The logo lockup is built in order: O, then wordmark, then URL, each ~0.5 s apart (#117–119).

## 6. Pacing curve
Very slow and nearly uniform. Text beats are long (4–6.5 s each, versus the manual's 0.5–1.5 s). The only faster passages are the glass-hardware interludes (#9–27, 2–4 s per shot) and the UI slide (#85–93). There is no climax by speed or scale; the "climax" is the breath sequence (#94–105), the longest calm hold. The ending is a 3.5 s logo hold in the same bubble. The rhythm alternates **type-in-bubble (5–6 s) ↔ glass/UI (3–5 s)** four times.

## 7. What makes it premium
- One physical, simulated object carries the whole film. It's a soft body, not a vector circle, and it is literally "Air".
- UI is re-imagined as glass hardware with depth-of-field and specular rims instead of screenshots.
- The motion is metaphorical and functional at once: "focus" blurs into focus, sound becomes ripples, and breathing rings pace the viewer.
- Colour per act is a mood, and the atom refracts it.
- Total restraint in type: one size and one weight for the whole film.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Soft-body atom stage.** A simulated bubble or blob at 60–75% of frame height stays on screen all film and holds all copy inside it. Per-frame rim deformation is ±3–6%; drift is ~2% of the frame per second. Swap text and ground around it instead of cutting.
2. **Semantic arrival state.** Pick the arrival effect from the word's meaning: "focus" blurs in (blur 20 px → 0 over ~2 s), "Create" types per letter (~3 letters/s).
3. **Icon as physical object pull-back.** Open inside a product glyph (a magnifier ring) at ~3× and pull back over 2 s until the viewer recognises it as the icon.
4. **Glass-hardware UI.** Re-model the sidebar and pills as frosted glass on a tilted plate (yaw ~25°) with a specular rim, and truck along the diagonal while icons stack in at ~0.5–1 s intervals.
5. **Rings as the verb.** Concentric outline rings stand for sound (spawned at random, drifting) and breath (centred, expanding over ~2.5 s, contracting over ~2.5 s). The rings also replace the UI (the card fades under them).
6. **Ground-hue chaptering through a refractive atom.** Change the full-frame gradient per act (blue → lilac → teal → blue). The atom picks up the new colour automatically.
7. **Cursorless selection.** A white fill on one tile, or a ring on an icon, held 1.5 s. It fits calm products.
