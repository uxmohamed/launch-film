# Claude keynote intro ("Code w/ Claude"): analysis (93 frames, ~46 s, 16:9)

## 1. One-line concept
A stage-opener for the "Code w/ Claude" event. A terminal caret blinks, collapses and **becomes the pixel mascot** (the Claude Code crab-creature, "Clawd"). The mascot then lives through ~25 micro-vignettes of human hobbies and professions: surfer, cook, astronaut, rock guitarist, footballer, swimmer, wizard, ballerina, cellist, kite-flyer, F1 driver, boxer, Viking, and more. Finally it sits on a bench watching a sunrise, and the caret types the event lock-up. The spine is **caret → character → "it can be anything / do anything" → caret → name.**

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** flat near-black (#1C1C1C) with an **LED/dot-matrix screen texture**. Every grey shape (pedestals, skyline bars, waves, kitchen, cube, orbit rings, football pitch, dots) is filled with a fine halftone dot grid, so the world reads as a terminal display. There are no gradients except a soft radial dim on some rings.
- **Palette:** background charcoal; props in 2–3 greys; **the mascot is the only saturated element** (Claude clay orange #D97757-ish). Props get one small secondary accent at most: a pink surfboard (#19–25), a purple guitar (#50–58), a purple wizard hat (#66–68), a pink tutu, a brown cello, a yellow kite, a blue ship sail. Sparkles and confetti are pale yellow.
- **Type:** only at the end. "Code w/" in **monospace** (small, ~4% of frame height) stacked over **"Claude" in the display serif** (~6% of frame height). The mono/serif pairing *is* the event identity: code plus the humane brand. A persistent tiny **Anthropic "A\" bug** sits top-right (~2% of frame) for the whole film.
- **Imagery:** 8-bit sprite animation, limited frames (1–3 poses per gag), no anti-aliasing on the mascot. Props sometimes use a higher pixel density than the mascot (the F1 car #78–79, the ship #83).
- **Staging:** the mascot is **centred and small** (~12–15% of frame width, ~10% of height) in almost every shot. Only the world around it changes.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Caret → character morph** (#4→5→6): the brand atom, since Claude Code lives in the terminal. The caret squashes to an underscore, then inflates into the body.
- **Ground grows from under the subject** (pedestal #8–11, waves #19, horizon #85–87): the world is built up around a fixed character.
- **Set slides in as a unit** (kitchen #27–28): a theatrical flat pushed on stage.
- **Rings grow into the ground** (#38–41): the manual's "dot grows into ground", done with concentric dot-matrix rings emanating from the helmet.
- **Pull-back reveal** (football #59–61): the pitch lines shrink until you understand what you're in.
- **Hard-cut costume swaps with a centred anchor** (#76–86): the mascot's body stays exactly centred and the same size, and only the costume and prop change. This is the manual's "centred anchor", except the anchor is a *character* rather than a dot.
- **Caret return** (#92): the mascot scene dims to ~30% and a typing caret overwrites it. It bookends the opening caret.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The caret blink is uneven (#2 at ~40% opacity, #3 100%, #4 60%), like a real fade-blink rather than an on/off terminal blink.
- The mascot **never leaves centre** (±5% of frame), even while the "camera" tracks the pedestal rise. The world moves, not the star.
- Every prop and background shares **the same dot-matrix pitch** (~0.4% of frame width per dot). This single texture rule unifies ~25 unrelated scenes.
- Secondary sparkles, spray and confetti are always **pale yellow, 1–3 pixels**, placed asymmetrically off one side of the body (#40, #42–47, #51–57, #66–68).
- **Stepped animation** (1–3 poses per gag, no tweening on the sprite) contrasts with **smooth motion on the vector backgrounds** (rings, orbits and circles scale smoothly). Two animation languages are layered.
- The riffle's backgrounds get *emptier* as the cut rate rises (#78–86 are nearly bare black). This is the manual's rule "accelerate by removing reading", but applied to backgrounds.
- The end lock-up puts the **mono above the serif**, and the mono is typed while the serif appears whole.

## 6. Pacing curve
A quiet 3 s caret open → a 1 s birth → a steady rhythm of 2–4.5 s vignettes (#8–75), each with one gag → accelerates to 1 cut per 0.5 s for ~5.5 s (#76–86) → a 2.5 s sunrise breath (the only calm "sunset/sunrise" moment) → 1 s lock-up (the keynote cuts to live stage). There's no long end hold because it's an intro: the stage takes over.

## 7. What makes it premium
- It uses one character and one texture rule, and every scene obeys both. The variety comes from costume and set, never from style changes.
- Colour discipline: the mascot is the only orange object for 46 s, so the eye never searches.
- The caret origin ties a cute mascot back to the product surface (the terminal) without showing any UI.
- The comedy is in the stepped sprite timing, e.g. the 2-frame backflip at #12–13.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Caret-born mascot.** Blink the caret 1–2 cycles, squash it to an underscore (1 frame), then inflate it into the character (2 frames, with overshoot). Reverse it at the end: the scene dims to 30% and the caret types the lock-up.
2. **Fixed star, moving world.** Keep the character centred at a fixed size. Grow ground from beneath it, slide sets in as flats, or grow rings out from it. The camera "tracks" by moving the world.
3. **One-texture world.** Fill every non-hero shape with the same halftone dot pitch in greys. The hero is the only flat saturated colour.
4. **Costume riffle.** After 3–4 long vignettes, hard-cut every 0.5 s through 8–10 costume swaps on an almost empty ground. The body stays pixel-identical, and only the props change.
5. **Mixed animation grammar.** Use a stepped sprite (on 12s or 6s) over smoothly tweened vector backgrounds (rings, orbits), so the character feels hand-made and the world feels engineered.
6. **Mono-over-serif lock-up.** The code word is typed in mono and the brand word appears whole in serif. Total ~1 s.
