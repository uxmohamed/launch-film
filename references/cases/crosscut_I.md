# Crosscut I — Index by Parallel, Claude "Keep thinking", Claude Cowork, Blueprint (Founders, Inc.)

These four films sit at the two edges of the library:
- two **manifesto/brand films** with no product UI: Index (abstract 2D dots, 96 s) and Keep thinking (live action, 90 s);
- one **archival teaser**: Blueprint (29 s);
- one **UI feature film**: Cowork (81 s).

Two of them are from the same brand (Claude), which lets us see how one company varies the language between a brand film and a feature film.

## 1. Recurring patterns
1. **The type is the timeline, even without UI.** Index is a spoken essay split around a dot (about 25 lines). Cowork's thesis bookends ("Claude keeps working, even when you're not" / "Claude keeps it moving."). Keep thinking's one line arrives only at the end ("Keep ___ / Keep thinking."). Blueprint has zero copy. Its "sentence" is the ordered list of patents.
2. **A slot line at the end.** Keep thinking uses "Keep [chip]" with 4 variants at ~1–1.5 s each. Index uses a spatial slot in the orange triptych ("Cures we ● couldn't see / Connections we ● couldn't make / Breakthroughs just ● beyond our reach"), where only the disc contents and the outer words change.
3. **Typing as the dominant text entrance.**
   - Index types every line with a caret whose style marks the era (block → I-beam).
   - Cowork types the prompt, the status pill labels and the wordmark.
   - Keep thinking has no typing; its text is diegetic.
   - Blueprint pops text in on the cut.

   In 3 of 4 films typing is how text arrives.
4. **The small brand atom carries state.** Index's dot is word gap → node → star → drop → user → logo. Cowork's spark is thinking indicator → status icon → collapsed tile → notification → logo. Keep thinking's orange joint dots in the AR overlays echo Claude's orange. Blueprint's pixel cell grid is the counter.
5. **The ground is the metaphor and changes by state, not by chapter title.** Index uses dark (pre-web / void) → printer paper (web) → orange (loss/hope) → paper. Cowork uses blue (you're here) → off-white (Claude thinking) → halftone cloud (you're away). Blueprint uses one cyanotype for everything, and the photos are "reality".
6. **Hard cuts carried by the world, not by lineage.** Keep thinking (~100 cuts) and Blueprint (~40 cuts) almost never morph. Continuity is the grade and grain, a recurring word (PROBLEMS), a fixed anchor cluster (the Blueprint grid), or a consistent overlay language.
7. **Verb-literal geometry.** Index: "closing up" → the ground irises shut; "weren't built for this" → a gravity collapse; "millions of agents" → a density ramp. Cowork: "keeps working even when you're not" → the laptop lid closes and the work continues in a cloud.

## 2. Contradictions between these films (and why)
- **Morph vs. cut.** Index morphs the atom constantly (it's abstract, so anything can become anything). Keep thinking and Blueprint cut constantly, because live action and archival scans can't morph, so they unify through grade and grain. Rule: *lineage for drawn worlds, grade/anchor for found or shot worlds.*
- **End on an emptied ground vs. a living frame.** Index and Cowork end on an empty ground (paper, off-white) with the logo held 3.5 s. Keep thinking puts the lockup **over live footage** with the subject still moving (#172–178). Blueprint ends on a busy drawing with the title and dates and only ~1.5 s of hold. Brand films of the photographic kind keep the world, and teasers cut short.
- **Where the brand appears.** Index shows the logomark at ~70% of runtime, then again as the end lockup. Keep thinking names Claude only in the last ~4%. Cowork shows the product UI at 3 s and the logo only at the end. Blueprint shows the presenter's logo first (#1) and the event's name last. That's two brands with two slots.
- **Camera.** Cowork's camera is a reader: caret-follow, pull-back, drift. Index's is locked for ~90% of the film (stillness = essay), with one zoom-through. Blueprint's big camera move is saved for the title sweep.
- **Colour for meaning.** Cowork uses two highlight colours with distinct meanings (salmon = task/attention, blue = needs you/selection). The manual's "one accent at a time" holds per frame but not per film.

## 4. vs. the manual

### (a) Techniques NOT in the manual (name + recipe)
1. **Backspace exit** (Index #9, #20–23, #41, #60, #129, #170). Text leaves by deleting right-to-left at ~2× typing speed, stops on a shared prefix or empties, and the next line types into the same spot. It replaces fades as the default text exit and gives text a full write/delete lifecycle. The manual only covers entrances and arrival states.
2. **Hinge-dot sentence** (Index). The key sentence is split into two halves with the atom at exact frame centre (left half right-aligned, right half left-aligned, gap ~3–4% of frame width). The dot grows with the line, and between clauses only the dot's contents change (spots → network → polar grid, #113–121). This goes beyond "inline icons as words": the atom is the fulcrum of every line.
3. **Iris-close on the verb** (Index #98–103). The ground itself becomes a disc and shrinks 130% → 45% of frame height over ~3 s while the text "…is closing up" types inside it, then cut to black for 1.5 s. It's the inverse of "dot grows into ground", motivated by a word rather than a click.
4. **Gravity collapse** (Index #78–82). A structured dot grid (the old model) drops under rigid-body physics in ~1 s, piles at the bottom edge, and the label pill lands tilted and stays. It's a verb transition for "broken / not built for this".
5. **Typing-history cascade** (Index #142–151). 12–16 rows of the wordmark at every typing stage, mirrored around one complete centre row, scroll vertically (~3 s), then collapse to the centre row (~1 s). A name reveal that shows the act of typing as a waveform.
6. **Pencil emphasis** (Index #49–52, #125–131). A hand-drawn underline or scribble draws on under one word ~0.5 s after the phrase lands. Emphasis without colour, weight or scale.
7. **Era-coded caret** (Index). A block caret for the terminal era, an I-beam for the editor era. The caret shape is the period marker.
8. **Paper-feed end** (Index #177–180). The ground sheet advances upward like printer paper (the perforation line travels up) and delivers the logo from below.
9. **Lid-close time-skip** (Cowork #51–58). Pull back through content → window → a flat illustrated laptop (~1.5× over 2 s), warm the screen to sunset, close the lid in ~1 s, and let the screen's glow persist as the next ground. A physical device action as the "time passes" cut.
10. **Presence-coded grounds** (Cowork). The ground encodes *who is present*, not the chapter: blue = user here, off-white = Claude thinking, a dithered halftone cloud = user away. It's switched by device actions (the send click, the lid close, the phone sinking).
11. **Away-state status pill with satellite chips** (Cowork #58–72, #119–131). One centred pill (atom plus a verb-ing label typed with shimmer) cycles 3–4 labels at 1.5–2 s; tool chips float at 3 depths with 6–10 px blur on the inactive ones; the pill turns the accent colour on the final state, then collapses into the atom tile.
12. **Prompt as table of contents** (Cowork #11–26). Each task clause in the prompt gets a marker highlight as it completes, and every highlighted clause pays off later, in order (prep doc → email → 8am brief → recap).
13. **Status-bar clock as timeline** (Cowork 8:14 → 8:19 → 8:21). Time passes on the device clock, not in captions.
14. **Diagonal-stagger → align** (Cowork #127–131). In-progress steps enter each offset +5% x (a diagonal), then snap flush-left when complete. Layout encodes progress.
15. **Human edit beat** (Cowork #101–103, #111–115). Macro into the AI's draft, select a placeholder or sentence (blue), and retype it in human words at reading speed. A trust beat that shows the user still authors the result.
16. **Devices as rising props** (Cowork #73, #118–119, #132). Flat vector devices enter and exit only vertically from the bottom edge (~40% of frame in 0.5 s).
17. **Diegetic word inventory** (Keep thinking #1–40). One noun (PROBLEMS) is physically present in 13+ materials and typefaces (CRT, Dymo label, dot-matrix, neon, file name, signage, eye reflection), about one per 0.5–1 s near frame centre, with the spelling broken in some. A type motif with no type animation.
18. **Black-breath punchline** (Keep thinking #40 → #41–45 → #46). End the fast act mid-action (a falling piano), give 2.5 s of black with live grain, and open the next act on the aftermath (the smashed piano).
19. **Hairline reasoning overlay** (Keep thinking #85–86, #108–109, #127–128, #131–134, #161–169). The AI's thinking is drawn as 1–2 px lines and small orange dots tracked to real motion (a trajectory, climbing route, pose skeleton, lung diagram, orbiting math glyphs). This is the manual's "HUD over footage" without frosted glass or boxes.
20. **Slot chip over footage** (Keep thinking #140–150). Fixed "Keep" plus the product's own UI chip (icon plus label) as the slot. The chip label and the footage cut on the same frame. The end-line is a UI component used as typography.
21. **Aspect-ratio as cut accent** (Keep thinking throughout). Switch 4:3 / 2.39 / 16:9 mattes from cut to cut for rhythm and an archive texture.
22. **Split reveal: plan vs. real** (Blueprint #9–12, #15, #20, #31). A hard vertical or diagonal split with the drawing on one side, the photo of the finished object on the other, and the drawing's title block overlaid across both. Rotate the axis each time.
23. **Anchor cluster over strobe** (Blueprint #37–47). A whole grid (not a single dot) stays locked at centre while the background hard-cuts every frame. The cell count accelerates 1 → 40, then inverts solid → hollow. It extends Harvey's centred anchor from a dot to a counter.
24. **Monochrome world pass with re-rolled grain** (Blueprint). All archival sources are mapped to two tones (ground + line) with per-frame grain at ~10% luminance. The world is made by treatment, not by a ground plate.
25. **Macro title sweep** (Blueprint #50–55). Truck across the wordmark at ~6× scale with ~10° tilt for ~2 s, then settle to the readable title at ~12% of frame height, with the rotation settling as the camera does.

### (b) Where these films contradict the manual
- **"End by subtraction on an emptied ground."** Keep thinking puts the lockup over live, moving footage (#172–178), and it also *adds* an 8.5 s epilogue after the tagline card (#155–171), so the tagline is not the end. Blueprint ends on a busy drawing with ~1.5 s of hold, below the manual's 2–5 s.
- **"Things become, they don't cut" / "follow one object."** Keep thinking (~100 hard cuts, no single object, a cast of ~40 people) and Blueprint (~40 cuts across 15 unrelated inventions) are premium with no lineage at all. Their carriers are a recurring word, the grade/grain, and an anchor cluster. The manual should allow a **"motif-anchored montage"** mode for brand and archival films.
- **"Show a full window 1–3 times; never device mockups by default"** and "device = someone else's world." Cowork spends ~45% of its runtime inside flat illustrated devices (laptop #54–57, #132–135; phone #73–118) of *its own* product. The device is there because *time and place* are the story (overnight at the desk, morning on the phone). The rule should be: a device is allowed when the device's state (lid, clock, notification) carries narrative.
- **"Captions 1.5–3.5% of frame height."** Index sets its entire essay at ~1.2–1.5%, the bottom of the range or below, over 96 s, and it still reads because the ground is empty and the line sits on the centre axis. The lower bound can go to ~1.2% for mono on an empty ground.
- **"One accent colour at a time."** Cowork runs two highlight hues in the same act with fixed meanings (salmon = attention/task, blue = placeholder/selection, #86–110). It works because each colour is a *type of mark*, not decoration.
- **"The camera is a reader; hold drift everywhere."** Index is locked-off for ~90% of its runtime with no hold drift on the text beats. Life comes from typing, backspacing, the dot growing and particle motion, not the camera. Stillness reads as essay, as the manual allows for Raindrop/Veryfront, but here it's in a 96 s film.
- **"Front-load proof in long films (logo by 20–35%)."** Index (logomark at ~70%) and Keep thinking (brand at ~96%) are long films that save the brand for the end. The manual's front-loading applies to product launches, not manifestos.

### (c) Manual claims these films strongly confirm
- **Arrival state: ghost at 30–40% then settle** (Cowork #1–5, and again in the wordmark "Claud[e]" #157), with one arrival state used at both ends.
- **Caret-follow macro** with the caret at ~60–65% of frame width and text running off the left edge (Cowork #11–20); **typing at 6–8 chars per 0.5 s** for readable text (Cowork, Index).
- **Micro-states demos skip:** the empty card frame before chrome fills (Cowork #7), hover → hand cursor one frame before the click (#24–26), spinner ring → check (#38–42), "Result" tags and shimmer on the in-progress row (#34).
- **Replace spinners with texture:** shimmer on status text (Cowork pills), a dithered cloud as "working" (Cowork), a colour-shimmer dot matrix (Index #69–77).
- **Dot grows into ground / ground from an atom** (Index #107–112, drop → splash → orange flood).
- **Centred anchor through hard cuts** (Blueprint grid #37–47, Keep thinking's PROBLEMS near centre in Act 1).
- **Density ramp for scale** ("millions of agents", Index #157–166; the Mac duplication, Blueprint #16–19).
- **Fixed-layout slideshow** (Index orange triptych #113–121; Keep thinking chip slot #140–150).
- **Real, specific data sells the craft** (Cowork's names, times and ticket counts; Blueprint's real patent numbers and inventors).
- **Bookend the film** (Cowork "Claude keeps working…" → "Claude keeps it moving."; Index opens and closes on the paper and the dot).
- **One zoom-through as a shout** (Index #121–123, the only camera shout in 96 s).
- **Chapter coding by ground** (Index dark → paper → orange → paper; Cowork blue/off-white/halftone).
