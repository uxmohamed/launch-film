# Crosscut N: Google AI Studio, Google Material 3, Spellbook, Uber Icon System, New Mac mini, Shopify

The set: two SaaS feature films (AI Studio at 42 s, Spellbook at 48 s), three brand or design-system films (Material 3 at 48 s, Uber at 41 s, Shopify at 34.5 s) and one hardware spot (Mac mini at 38.5 s). Frame refs are `Film #n` at ~2 fps.

## 1. Patterns that recur across these films

1. **The thing demonstrates itself (self-exemplifying motion).** Whatever is being launched becomes the animation's own behaviour:
   - Material's motion chapter is drawn with easing curves (M3 #22–#33).
   - Uber's LIGHTER loses weight and FRIENDLIER bends into a smile (Uber #13–#17).
   - Material's type chapter flips font axes (M3 #76–#81).
   - The AI Studio URL is typed into the real field (AIS #33–#37).
   - Spellbook's "seconds" is set as a stopwatch, "se:co:nds" (SB #58).

   The brand films push this furthest: there is no demo, because the type *is* the demo.
2. **A UI component becomes the transition device.** The manual has "product verb" transitions. These films go more literal and use a real component *as* the wipe:
   - Material's pull-to-refresh spinner drags the old title off frame (M3 #3–#6).
   - The AI Studio field turns into a button, then a status pill, then an app icon (AIS #42–#56).
   - Spellbook's dashed "loading" rings complete into checks as the list scrolls (SB #50–#52).
   - Uber's tab bar pushes the word ACCESSIBLE down as it enters (Uber #44–#45).
3. **Status pills are staged as a beat.** A waiting state is shown, not skipped:
   - AI Studio: "Setting up domain" → "Publishing your app" (#47–#52, 3 s).
   - Spellbook: "Extracting clauses…" → "Scanning for risks…" → "Comparing terms…" (#24–#26). A giant outline echo word fills the background behind the pill.

   A 1.5–3 s wait becomes a designed breath.
4. **Words part to admit an image or window** (the manual's "word split, insertion", seen much more often here):
   - Spellbook: "Every [hexagon of footage] deal" (#4–#6) and "at [collage] scale" (#63–#65).
   - Shopify: "New [photo] York" (#53–#54).
   - Uber: "ICON [cycling icon] SYSTEM" (#6–#7) and "From ——— Base", where a rule retracts between the words (#8–#10).
   - Mac mini: "The Mighty [product] Mac mini" (#68–#69).

   In every case the gap is sized to the thing inside it. The gap opens first and closes after.
5. **Icons and glyphs trade places.** This is the atom idea applied to letterforms:
   - AI Studio: the letters of "Introducing" scatter and re-form into the link icon (#3–#6).
   - Uber: four icons morph into the letters A C V D, and an icon is typed with a caret (#27–#32).
   - Material: shapes morph into the "M" mark, then the G (#89–#91).
   - Spellbook: "contract" becomes a Word file chip (#10–#11).
6. **Colour as light, not fill.** AI Studio's brand gradient lives only on strokes and halos. Material's colour lives in the grounds and never inside the text. Spellbook's colour is purely semantic (red = risk, green = inserted, blue = action). Uber's single accent blue annotates a principle and is then removed.
7. **Binary ground switching as chaptering.** Uber cuts black ↔ white on every statement/specimen boundary. Spellbook runs dark → dot-screen → paper grey. Shopify runs white → black 3D → white → black 3D. Material changes ground on nearly every chapter (14+ grounds in 48 s).
8. **Numbers rendered as mechanisms, not counters:**
   - Uber: slot-machine digits with clipped neighbours, "0,04…" → "1,500" (#56–#58).
   - Spellbook: a ring whose colour maps to the value, red 12% → yellow 41%, with the card growing 1.3× as it ticks (#79–#83).
   - Shopify: a clock dial counting *down* 26 → 25 (#43–#46).

## 2. Contradictions (opposite choices, and why)

- **Brand at the start or at the end.**
  - AI Studio front-loads the brand: the logo lockup sits at #11–#15 (≈6 s) and the end card is a URL only.
  - Spellbook and Mac mini hold the brand until the last 10%.
  - Shopify never shows a logo in the sampled frames.

  Why: AI Studio is an incremental feature on a known product, so the brand orients the viewer. Spellbook and Mac mini are story-driven and payoff-ended.
- **Ending by subtraction vs ending by addition.**
  - Subtraction: Uber ("One Uber" → "One" → mark), Mac mini (costumes → bare object → logo), Spellbook (a fade).
  - Addition: Shopify ends in a dense 3D point-cloud fly-through with a floating yellow object (#56–#69), and Material ends in a riffle and "NICE" shout before the G.

  Why: design-culture reels want to leave an aftertaste of maximalism. Product films want a clean handoff.
- **Real screen UI vs abstraction.** AI Studio uses *real* dark UI in real browser chrome for its payoff (#62–#70), after 30 s of abstracted single controls. Spellbook uses real Word chrome. Uber and Material rebuild everything. Mac mini has zero UI. The rule seems to be: use real chrome exactly once, as proof, after the abstraction.
- **Type size.** Whisper-small type (the manual's 1.5–3.5%) appears only in Spellbook's serif (~4–5%) and AI Studio's end URL (~3%). Uber (8–10%), Material (12–15%), Shopify (10–12%) and AI Studio's titles (7–8%) all use big type for every statement. Brand films treat headline type as the main visual, with no small-type baseline to shout against.
- **One accent vs maximal palette.** Uber and Spellbook use one accent. Material shows a different ground every 1–2 s, but each frame has only 2–3 hues. Constraint per frame, not per film.
- **Stock or live footage.** Spellbook uses cinematic stock (trains, towers), but only caged in a logo-shaped window. Mac mini uses real hands. Both keep the footage "designed" by containment or by making the hand the only real element.

## 4. vs. the manual

### (a) Techniques NOT in the manual (name + recipe)
1. **Component-as-wipe.** Use a real UI component of the product as the scene transition. It enters with its own native motion and physically displaces the previous content. Material's pull-to-refresh spinner drops in from the top with overshoot (0.4 s), drags the title out of frame (0.5 s) and spins out (0.3 s) (M3 #3–#6). Uber's tab bar descends and pushes "ACCESSIBLE" down (Uber #44–#45).
2. **Self-describing adjective.** Animate each claim word by the property it names:
   - outline + construction grid (open);
   - weight axis 900→100 (lighter);
   - text-on-path, with the Bézier relaxing V→U (friendlier);
   - a left-to-right contrast ramp (accessible);
   - step-and-repeat outlines (scalable).

   Hard cut between them, 1–1.5 s each (Uber #11–#17, #43, #53).
3. **Glyph-scatter → icon / icon → glyph.** Title letters break into rotating strokes (30–90°) over ~1 s, and 2–3 strokes re-form the feature icon (AIS #3–#6). The inverse: icons on a baseline morph into letters with similar skeletons over ~2 frames (Uber #27–#29). This is richer than the manual's "punctuation → logo".
4. **Echo word behind status.** While a status pill cycles verbs every 0.5 s, the pill's noun sits huge (~50% of frame height), as a pale outline at ~8% contrast, drifting horizontally behind it (SB #24–#26).
5. **Hue-rotating focus stroke + wandering halo.** The only coloured element is a 1.5 px conic-gradient stroke on the focused control, rotating ~180°/s, with an off-centre 60 px halo that drifts. It hands over from field to button to pill to icon (AIS #31–#56). It shows "AI is working" with no sparkle cliché.
6. **Field → button → split-flap pill → squircle icon.** A four-station morph chain, all at one centre point:
   - a white fill wipe L→R turns the field into a button (0.5 s);
   - a split-flap roll turns the button into a dark status pill;
   - the pill labels slot-roll;
   - the pill contracts into a squircle, a line icon draws, and a diagonal colour wipe fills it (AIS #42–#56).
7. **Logo-shaped aperture with mutating facets.** Footage is shown only through a window in the brand mark's silhouette, between two words. The window's facet geometry changes with each 0.5 s clip, like a gem turning. On exit it closes to zero and the words slide together (SB #3–#8).
8. **Sibling-coupled press.** The pressed button widens ~20% and changes corner radius, and its siblings narrow so the group's total width is constant (M3 #36–#37, #44). It is a physical rule, shown twice.
9. **Theme-swap reveal.** Hold one layout, pass a full-frame tint wash (1 frame), and reveal the same layout in its dark theme (M3 #14–#16). The transition is a colour-scheme change only.
10. **Mask swell.** A thumbnail's shaped mask (cookie/scallop) scales from ~8% to ~60% of the frame, goes solid, and bursts into the next chapter's elements (M3 #56–#59).
11. **Checklist completion scroll.** A full-frame list of issues at ~8% cap height scrolls up one row per 0.5 s. Dashed "loading" rings complete into check marks as each row passes the upper third. Then the list scales down into a small card (SB #50–#55).
12. **Dimensional collapse to a dial.** 3D pills fly in along a curve and flatten into the minute ticks of a clock bezel. An accent hand sweeps, the outline numeral fills solid, and it then counts *down* (Shopify #32–#46).
13. **Costume escalation (hardware).** The product is the fixed centre. Each benefit grows out of its body as a new costume (arms → wheels/engine → tentacles → rocket). Each lasts 2.5–8 s, escalates in complexity, and the object resets to bare for 0.5 s between costumes (Mac #16–#62). This is object lineage for physical products.
14. **Ingredient insertion + jolt.** The spec is a physical token (a paper-cut "M6" chip) lowered *into* the product. It is followed by a one-frame hand-drawn lightning overlay in the token's colour (Mac #9–#12).
15. **Material wipe.** The world's own substance (clay clouds) fills the frame for ~1.5 s and parts onto the final flat-lay (Mac #63–#66).
16. **Word-shuffle into logo.** The thesis line's words scatter and re-order ("The world runs on contracts" → "contracts run on"), then collapse to the wordmark, inside slowly rotating orbit lines (SB #84–#89).
17. **Row that keeps its width.** Each new tile makes the whole row scale down so the total stays ~60% of the frame width. The new caption fades grey→black (Uber #19–#26).
18. **Principle annotation accent.** Colour only the icon segment that illustrates the captioned principle. Remove all accents once the list completes (Uber #19–#27).
19. **Spread-and-converge headline with registration dots.** The first and last words start at the far left and right, small, with tiny corner dots marking an invisible frame. They converge and scale ~3× in 0.5 s (Shopify #2–#3).
20. **Flash + glitch decay** as a bookend "boom": a one-frame radial white burst, then RGB-split blocks on the key word decaying over 2 frames (Shopify #4–#6, #55). The manual lists glitch presets as cheap. Here it is used once, on the one word being claimed ("new"), and cleared in 2 frames.

### (b) Where these films CONTRADICT the manual
- **"Type is small (captions 1.5–3.5%)."** Four of six films run statement type at 7–15% of frame height throughout: Uber 8–10% (#4–#17), Material 12–15% (#1–#11), Shopify 10–12% (#3, #50–#52), AI Studio 7–8% (#2–#9). For brand and design-system films, big type *is* the ground plate. Emphasis comes from behaviour (weight, path, glitch) and not from scale contrast. Spellbook creates its shout by camera (a 15× macro, #37–#39), not by font size.
- **"The UI lives in a world; never on a void."** AI Studio stages everything on pure black with no texture (#31–#56), and Uber on flat black or white. The world is supplied by *light* (AI Studio's halos) or by the binary ground rhythm (Uber). A void is fine when the element carries its own light.
- **"One accent at a time."** Material shows 14+ grounds in 48 s, and AI Studio uses a full four-colour gradient on one stroke. It holds per frame (2–3 hues) but not per film.
- **"End by subtraction; nothing slams."** Shopify ends by *adding* density: a 3D point-cloud fly-through (#56–#69). Material's last 4 s before the logo are a 0.5 s macro riffle plus a "NICE" scale shout (#82–#88). Both oversize-settle logos (AIS 2.5×, M3 1.75×) exceed the manual's 150–160%.
- **"Stock transitions (glitch, light leaks, whip) are cheap."** Shopify uses a light-burst and RGB glitch (#4–#6, #55), and Material uses motion-blur whip crops (#82–#86). They work because each is used once or twice, is tied to the claim word or to a chapter boundary, and decays within 2 frames.
- **"Rebuild the UI; never screen-record."** AI Studio shows real dark UI and a real browser window as its proof (#62–#70), and Spellbook shows real Word chrome (#13–#18). They are curated, but they are not rebuilt at film type sizes.
- **Serif rule** ("only if it's the brand's, or for editorial captions"). Spellbook uses a literary serif as the main narrative voice (#4–#12, #56–#62, #84–#88), strictly split from the grotesk UI. The serif is the voice and the grotesk is the product.
- **Front-loading in long films.** The manual says to put the logo by 20–35%. Spellbook (48 s) and Mac mini (38.5 s) show the brand only at ~92–95%. The manual's rule holds only for feature films on known brands (AI Studio, 15%).

### (c) Manual claims these films strongly confirm
- **Follow one object.** AI Studio follows the URL string (field → icon → browser bar), Spellbook follows one construction agreement, and Mac mini follows one physical unit through every costume.
- **Things become, they don't cut.** AI Studio from #38 to #61 is a single morph chain with the centre of mass barely moving. Mac mini never cuts away from its product.
- **Show the verb, not before and after.** Spellbook types the redline at 15× (#37–#39). The AI Studio slug is typed, validated ("Available" appears after typing stops) and published with visible waiting states.
- **Fixed-layout slideshow at 2/s.** Material's type grid (#76–#81), Shopify's deck-ground cuts (#26–#29) and Uber's tab-state walk (#46–#51).
- **Numbers tick in decelerating steps.** Spellbook 12 → 14 → 29 → 41 (#79–#83) and Uber's slot-rolled 1,500 (#56–#58).
- **Macro → context pull-back.** Spellbook issue card → full doc (#27–#30), and AI Studio published card → full window (#61–#63).
- **Chapter coding by ground.** Taken to the maximum by Material and Uber.
- **Device-frame rule.** Shopify's Shop Pay is in a real iPhone (a mobile-native product). Material shows phones frameless as cropped screen bottoms with only a home bar, because they *are* the system.
- **Final hold of 2–5 s.** Every film that ends on a mark or URL lands within 2–4 s.
- **Life during holds.** Mac mini's multitask shot does it without camera drift: a dozen props change state at staggered times (#43–#55), a strong alternative to the 3–5% push.
