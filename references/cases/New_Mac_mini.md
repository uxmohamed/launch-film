# New Mac mini ("The Mighty Mac mini with M6"), analysis (77 frames, ≈38.5 s)

*A hardware brand spot, not a SaaS demo. Read it for the object-transformation system and the end-card grammar. There is no UI at all.*

## 1. One-line concept
The Mac mini is tiny but mighty. The spine is **one object that keeps growing new bodies**. A hand places a Mac mini on a seamless white sweep; a hand-cut "M6" chip is dropped into it; it gets a lightning jolt, then sprouts clay muscle-arms and does push-ups, stands up flexing, becomes a hot-rod drifting through clay smoke, becomes a brain-headed robot with a dozen tentacle arms juggling pro tasks (music, photo, science, charts, 3D), becomes a rocket, and launches into clay clouds. The clouds part, and it is a plain Mac mini again: "The Mighty Mac mini with M6" → the Apple logo. Every feature (performance, graphics, multitasking, speed) is a **costume**, never a spec.

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** one continuous **seamless photo-studio sweep** in cool light grey (#D8DBDF → #C9CDD2 at the edges, a soft vignette). The camera changes angle but the world never changes. No type until #68.
- **Imagery:** a hybrid of live action and stop-motion-style CG. There is a real hand (#3–#4, #9–#11), a real (or photoreal) aluminium Mac mini, and **plasticine/clay** add-ons with visible fingerprint texture and a slight matte sheen: yellow muscle arms, red gym shorts, white sneakers, green hot-rod body, V8 engines with flame exhausts, grey corrugated tentacles, a pink brain, a grey/orange rocket. Clay smoke puffs and clouds are lumpy white balls. Painted tyre marks (#30–#32) look like **brush strokes**, not rubber.
- **Colour:** the world is grey/white/aluminium; colour comes only from the clay costumes (yellow, red, green, pink, orange, blue) and the "M6" chip's blue-green gradient. The final card returns to monochrome.
- **Type:** SF Pro Display semibold, black, cap height ~4% of the frame, placed **either side of the product** ("The Mighty" [product] "Mac mini / with M6"). A legal footnote in ~0.8% grey at the bottom of the logo card (#73–#77).
- **Light:** soft overhead key with a long soft contact shadow; the shadow falls down-right on the final card (#68–#72), a flat-lay top-down shot.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Hand-in-frame placement** (#2→#5): the film starts with a human placing the product, a "real object" contract that makes all later CG feel tactile.
- **Physical insertion** (#9→#11): the chip goes *into* the product. The spec (M6) is shown as an ingredient, not a label.
- **Drawn jolt** (#12): a 2D lightning overlay for one frame as the energy trigger. It motivates every transformation that follows.
- **Sprout morphs** (#15→#16, #28→#29, #33→#34, #41→#43, #57→#58): each new ability grows out of the object's body. Arms, wheels, engines, tentacles and boosters. There is never a cut to a different object. The product is the constant anchor, like the manual's centred anchor but made physical.
- **Shrink-to-reset** (#27→#28, #55→#57): the costume is removed and the mini is briefly plain again before the next costume. It is a breath between costumes.
- **Motion carry** (#32→#33): the drift ends facing camera and the engine sprout starts from that pose.
- **Cloud wipe** (#62→#66): exhaust clouds fill the frame and part onto the final flat-lay. The world's own material is the wipe.
- **Push → logo cut** (#72→#73): a hard cut from the title card to the Apple logo on the same grey.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The hand's **shadow enters before the hand** (#2), a real-photography cue.
- The "M6" chip is a **paper cut-out with a rough white border** (#9–#11), deliberately handmade; it matches the clay world rather than a CG chip render.
- The lightning (#12) is **flat, hand-drawn and blue**, the same blue as the M6 chip, so colour links the spec to the power.
- Skid marks are **painted brush strokes** with dry-brush edges (#31), so even physics is rendered in the craft medium.
- During the multitasking hold, **every prop changes state at least once**: pie → bars, bulb off → on, camera flash, the drawing on the tablet advancing. This is the "life during holds" rule, done with a dozen simultaneous micro-events rather than a camera drift.
- The exhaust pipes fan out **like a peacock tail** before igniting (#34), an anticipation beat before the flame.
- The final flat-lay title card sets **type on both sides of the product**: the product is the middle word of the sentence ("The Mighty [Mac mini] Mac mini with M6").
- The legal footnote appears only on the logo card, ~0.8% of the frame height.
- The final card's grey is **a shade darker** than the opening sweep (#73 vs #1): the ending is slightly quieter.

## 6. Pacing curve
Quiet open (1 s of empty sweep, then a slow placement and orbit, ~4 s). The chip drop and jolt are the ignition (#9–#12). Then escalating costumes, each bigger than the last: push-ups (3.5 s) → flex (2.5 s) → hot rod (5.5 s) → robot multitasker (8 s, the longest and densest shot) → rocket (2.5 s). The climax is the lift-off into clouds. The exhale is the cloud wipe to the plain product (#63–#67). Then two calm cards, title 2.5 s and logo 2.5 s. The shape is a pure crescendo: each act escalates scale and complexity, then everything is subtracted back to the bare object.

## 7. What makes it premium
- The product is the only constant and is never cut away from. Every feature is a transformation of the same object, which is object lineage at its most literal.
- **Handmade medium** (clay, paper cut-out, painted marks, real hands) makes a computer feel joyful and tangible; there is not a single spec number or UI.
- One seamless world, one ground colour, for 38 s.
- The ending takes away every costume and leaves the plain object and two words either side of it.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Costume escalation.** One hero object stays at the centre; each benefit grows out of it as a new "body" (2.5–8 s per costume), escalating in complexity; reset to the bare object for 0.5 s between costumes.
2. **Ingredient insertion.** Show the key spec as a physical token dropped *into* the product (a paper chip, a card, a pill), followed by a one-frame drawn energy jolt in the token's colour.
3. **Many small state changes during a hold.** In a long hold, give each of 8–12 props its own state change at different times (0.5–1 s apart) instead of moving the camera.
4. **Material wipe.** Use the world's own stuff (clay clouds, smoke, paper) to fill the frame for 1–1.5 s and part onto the next scene.
5. **Product as the middle word.** For the end title, flat-lay the product at the centre and set the words either side: the left phrase first, the right 0.5 s later; push 1.3× over 2 s; cut to the logo on the same ground.
6. **Real hand, CG body.** Open with a real hand placing the object (the shadow leads by ~1 frame), then let the fantasy start from that believable anchor.
