# Affinity — Creative Freedom ("affinity is free") (94 sampled frames, source idx to 101 ≈ 50 s)

Labels skip source indices (#22=023, #47=051, …), so durations use the source index.

## 1. One-line concept
The Affinity creative suite (vector, pixel and layout in one app) announces that it is now free. The film is a **manifesto on the history of making marks**:
- "YOUR SUBSCRIPTION" is dismissed by "affinity is free";
- a 40,000-year art history (cave carving → painting → paper doodle) collapses into a digital brush stroke;
- the frustrations of subscription-era software ("Missing Plug-in", "Final_final_final.png") are blamed and changed;
- a rapid tour of Vector / Pixel / Layout work follows;
- the word "Free" is performed typographically;
- it resolves to "Creative freedom. Free forever."

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** a warm near-black (#1f1c1c) with subtle grain, for about 70% of the film. It's interrupted by full-colour grounds: off-white (#1–#3, #33), **Affinity lime green (#a3f07a-ish)** (#46–#48, #69–#72), a light warm grey (#63, #74), peach (#66, #75).
- **Palette:** black + lime green is the brand pair. Lime appears on "is free", the Free wordmarks, the app icon and the chair ground. Purple/pink is used for Pixel-mode UI accents and some brush strokes.
- **Type (a deliberate multi-voice system):**
  - Opener: a **heavy slab/Clarendon-style serif**, all caps, huge (~20% of frame height for "YOUR", with "SUBSCRIPTION" set edge-to-edge at ~95% width).
  - The Affinity wordmark: a rounded geometric sans (~8%).
  - "Blame them": a heavy editorial serif, black on white.
  - "Change them" / "Free" / "Forever" / "Craft": a **swashy, high-contrast, reverse-stress display face** (Western/psychedelic), ~12–15% of frame height.
  - End card: a **book serif** at ~4%.

  The type changes voice every beat; that variety *is* the product claim (you can do anything with type).
- **UI staging:**
  - **Almost no UI.** The single persistent UI element is the floating **"Vector | Pixel | Layout" studio-switcher pill** (~12% of frame width, dark, with the active segment coloured: cyan for Vector, pink for Pixel, orange for Layout). It hovers over artwork at #46–#62.
  - A small "My Studio / My Painting / My Essentials / My Type" menu card appears top-left (#63–#67).
  - A tiny Layers panel appears (#60–#62).
  - The full app appears only at the end, **on a 3D MacBook** (#84–#87).
- **Imagery:**
  - photographed artefacts (a stone carving, an ochre cave painting, parchment, a gilt-framed painting, a sketch, crumpled paper);
  - textured digital brush strokes;
  - retro OS error windows (Windows 3.1/Mac OS 9-style grey dialogs);
  - flat colourful illustration (collaborator artwork);
  - 3D chairs;
  - a photographed dachshund.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Hard-cut single-word slams (#1→#3),** then a **ground inversion** (white → black) on "affinity" (#4). The inversion marks the rebuttal.
- **Shrinking-artefact riffle (#11→#15):** each historical artefact is smaller than the last (75% → 55% → 25% → 18% → 12% of width). History "recedes" to a point (the paper square), and from that point the digital stroke is born. It's a scale-down funnel carried by the centre.
- **Paper → stroke (#19→#20):** the drawn line peels off the crumpled paper; the paper disappears and the line remains. Object lineage from analogue to digital.
- **Stroke swarm → error windows (#26→#27):** creative joy is cut against pain. It's a deliberate contrast cut (a hard cut with no carrier, motivated by the argument).
- **Perspective-plane swing (#32→#36):** a thin sliver rotates open like a door, carrying "Blame them" (white ground) and then "Change them" (black ground). The same plane changes from a red grid to a vector path with anchor points, so the metaphor itself becomes a vector object.
- **Zoom into pixels → pull-back to collage (#37→#40):** a 12× macro pixel frame reads as abstract colour, then resolves into artwork.
- **Studio-pill switch as cut (#46→#48, #51→#62):** each click on Vector/Pixel/Layout changes the rendering of the same object. The UI pill is the continuity anchor across ~8 s of hard cuts.
- **Same-object style riffle (dog #55–#62, mountain #63–#67, "Free" #68–#74):** a fixed subject, with the treatment changing every 0.5 s.
- **Walk-cycle match across styles (#75→#78):** the pose continuity carries four illustration styles.
- **Strokes → screen pull-back → icon (#82→#88):** the strokes from act 1 return, the camera pulls back to reveal they're on a MacBook, the strokes form the app icon on screen, then a cut to the clean icon on the ground.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The artefact riffle **shrinks the frame by about 25–40% per cut** while the centre stays fixed. The eye is funnelled into the tiny paper square exactly where the digital stroke will appear.
- The paper doodle (#15–#19) is animated at **stop-motion rate** (a new crumple pose per sampled frame), a deliberate "analogue" frame rate before the smooth digital strokes.
- The error windows' titles are **real subscription-software pain jokes** ("Final_final_final.png", "Looooading…", "Missing Plug-in"), set in a retro system font with pixel cursors (hourglass, hand).
- "Change them" enters mid-word ("Chang" at #34): a type-on that is still in progress while the plane rotates. Two things animate, offset by ~0.5 s.
- The studio pill's active segment colour **matches the mode** (cyan for Vector, pink for Pixel, orange for Layout). At #62 an extra cyan blob appears left of the pill, a hover state before the next switch.
- The "Sit Happen|s" caret (#49) shows typing in progress. The quote reflows from 3 lines to 5 between #49 and #50, which shows live text reflow.
- In "F r e e" (#70) the letters spread apart to make room for **winged money bags flying away**. It's the manual's "word split, insertion", used as a visual pun (money leaving = free).
- End card: the period on "Creative freedom" arrives one beat after the words (#88 → #89): a "late tail" on punctuation.
- The device reveal uses heavy depth of field (the near laptop edge is blurred ~20 px) and an orbit. It's the film's only 3D camera.

## 6. Pacing curve
- A staccato open: three 0.5 s single-word cuts (#1–#3).
- The statement ("affinity is free") gets 1.5 s.
- The history act slows (2 s on the stone), then accelerates (0.5 s per artefact).
- Breath on the paper doodle (2.5 s), then an explosive brush swarm (2 s).
- Pain montage (2.5 s), then a type pivot "Blame them / Change them" (2 s).
- The long rapid middle (#37–#78, ~21 s) has an image change every 0.5 s with nothing to read except the pill and a few words. It is fast but held together by fixed subjects (chair, dog, mountain, Free).
- The climax is the "Free / Forever" type riffle.
- Resolution: strokes return (the bookend), the device reveal, and a 3.5 s end card with a per-word append.

## 7. What makes it premium
- It argues rather than demos: the old way (subscriptions, missing plug-ins) versus the new (free, all studios in one).
- **Continuity by subject, not by camera.** A single chair, dog, mountain or word is re-rendered 5–8 ways in 0.5 s cuts. That proves range without a feature list.
- **Type as performance**: every appearance of "Free" is a different typographic idea (selection box, perspective, money wings, colour inversion), so the key word is repeated about 7 times without ever repeating a treatment.
- UI is reduced to the one control that expresses the product's big idea (the 3-way studio switch).

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Shrinking-history funnel.** Show 4–6 artefacts on a black ground, each cut 0.5 s, each 25–40% smaller than the previous and centred, ending on a tiny object that "becomes" the product's primitive (a stroke, a pixel, a cursor).
2. **Mode-switch pill as anchor.** One persistent floating segmented control stays in a fixed position while the artwork under it re-renders per mode. Colour the active segment per mode, and cut every 0.5–1 s on each "click".
3. **Same-subject style riffle.** Fix one subject (an object, an animal, a word). Cut every 0.5 s through 5–8 treatments that each show a different tool operation (cut-out, particles, stretch, liquify, composite).
4. **Pain montage of dialogs.** Multiply retro error windows 2 → 8 → 12 over 1.5 s, with period-accurate chrome and joke titles. Hard-cut away to the solution statement.
5. **Door-swing plane for a type pivot.** A thin sliver rotates open around one edge (0.5–1 s), revealing statement A on one ground. Continue the rotation to reveal statement B on the inverted ground, and turn the plane into a product-native object (a vector path with anchors).
6. **Keyword typographic riffle.** Repeat the thesis word 6–8 times, each with a new treatment (inverted ground, letter-spacing split with insertions, selection box, perspective quad, a synonym swap "Free → Forever").
7. **Strokes-to-screen pull-back.** Bookend the open's abstract marks; pull back and orbit to reveal they live on a device screen, then let them converge into the app icon.
