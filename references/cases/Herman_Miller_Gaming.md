# Herman Miller Gaming — COYL Gaming Desk (117 sampled frames, source idx to 123 ≈ 62 s)

This is a physical-product film (CGI), not SaaS, and it's analysed for its motion and type system. Labels skip source indices after #93, so durations use the source index.

## 1. One-line concept
A CG product film for an electric standing desk. It's structured as a **spec sheet read aloud**: five chaptered features (Four Premium Finishes → Signature Detail → Sophisticated Management → Easy Access → Effortless Control → Limitless Expression), each introduced by a heavy all-caps label. It ends on the tagline "DIAL IT UP". The spine is **the red coiled power cable**: it's the one colour in a black/grey world, and it appears in nearly every chapter.

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** studio CG in neutral greys: charcoal for macros, and a light grey infinity cove for hero wides (#70–#74, #88–#90, #102–#112). Lighting is soft-box with strong specular streaks on the black powder-coat.
- **Palette:** black, grey, white and **one red** (the coil cable, the magnetic door strap, the drawer pulls, a red lamp/controller as accessories). Wood (walnut/ash) appears only in the finishes chapter and the final configurations.
- **Type system (the most transferable thing here):**
  - The **chapter label** is an ultra-heavy condensed grotesk, all caps, white, ~6% of frame height. It's set **flush to a frame corner with almost no margin** (~1% inset) and moves corner per chapter: bottom-left for finishes and signature, top-left for management and access, top-right for control, bottom-left again for expression.
  - A **HUD frame** of four micro-text blocks (~1% height) sits at the top corners and top-centre: "COYL GAMING 2026", "© 2026 HERMAN MILLER GAMING", "NO. 5977/5477", "MADE FOR YOUR BIGGEST MOMENTS". A spec-sheet margin, it is present on almost every captioned shot.
  - **Callouts** are two small (~1.5%) two-line labels side by side, e.g. "Red power / cable" + "Accents a / distinct design", or "Felt-lined / magnetic door" + "Discreetly / conceals cables". They're placed near the feature in empty space, not with leader lines.
  - Data: a **7-segment-style height readout** on the controller ("▾23.8▴").
- **Imagery:** macro CGI with very shallow depth of field (foreground parts blurred 20–40 px at 1080p), plus exploded-view assemblies and a floating-parts "vortex".

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Exposure flash (#3→#4→#5):** the frame lifts to grey for 1 frame. It's a light bump that marks "start".
- **Perforation → light points → ribbon → coil tunnel (#24→#30):** the film's signature. The row of shroud holes is lit as points; the camera trucks until they blur into a line, the line curls, and it resolves as the end-on view of the red coil. That's **object lineage via light**: holes → dots → line → ring → cable.
- **Down-the-axis push → side view (#30→#31):** the tunnel of rings "unrolls" into the side of the same coil. The same object is seen from two axes.
- **Motion-blur whip (#33, #87):** the subject exits laterally with heavy blur, and a hard cut lands on the next chapter.
- **Continuous pull-back from macro to wide (#36→#41):** a detail to context in one move.
- **Exploded → assembled (#52→#55):** parts converge into the controller that the next shot uses.
- **Whole object → debris → docking (#74→#81):** the hero desk "explodes" into accessory parts that fly at the camera, then the parts land in the shroud. It's a scale/volume transition: one object → many → slotting into one.
- **Framed-inset (#75–#78):** during the debris shot the frame gets a dark border, so the shot becomes a "card" within the frame. It separates the abstract interlude from the product world.
- **Identical-framing riffle (#106→#112):** configurations cut on a locked camera.
- **Hard cut to black for the tagline (#112→#113).**

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- **Chapter label position rotates around the frame's corners** (BL, BL, TL, TL, TR, BL). It's never centred. Each label sits so the product's hero detail occupies the opposite quadrant.
- **Typing artefacts are kept**:
  - "EFFORTLESSCONTROL" with no space (#56) before the space opens (#57): letter-spacing snaps in;
  - "I NA URE" (#28) with the letters S, G, T missing: a **random-letter fill-in** rather than left-to-right typing;
  - "SIGNATURE DETAIL" exits with DETAIL fading first (#33).
- **The "IT" in "DIAL IT UP" enters as a small superscript** (#113) and drops to the baseline. It's a mini oversize-settle in reverse (undersize-rise) that visually "dials up".
- The **height readout ticks up in 5 steps over 2.5 s** (+1.5, +2.6, +1.8, +1.6) and later **down in 4 steps, each from a different camera angle** (#91–#94). The number is the continuity while the camera jump-cuts.
- The red coil **changes pitch** with desk height: stretched at #63–#65, compressed at #95–#100. The accent object *is* the height indicator.
- The HM logo is embossed on the red strap (#49–#51). The brand sits on the accent object.
- The finish list shows the active finish at 100% white and the others at ~40% (#15–#19). It's a slot reel rendered as a static list.
- Callouts **type in their second line after the first** (#46 "Felt-lined" → #47 "Felt-lined magnetic door" + "Discreetly conceals cables"), with a ~0.5 s stagger.
- Very shallow depth of field: in the accessory rail shots, the objects nearest the lens are blurred ~30 px (#86 plant) while the centre object is sharp.

## 6. Pacing curve
- 0–3 s: logo emergence (slow).
- 3–30 s: feature chapters at an even ~5 s each.
- The biggest energy spike is the light-ribbon → coil tunnel (#24–#30, ~3 s of fast truck).
- A second spike is the parts vortex (#75–#78).
- Breaths are the hero wides on the grey cove (#70–#74, #88–#90).
- The ending accelerates slightly with the configuration riffle (0.5 s cuts), then drops to black for a 1.5 s tagline and a 1 s logo. That's a short end hold of about 2.5 s, of which the logo alone is ~1 s.

## 7. What makes it premium
- **One accent colour with a job**: the red coil is the brand, the height indicator, the transition vehicle (the tunnel) and the recurring anchor.
- The **type is architectural**: corner-flush heavy caps plus a HUD margin makes each frame look like a spec-sheet page.
- Light and focus do the transitions (perforations becoming bokeh becoming a ribbon) instead of wipes.
- Macros are cropped so aggressively that the object is only recognisable in the pull-backs. That creates curiosity, then payoff.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Corner-flush chapter label.** A heavy condensed caps label at ~6% of frame height, 1% inset from a frame corner, with a 4-block micro-text HUD margin. Rotate the corner per chapter so the label sits opposite the subject. Type it in letter by letter over 0.5–1 s, and de-type the last word first on exit.
2. **Perforation-to-ribbon light transition.** A row of lit holes → truck fast while defocusing (the dots smear into a line, 1 s) → the line curls into a ring → the ring resolves as the end-on view of the next object (0.5 s). Use it to connect a texture to a hero part.
3. **Axis flip on the same object.** Look down an object's axis (a tunnel of rings), then cut or rotate to its side view, keeping the colour continuous.
4. **Ticking readout across jump cuts.** Keep a numeric display in frame while cutting angle every 0.5 s. The number progresses monotonically in uneven steps, and continuity comes from the value, not the camera.
5. **Explode → dock.** The hero object bursts into parts (inside an inset border for 2 s), and the parts land and slot into a modular rail. Afterwards, the accessories swap on a lateral track every 0.5 s.
6. **Undersize-rise word.** In a short tagline, one word enters at ~60% size and raised by half a cap-height, then settles to the baseline on the next beat. Use it when the verb implies "up".
7. **Active-item list.** Show all option names at 40% opacity with the current one at 100%, synced to the in-place material swap.
