# LaunchAnything — launch-video studio sizzle / agency reel (54 frames ≈ 27 s)

## 1. One-line concept
LaunchAnything (launchanything.now) is a studio that makes product launch videos. Spine: "Your product is ready. Now let's [Launch it] … Trusted by Brands & Enterprises. Any industry. We Launch it all. Launch. launchanything.now [logo]". The reel follows **the brand's cursor** (a blue arrowhead/triangle = the logo's triangle) from clicking a "Launch it" button into its own portfolio playing on a laptop, then to a literal rocket launch. The film is a demonstration by example — its portfolio clips are the product.

## 2. Visual world
- **Grounds:** (a) pure black with type lit by an internal blue→white gradient sweep (#1–6); (b) photographic dusk horizon — orange sky band over dark water, heavily blurred (#7–8); (c) white/lavender-white UI world (#9–15); (d) warm photographic desk scene — laptop, potted plant, mug, venetian-blind shadows raking across a peach wall (#16–31); (e) white with scattered frosted logo tiles (#32–35); (f) black with thin white arcs (planet-rim/eclipse curves at corners, #36–41); (g) real rocket-launch + Earth-from-orbit footage (#42–44); (h) white end card with black logo (#49–54).
- **Type:** rounded geometric sans (Satoshi/General Sans-like), medium weight. Opening words ≈ 18–20% of frame height ("Your", "product"), mid lines ≈ 6–7% ("Now let's", "Any industry", "We Launch it all."), "Launch" ≈ 16% over footage (#42–44), URL ≈ 4%.
- **Imagery:** client work (dashboards, "your shoppers do" ad, crypto trading app "120+ Cryptocurrency, Equity & Commodity pairs", "Place Orders Instantly", a 2D character-animation "that's bleeding money" monster mouth, Slack/HubSpot integration cards) shown *on the laptop screen* — a device mock with photoreal lighting.
- **Atom:** the logo = **triangle over a dot** (#49–54). The triangle appears first as the blue cursor (#11–13), then the rocket (#42–43), then the mark.
- **Button:** frosted glassy pill "Launch it" with a soft blue under-glow and a lavender outer ring — a neumorphic/glass CTA.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Light-sweep type reveal (#1–6)**: glyphs are dark shapes lit by a moving gradient; between words, the sweep exits and the next word's sweep enters (#3 is nearly black) — the transition is the light.
- **Soft dissolve to photo (#6→#7)**, **whiteout (#8→#9)**.
- **Line slide + button entrance (#10→#11)** — the sentence yields space to its own last word, which is a button (text → UI).
- **Cursor press → zoom-through the button (#12–15)** — CTA zoom-through (manual B3) but lands on a *photographic laptop scene* rather than a glyph; the nested frosted rims read as flying through the glass.
- **Screen-content hard cuts under a continuous camera (#18–31)** — the laptop and room light never change; only the screen cuts. Continuity from the set.
- **Hard cut to logo field (#31→#32)** and to black (#35→#36).
- **Rocket through type (#42–43)** — footage and type interleaved in depth: masked so the rocket passes in front of part of the word.
- **Neon URL cool-down (#46–48)**.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- "Now let's" arrives blue and cools to black within 0.5 s (#9→#10) — arrival-state by colour temperature, the same move as the neon URL cooling (#46–48). Two instances = a film rule.
- The cursor is the logo's triangle, tilted like a pointer (#11–13); it reappears on the laptop screen (#20 — a triangle on a dark screen) = cross-reference that the laptop is "inside" the button.
- Sunlight shadows on the wall *move* (leaves sway) during the laptop section — the only ambient life, keeping 7 s of screen cuts from feeling like a slideshow.
- Camera framing on the laptop tightens in two steps (#16 wide → #21 medium → #26 tight, ~1.3× each) as content gets denser.
- Loyalty-program cards (#26) have horizontal motion blur — the client clips are shown at real speed; the reel doesn't re-time them.
- Logo tiles (#32–35) use depth blur: near tiles at the edges are defocused ~6–8 px, far tiles sharp-ish.
- "Any industry": "industry" starts blue-tinted and settles to white (#36→#38) — the same colour-settle rule.
- The period in "We Launch it all." is part of the final word's arrival, not a late tail.
- URL is mistyped mid-arrival "Launchanything.no" → ".now" (#46→#47) — typing cadence visible.

## 6. Pacing curve
- 0–5 s: slow cinematic type (1 word per 1–1.5 s) — Apple-keynote register.
- 5–8 s: the CTA press + zoom-through — the pivot.
- 8–16 s: dense riffle on laptop (0.5–1 s per clip) — the busiest passage.
- 16–21 s: breath: logo field, "Any industry", "We Launch it all."
- 21–23 s: rocket footage climax.
- 23–27 s: URL + logo hold 3 s on white.

## 7. What makes it premium (and where it's not)
- The CTA-press zoom-through into the laptop is a clean causal link: "Launch it" literally launches the portfolio.
- Photoreal desk with moving sunlight makes client clips feel like finished, shipped films.
- But it breaks several manual rules on purpose: device mock used as the main stage, stock rocket/Earth footage as a pun, glow on type. It's a studio reel, not a product film — the "product" is other films, so the device frame is justified by the manual's own rule (someone else's world).

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Light-sweep type reveal.** Word starts as near-black glyphs on black; a blue→white linear gradient passes L→R through the glyphs in ~0.5 s and leaves them white. Next word's sweep begins as the previous word's leaves (overlap ~1 frame of near-black).
2. **Sentence yields to its own CTA.** Type line slides left ~30% to free space; last word arrives as a glass button; the brand cursor flies in (0.5 s, ease-out) and presses; push ~6× through the button's nested rims into the next scene (1 s, ease-in).
3. **Continuous set, cutting screen.** Hold one photographic device set with ambient light motion (shadow sway) for 7 s; cut only the screen content every 0.5–1 s; tighten framing 1.3× every ~2.5 s.
4. **Colour-temperature arrival.** New word/URL arrives in brand blue (or blue neon glow), cools to neutral black/white in 0.5 s.
5. **Footage through type.** A hero word at ~16% height with a subject (rocket) masked to pass in front of some letters and behind others; camera tilts with the subject.
