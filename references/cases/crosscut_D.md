# Crosscut D — Figr, Flashback, Layo, Raindrop

## Recurring patterns
1. **Copy is the spine; one short line at a time, centered, small.** All four films are driven by a manifesto sentence split across cards. Type is small in all four: Figr, Layo, and Flashback use a cap height of ~1.5–2% of frame height, and Raindrop ~2.5–3%. The only giant type is Raindrop's single word "Track" (#26), used once as a punctuation mark.
2. **The newest word gets a distinct "arrival state."** Every film treats the incoming word differently from the settled text:
   - Figr: blur + downward offset on the final word (#63, #72).
   - Layo: ghost/light-blue selection tint plus a caret (#5, #7, #20).
   - Raindrop: an inverted white block and a glyph scramble (#8, #11, #48).
   - Flashback: an inverted black box on the key word (#7) and a tonal fade within a line ("unclear.", #10).
3. **Keyword highlight box as a device.** Three of the four put the key word in a solid box (Figr's "store", Flashback's "incompletely.", Raindrop's many inverted blocks). In Figr the box goes further and *becomes* the next object (#10→#11).
4. **Object-lineage morph chains instead of cuts.** Each film has at least one run where a single element keeps changing form while staying centered:
   - Figr: word box → card → button → tile → photo → phone.
   - Flashback: photo window → hourglass logomark.
   - Layo: Publish button → integration card → chat bubble → phone.
   - Raindrop: ASCII sphere → bar chart; headline → bloom bar → scanner.
   The shared rule: keep the center fixed and change only the container's size and aspect.
5. **Push to one affordance, then isolate it.** Figr pushes 3–4× onto the try-on button and re-seats it alone (#15–#17). Layo strips everything but "Publish" (#48). Raindrop macro-crops a chart label before pulling out (#21→#23). Flashback isolates a photo inside a narrowing window (#30–#32).
6. **Chapter coding by background state, not title cards.**
   - Figr changes the background palette per act.
   - Layo changes the corner-glow hue per chapter.
   - Raindrop changes texture density.
   - Flashback switches light and dark grounds (light for narrative, dark for "epochs").
7. **Macro crops of UI rather than full screens.** Figr and Layo show the full UI once for context (Figr #59, Layo #26), then work in crops at 1.5–3× (Figr #56–#58, Layo #44–#47). Raindrop does the same with its chart (#21–#22) and code (#54).
8. **Endings are quiet.** All four end on a small logo or URL held 2–3.5 s, with no slam:
   - Figr: logo hold of ~2 s.
   - Flashback: blur-fade into empty paper (~1.5 s).
   - Layo: blinking caret, then a lone-caret bookend.
   - Raindrop: dimmed ASCII logo, then the URL in a different font.

## Contradictions (opposite choices and why)
- **Real screenshots vs redrawn UI.** Figr and Layo show actual product UI on frameless cards, because their products are visual and consumer-facing. Raindrop redraws all UI in its own mono/ASCII language; its product is invisible infrastructure, so the style *is* the product feel. Flashback shows no UI at all: its product is the content (the archive), so it is sold through archival imagery.
- **Device frames.** Layo uses a real bezelled iPhone only for the *third-party* context (ChatGPT) and frameless panels for its own editor, which separates the two worlds. Figr never uses a bezel, keeping its phone UI editorial and fashion-flat.
- **Atmosphere source.** Figr and Layo get richness from large blurred color gradients. Raindrop has no gradient at all and gets richness from character texture. Flashback avoids both and uses hairline construction lines plus archival grain.
- **Camera.** Figr and Flashback move the camera a lot (pushes, rotations, pans). Raindrop and Layo lock the camera almost all the time and let type and containers carry the motion. Rule of thumb: the more text-driven the film, the more static the camera.
- **Tempo.** Raindrop is the densest, with a new beat every ~2 s across 50 s. Figr and Layo sit at ~3 s per beat with explicit type-only breathers. Flashback swings hardest, from a 5-image-in-5-s montage to 4 s holds.
- **Emphasis polarity.** On light grounds emphasis is dark (Flashback's black box, Figr's plum tagline). On dark grounds it is white (Raindrop's white block and white bar). Color is reserved for meaning: Raindrop's orange means anomaly, and Flashback's gradients mean era.
