# Crosscut E: Sphinx, Burp and Medium

The three films are very different in length: Medium is about 20 s (a single-feature teaser), Burp about 105–124 s (a product launch) and Sphinx about 200 s or more (a founder documentary; the sampling ends at #400). Between them they span the runtime tiers.

## 1. Recurring patterns
- **The problem is staged before the product, in a different register.**
  - Sphinx: a 1707 shipwreck parable, 34 s of engravings and maps.
  - Burp: a live-action, shallow-DOF "old way" montage plus a Slack cop-out joke, about 17 s.
  - Medium: skips the problem. At 20 s there's no room for one.

  In both long films the problem register uses *different imagery* from the product world (historic engraving, or dark cinematic B-roll) and is bridged by one device: a stipple match onto the CEO (Sphinx #68), or a rewind (Burp #37).
- **A physical or rendered texture ground, swapped per clause.** Medium swaps wood, handwritten paper and chalkboard at clause boundaries. Burp swaps solid project colours in the editor tour. Sphinx alternates maroon and cream, and cream with an orange vignette, per act.
- **The prompt or input is the followed object.**
  - Burp: the prompt card, then the URL chip, then the site behind it, then the outputs.
  - Sphinx: the prompt, then a bubble (#148), and later the prompt swallowed by the rising app (#278).
  - Medium: the note typed into the compose card.

  In all three, typing is proven by a secondary counter or indicator: Medium's 0/280 counter, Burp's travelling rainbow border and percentage, Sphinx's "Thought for 2 seconds" and "Successfully ran a cell".
- **Slot reels with a *fixed hero* and a changing word.** Medium: names beside a photo window (#27–31). Sphinx: Prompts / Data / Outputs beside the cube (#364–371). Burp: post / advertisement / launch beside a collage (#2–9). The rate follows the film's tempo: 0.5 s per item (Medium), 1 s (Burp), 2 s (Sphinx).
- **Selection handles as the brand grammar of "editable".** Medium puts blue handles on the photo window (#28). Sphinx puts corner handles on the keyword box (#101) and the testimonial card (#378). Burp's editor is full of them.
- **Proof by a running number.** Medium: the counter and "57" (#8 → #34). Burp: the percentage (#63–70) and the stopwatch carried across a cut (#217 → #218). Sphinx: 1,256 → 1,400, and the error −0.07° → −0.50°.

## 2. Contradictions between the films (and why)
| Axis | Choice A | Choice B | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| People | Sphinx: talking heads make up about 45% of runtime, hard-cut, not integrated | Burp: only anonymous hands; Medium: people only as data (portraits, names) | Sphinx sells trust and expertise (a founder, a CDO testimonial). Burp and Medium sell a tool's output. |
| Logo position | Sphinx: mid-film at about 45% (#182) | Burp: at about 18–22% (#45) after a URL; Medium: only at the end, plus the logotype in frame 1 | The documentary uses the logo as a chapter stamp, the launch as a reveal after the problem, the teaser as a sign-off. |
| Tilt | Burp: most detail shots tilted 5–10° | Medium: flat throughout; Sphinx: flat, except the #278–291 macro | Burp's product *is* a canvas or editor, so tilting signals "zoomed into a tool". |
| Colour count | Burp: a rainbow logo plus a new saturated ground every 2–4 s | Sphinx: one orange accent in 200 s; Medium: almost colourless | Burp's product output is colour. Sphinx's signal is scarce by design. |
| Glitch/RGB | Burp: RGB split used deliberately (#14, #22, #35, #50–55, #120) | Sphinx and Medium: none | In Burp it's diegetic: the content being animated in the old tools. |
| Counter easing | Burp: linear percentage (about 11% per 0.5 s) | Sphinx: a single jump (1,256 → 1,400) | Burp is showing a process, Sphinx a fact. |

## 3. Numbers and ranges inferred
- **Calibration warning.** In Burp, a stopwatch ticks 3.8 s over 9 frame intervals (#218–227), which is about 0.42 s per frame. A timeline playhead advances about 0.53 s per frame (#200–203). So assume 0.45–0.55 s per sampled frame, not exactly 0.5.
- **Opening silence or empty ground:** 0.5 s (Sphinx #1; Burp #1 logo only).
- **Thesis line hold after the last word:** 1–1.5 s (Medium #3–5, Burp #12–13).
- **Slot-reel item duration:** 0.5 s (Medium), 1 s (Burp), 2 s (Sphinx).
- **Push onto the key message or CTA:** 2.5–3× (Burp #29, Medium #24). Result-chart push: about 1.6× (Sphinx #162).
- **Longest calm hold:** 10.5 s (the Sphinx Pacific map, with a slow rotation of about 8° and a dashed-route draw).
- **Demo "reading" beats:** typing a prompt of 20–40 words takes 3–6 s (Burp #81–92: 6 s; Sphinx #271–277: 3.5 s; Medium #17–22: 3 s).
- **Generation or result hold after completion:** 4–5 s (Burp #71–80, Sphinx #298–305).
- **Big kinetic caption over footage:** 12–15% of frame height, a new phrase every 0.5–1.5 s (Sphinx).
- **Small captions:** 1.8–4% of frame height, consistent across all three.
- **End sequence:** Medium's wordmark is held about 1.5 s. Burp's tagline (3.5 s), logo (2 s) and URL (1.5 s) add up to about 7 s of subtraction.
- **App-tour sub-beat:** 1.5–4 s, alternating flat and tilted (Burp #153–217).
- **Particle morph station:** 1.5–3 s (Sphinx #259–270).

## vs. the manual

### (a) Techniques in these films that are NOT in the manual
1. **Rewind reset** (Burp #37–39). After the problem montage, the film's own opening plays backwards at 2–3× with horizontal smear (about 40 px), for 1–1.5 s, then cuts to the solution. It's a transition caused by "let's start over".
2. **Historical parable open** (Sphinx #1–67). A dated historic failure is told in 5–6 captioned graphic beats (a date on the grid, a stipple portrait with a name, a map with a route, an instrument with an error readout, a casualty count, "N years later" and the successor). Then the parable's render style is match-cut onto the founder. 30–35 s. This only suits films of 90 s or more.
3. **Stipple-portrait → footage crossfade** (Sphinx #68–70). A person is introduced first as a dot cloud (the brand atom) and then crossfades to real footage over about 1 s. Humans enter *through* the atom.
4. **Caption composited behind the speaker** (Sphinx #189–213). Big words at 12–15% of frame height sit behind the rotoscoped head, so the head occludes letters, and the side alternates with each phrase. This is a fourth "human in the world" option beyond the manual's HUD, wash and avatar.
5. **Mosaic-resolve arrival** (Sphinx #10, #47–50). The image arrives as 3%-wide pixel blocks in a tilted perspective and sharpens as the camera levels out, over 1.5–2 s. It's the inverse of B8's pixelate dissolve and works as an *entrance*.
6. **Two-render frame flicker** (Sphinx #23–31). The object alternates every frame between two renderings (an engraving, and an accent-colour dither) while its position jumps, which reads as instability or disaster.
7. **Paper-tear ground change** (Medium #15–16). On a click, a torn-paper silhouette carrying the next texture appears behind the clicked element, then cuts. It works when the ground is physical texture.
8. **Icon-as-letter logotype shout that shrinks into the caption** (Medium #1–2). An 18% logotype with the product icon replacing a letter scales to 4% and becomes the first word of the thesis line.
9. **Relayout slot reel** (Burp #2–9). The words don't roll in place. Instead the caption and collage swap halves of the frame with each item.
10. **Denoise with a linear big percentage** (Burp #61–70). Generation is shown as blur resolving to sharp alongside a large white percentage ticking linearly. Title type types on at about 60–80%.
11. **Still → alive handoff** (Burp #70–80). The verb transition for "animate": the generated still simply starts moving (pose changes every frame) under a 10% push.
12. **URL becomes the backdrop plus scan boxes** (Burp #102–113). A URL chip in the prompt pulls the site in behind a blurred ground, the prompt card shrinks to a corner, and outline boxes hop across 3–4 page elements at about 0.5 s each (the "fetch" verb).
13. **Ground colour taken from the artwork** (Burp #153–217). The solid ground behind the app window is the dominant colour of the current project, and cuts with it.
14. **Running counter across a cut** (Burp #217 → #218). A live stopwatch keeps counting through a layout change, so continuity comes from time itself.
15. **Slit-open testimonial** (Sphinx #377–378). Customer video opens from a narrow vertical slit into a card with handles, ruler ticks and a live waveform on its sides, plus per-word subtitles. It then recedes and blurs under a pull-quote (#394).
16. **Search-bar CTA mid-film** (Burp #40–42). The brand URL is typed into a generic search pill as the bridge from problem to brand.

### (b) Where these films contradict the manual
- **"Never use bullets"** (§3 slot reel, §11). Sphinx #105–112 uses a vertical list with square bullets ("outliers / missing values / poorly documented schemas"). It survives because the items are styled as data tags on a hairline grid and appear one per 0.5–1 s. Suggested revision: bullets are acceptable when styled as *data labels* in the film's own system, never as slide text.
- **"Humans are integrated, not intercut"** (A10). Sphinx hard-cuts to plain, un-graded interview footage more than 10 times (#70, #113, #164, #185, #321…). The interview is the film's home base. For founder documentaries, intercutting is the norm, and integration is kept for the kinetic captions and the testimonial.
- **"One accent colour at a time"** (§2.7). Burp runs a rainbow logo border plus a new saturated ground every 2–4 s (#153–217). This is justified because the product's output *is* colour. Suggested amendment: the accent rule applies to *meaning*. Grounds may take colour from the showcased artwork.
- **"3D tilt on every card" is listed as cheap** (§11). Burp tilts nearly every detail macro (#81–92, #142–152, #163–167, #184–188, #198–206) and still reads premium. That's because the tilt only ever appears on *macros* and alternates with flat full windows. The rule should be "tilt means detail; never tilt a wide".
- **"Glitch preset" is listed as a stock transition** (§11). Burp uses RGB split (#14, #22, #35, #50–55) as diegetic content and as a brand fringe on posters. It's acceptable when it is the content, not the cut.
- **"Numbers tick in 3–7 decelerating steps"** (§3). Burp's percentage is linear (8 → 88% in equal steps, #63–70), and Sphinx's casualty count ticks once (#32 → #33). Process meters should be linear; factual totals can be a single jump followed by a scale-up of about 20% (Sphinx #36).
- **"End card held 2–5 s"** (§2.11, A13). Medium's wordmark is held only about 1.5 s (#39–41). Burp holds each end element under 2.5 s but chains three (tagline, logo, URL) for about 7 s. For teasers of 20 s or less, 1.5 s seems enough.
- **"Arrival state: pick one per film"** (§3). Burp uses both a grey ghost for the newest word (#10–11) and a moving gradient fill in the final line (#238–241).
- **"Scale shout comes after tiny type"** (A2). Medium opens *with* the shout and shrinks it into tiny type (#1 → #2). It's a reverse shout, used as a title card.
- **"Front-load proof; logo by 20–35%"** (§6). Sphinx shows its logo at about 45% (#182), and no product appears for the first 70 s. A documentary structure deliberately delays the brand.

### (c) Manual claims these films strongly confirm
- **One brand atom as the transition engine.** Sphinx's dot/particle does portraits, network, lattice, cube, chart, ring and the prompt handoff (#3, #68, #248–270, #306–359). This is the clearest atom case in the library.
- **Object lineage.** Sphinx's prompt → bubble (#148) and prompt → first message in a rising window (#278). Burp's URL chip → the site backdrop (#102–106). Medium's action bar → floating card → menu → note (#9–16).
- **Letter dropout for step-through verbs.** Sphinx's "Explore" → "Examine" (#255) is almost identical to Merciv.
- **Follow the line.** Sphinx's dashed routes on the maps (#12–16, #51–65) carry the narrative of navigation, and therefore data.
- **Show the verb, not before and after.** Burp's still-to-moving handoff (#70–71) and denoise (#63–70). Sphinx's orange activation spreading through the lattice (#312–319).
- **Chapter coding by ground.** All three: Medium's textures, Burp's per-project grounds, Sphinx's maroon/cream/orange acts.
- **Push to affordance.** Medium's Repost pill (#24–25) and Burp's Slack message (#29).
- **Macro UI plus few wides.** Burp's prompt macros, and Sphinx's tilted log scroll (#279–291) with only 2–3 full windows.
- **Dim the context / depth.** Sphinx's network bokeh (#251–253) and the blurred plus-field behind the prompt (#272–277).
- **End by subtraction.** Burp goes mosaic → white bento → one line → logo → small URL → empty white (#218–248).
- **Hard cuts need a carrier.** Medium carries the repost count "57" across cuts (#8 → #34). Burp carries the stopwatch (#217 → #218).
