# t0.ai (T:0): analysis (211 frames, ≈105 s)

## 1. One-line concept
T:0 is an AI accountant and finance team for startups. The film is **one spoken-style sentence in giant kinetic type**:

> "You're scaling / your business / with AI [code, content…] / but your finances / are run / by you / like in the pre-AI era [2026 → 1920] / everything / by hand !! / Introducing T:0 / T:0 is your AI accountant & finance team / [the accountant, the tax prepper, the CFO] / connected / in real-time / so we understand your data / [demo] / run your business, / not your books / t0.ai"

A hand-drawn mint-green marker layer annotates the type throughout (arrows, underlines, crosses, circles), as if a founder were scribbling on a whiteboard. Real UI appears only in the last third.

## 2. Visual world
- **Grounds change with the argument's polarity:**
  - warm cream (#F3F1EC) for "you're scaling / business with AI" (#1–7, #21–41);
  - pure black for "Scaling" metrics and for the problem ("but your finances are run by you… by hand", #8–20, #42–108);
  - a **mint-green full-frame card** for the product reveal (#109–112) and the demo prompt (#169–171);
  - white for the solution and ending (#113–211).
- **Grid:** hairline dashed lines, a modular grid of about 3 columns × 3 rows, on both cream and black. On #8–10 the grid is **barrel-distorted**, as if seen through a wide lens during the push.
- **Type:** one neo-grotesk (Inter/Helvetica-Now-like, medium weight). Sizes are extreme and mostly large:
  - "You're" opens at ~60% frame height, cropped (#1);
  - most words sit at 12–20% of frame height;
  - metadata labels ("team size", "$10m ARR", "bookkeeping", "the tax prepper") are tiny, ~1–1.5%.
  - The logo is a **monospace "T:0"** with a slashed zero, then a lowercase "t0.ai" wordmark.
- **Accents, colour-coded:**
  - mint green brush strokes mean *good / AI / the path*;
  - hot pink (#FF3D7F) means *wrong / manual* (the X in the circle #57, error cells #100–104);
  - lavender-blue halftone dots stand for "team" texture (#124–127);
  - black rounded-square dot-glyphs stand for roles (#128–136).
- **Marker layer:** dry-brush strokes with ragged ends, drawn on over 1–3 frames and erased by reverse-drawing.
- **Diagram language:** dotted circles (Venn), solid arcs drawn over them, racetrack rings, a clock face, timelines with tick hairlines, a spreadsheet grid (A/B/C, X/Y/Z).
- **UI:** a light app ("Connected accounting map" node graph, chat with a clarifying-question form, table, green/blue bar chart), flat on white, very soft shadow.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- **Semantic colour of strokes.** Green = the right path or AI; pink = wrong or manual. The one exception is deliberate: the green X over "Optimize" (#39) and "AI" (#68). Green crossing out AI marks the old way of doing AI-free finance, so green stays tied to the product's voice.
- **Strokes are drawn with a velocity profile.** A dot appears first (#3, #49 small nub), then the line is extended over 1–2 frames, and the arrowhead arrives last (#5, #52). Strokes leave by *reverse-drawing*, not fading (#63–65, #196, #205–206).
- **Label arrival state = green → black.** Role labels flash mint for 1 frame, then settle black (#128/#130). "run" arrives grey and turns black (#189→#190).
- **Kerning collision.** "AIaccountant" arrives with zero space, then letter-spaces open over 2 frames (#121–123). The words physically make room.
- **Echo-stack slices.** In #12–16 each duplicate row is clipped at a different horizontal position ("S", "Sc", "Sca"…), like a mis-registered slot reel, so "Scaling" reads as motion or growth.
- **Barrel-distorted grid** only on the first black frames (#8–10). It sells the push as a lens move.
- **Tiny "Introducing"** at ~1% height beside a T:0 at ~25% (#110–112): a scale contrast of ~25×.
- **Drag tilt.** The dragged cell rotates ~15° while moving and straightens when dropped (#93–99), like a physical card.
- **The error flood has a hotspot**: a darker pink centre cell (#101–104), so the error has an origin.
- **The ghost zero.** In the logo, the "0" first appears at ~40% opacity mint (#109, #203) before going solid black. It's a two-stage logo arrival.

## 6. Pacing curve
- **0–10 s:** fast, loud type (a word every 0.5–1.5 s) and two ground inversions in 10 s.
- **10–21 s:** a prompt-card slot reel ("code", "content"), then the cross-out.
- **21–53 s:** a long problem argument (32 s!), trucking word by word through circles, a timeline and a spreadsheet. The camera almost never stops, so the argument feels like one continuous pan.
- **53–56 s:** the error flood, then collapse to one cell. This is the climax of the problem half, and the only frozen-camera beat.
- **55 s:** the product name (52% of runtime). The green full frame is the loudest colour moment.
- **56–78 s:** calm white; the definition of T:0 with dot glyphs, rings and a clock. Slower, ~1 word per second.
- **78–94 s:** UI at a slow, reading pace (5.5 s push, 8.5 s scroll).
- **94–105 s:** the closing clause, then the logo. It ends with a 2 s hold on the wordmark.

## 7. What makes it premium
- **The argument is visual logic, not just decoration.** Circles hold words; crossing out is literal negation; the timeline runs backwards to 1920 to say "pre-AI". Lines become spreadsheet rows, and one cell becomes the brand ground.
- **Hand-drawn annotation on a strict grid.** The contrast between rigid hairline geometry and messy marker creates warmth without illustration.
- **The whole problem is told with zero UI for 50 s**, so when the green T:0 card arrives it's a release.
- **Colour semantics are held strictly**: pink appears only for "wrong". The mint green is both annotation and brand ground.
- **Real finance vocabulary as texture** ($100k MRR, $10m ARR, bookkeeping, payroll provider, payment processor) makes it credible to the target audience.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Marker annotation layer.** A single brush-stroke colour on top of type and UI. Draw: a 1-frame dot, then a 1–2 frame line extension, then the arrowhead last (total 0.5–1.5 s). Erase: reverse-draw in 0.5–1 s. Use arrows to lead the camera, underlines for emphasis, an X for negation and a circle for selection. Add a second "wrong" colour only for negation.
2. **Match-cut inversion.** Repeat the last word in the next shot, at 1.2–1.5× the size, on the inverted ground (cream ↔ black), with the annotation continuing. It switches chapter polarity without breaking the sentence.
3. **Echo stack.** Duplicate a key word into 4–6 stacked rows, each clipped at a different x, and scatter 6–10 tiny metric labels around it, 2–3 per 0.5 s. Hold 2–3 s. It signals scale, growth or noise.
4. **Diagram truck.** Put each word of a clause inside a primitive (circle, cell, tick) on a shared line. Truck the camera one word per 1–1.5 s, drawing a stroke from word to word just before the camera moves.
5. **Line lineage.** Timeline ticks rotate 90° and stretch into table rows, then a cell. One cell becomes the ground (scale ~0.05 → 1.2 of frame in 1 frame, landing on the logo).
6. **Error flood.** One bad cell spawns neighbours in a plus pattern, and the whole grid fills in 3 frames (1.5 s) with a hotter centre. Then collapse everything to the single good cell.
7. **Role glyph morph.** Build each persona as a pixel glyph from rounded squares (5 → 13 → 12 → 1). Morph between glyphs every 1–1.5 s. The label arrives in accent and settles black.
8. **Ring → clock.** Integrations orbit a pill on dotted racetrack rings. Push 2× so the outer ring gains clock numerals, and a drawn hand sweeps for "real-time".
