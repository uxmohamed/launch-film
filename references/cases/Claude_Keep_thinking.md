# Claude — "Keep thinking" brand film (178 sheet frames, source idx up to 181 ≈ 90 s)

This is a brand film, not a SaaS demo: live-action photography and almost no UI. It's analysed here for its motion and identity system. Timings use the source index (t ≈ idx × 0.5 s).

## 1. One-line concept
Claude positioned as "the AI for problem solvers". The spine is a two-act argument:
- **Act 1, "Problems"** (0–20 s): the single word PROBLEMS appears diegetically in dozens of materials and places, as an obsessive, comic, surreal inventory of the world's problems.
- **A 2.5 s black breath.**
- **Act 2, "problem solvers"** (23–70 s): people working on problems, with Claude's thinking visualised as light-touch AR overlays on the real world.
- **The slot-line "Keep [Creating / Researching / Learning / Coding]"** leads to "Keep thinking.", then an epilogue of people still working, and the Claude lockup over live footage.

## 2. Visual world
- **Photography** with a warm 35 mm look: soft halation, lifted blacks, teal/amber split, visible grain. Many set-ups are symmetrical, Wes-Anderson/Kubrick-style (#13, #20, #24–26, #34, #161–169).
- **Mixed aspect ratios as texture.** 4:3 pillarbox (#4, #13–14, #20–28, #36–38, #50–51, #60–67, #101–114, #122–130, #135–139, #148–150, #159–178), 2.39:1 letterbox (#12–13, #46–47, #83–90, #97–98, #115–116), and full 16:9 (#15–19, #29–35, #52–59, #69–82). The frame shape flips on almost every cut. It's the "archive/found footage" texture, and it also tells you which shots are "cinema" (2.39) and which are "document" (4:3).
- **Act 1 typography is diegetic, never overlay:** "PROBLEMS" as
  - a green CRT phosphor block (#1–3), with its glyphs corrupting to "DDADI EMC" (#3);
  - a window decal / reflection (#4);
  - a Dymo-style label under a magnifying dome ("Pr", "Probl m", #5–6);
  - a dot-matrix/letterpress print feeding from a roller (#10–11);
  - red letters on scattered paper on a parquet floor (#12);
  - a desktop file "problems.crpt" dragged under a cursor (#14);
  - "ECHO CHAMBER OF INTRUSIVE THOUGHTS" on a pressure vessel (#17–18);
  - a "DEPARTMENT OF PROBLEMS" wall sign (#20);
  - a projected text wall on a street (#32);
  - under a magnifier on a circuit board (#35);
  - neon/LED signage on a tower (#36);
  - a HUD label over a globe (#37);
  - reflected in an iris (#38).

  Same word, about 13 materials, 13 typefaces.
- **Act 2 "Claude thinking" overlays:** thin white/cyan hairline HUD on real footage:
  - lung diagrams and readouts on runners (#85–86);
  - a dotted parabolic trajectory with labels on a rocket launch (#108–109);
  - a pose skeleton with orange joint dots on dancers (#127–128);
  - a dashed climbing route traced over a rock face (#131–134);
  - floating handwritten math glyphs orbiting a man (#161–163, #166–169).

  No boxes and no glow clichés. Hairlines at ~1–2 px and small labels.
- **Real product UI appears only as a prop:** a phone showing "What are you thinking?" (#48), a laptop code/doc screen (#52–53, #60–61, #65–66, #88–90). Always in a device, always out of focus or partly cropped. This is the manual's device-frame rule inverted: here the device is the user's world, and Claude is inside it.
- **End type:** Claude's serif (Tiempos-like) in white. "Keep" at ~4% of frame height plus a **UI chip** (outlined rounded rectangle with an icon and a sans label, ~2.5% height) holding the variable word. "Keep thinking." in serif on a dark teal ground (#151–154). The lockup "✳ Claude / The AI for problem solvers" goes over footage (#172–178).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Hard cuts only**, about 100 of them. There are no morphs, wipes or zooms-through. Continuity comes from (a) the repeated **word PROBLEMS** as an anchor in Act 1 (13+ materials, always near centre); (b) the **overlay language** in Act 2 (same hairline weight, same white/orange dots); and (c) the grade.
- **CRT power-on** (#1→#2): the opening is a scanline opening vertically.
- **Glyph corruption** (#3): letters degrade into a similar-shaped nonsense string. It's the only "type effect" in the film.
- **Cut to black as the act break** (#41–45, 2.5 s), then a gag payoff: the falling piano (#40) lands as a smashed piano (#46) after the black. The black hides the impact.
- **Aspect-ratio change as a cut accent:** nearly every cut also changes the matte (4:3 ↔ 16:9 ↔ 2.39).
- **Slot chip over footage** (#140–150): the sentence is locked while the footage and the chip label cut together. The cut point is the word change.
- **Type → ground → footage → lockup-on-footage**: the tagline gets the only clean ground; the brand lockup doesn't.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- Act 1's "PROBLEMS" is spelled imperfectly in several shots ("Probl m" #6, "DDADI EMC" #3, the half-printed #10–11). Problems are broken words.
- The **file name "problems.crpt"** (#14) gets a small cursor drag: the one computer-UI joke in Act 1.
- **Mirror-gag structure:** the piano falls in #40, the black breath follows, and the piano is smashed in #46. The act break is a punchline.
- The **phone line "What are you thinking?"** (#48) is Claude's prompt placeholder, shown as a prop right after the black. It's the hinge from problems to thinking.
- AR overlays are **locked to real motion** (joints on the dancers, a route on the rock, lungs on the runners) and drawn **thin and sparse**: ~1–2 px lines, orange dots ~0.6% of frame, labels ~1% of frame height.
- The **"Keep" chip reuses the Claude UI chip style** (outlined, rounded, icon plus label, like Claude's mode/tool chips). The end-line is the product's own UI component, blown up as typography.
- The chip icon changes with the word (pencil/sparkle, clipboard, book, `</>`).
- The stippled-cone drawing (#73) is a single-frame graphic insert, a subliminal flash between live shots.
- In the lockup shot the subject keeps moving (#172–178); the logo sits on a living frame, not a still.
- A grain layer is visible even in the black frames (#41–45).

## 6. Pacing curve
- 0–20 s is a fast inventory with cuts every 0.5–1 s. Density rises toward #36–40, then a 2.5 s black breath.
- 23–70 s is the long "solvers" act at a steadier 1–2.5 s per shot, with AR overlay beats as mini-peaks (runners, rocket, dancers, climber).
- The climax is the slot line (70–75 s), where cut and word change are synced at ~1–1.5 s.
- Then the calm tagline card (2 s), an 8.5 s epilogue that breathes after the tagline (unusual), and the lockup held 3.5 s over live footage.
- The brand name appears only in the last 4% of runtime. The tagline sits at 83%.

## 7. What makes it premium
- **One word, many materials.** The problem is shown as a typographic object that exists physically in the world, not as overlay text. That's a type motif without a type animation.
- **The product is a verb, not a screen:** Claude is visualised as a thin layer of reasoning drawn over reality (trajectories, routes, skeletons), never as a chat window.
- **The end-line borrows the product's UI chip**, so the only UI in the film is a typographic device.
- Casting, symmetrical set design and a mixed-format archive feel. Humour (the piano, "Department of Problems", "Universe closed, use rainbow") keeps it human.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Diegetic word inventory.** Pick the problem noun and place it physically in 10–15 materials (CRT, label maker, neon, print, file name, sign, reflection) at ~0.5–1 s per shot, always near the centre so the cuts read as one anchor. Break the spelling in 2–3 of them.
2. **Black-breath punchline.** End the fast act on an action in progress (a falling object), cut to 2–2.5 s of black with live grain, then open act two on its aftermath.
3. **Hairline reasoning overlay.** Draw the AI's "thinking" as 1–2 px white lines and 0.5% orange dots tracked to real motion: a trajectory, route, skeleton or anatomy. Draw it on over ~1–2 s after the action starts. No boxes, glows or sparkles.
4. **Slot chip over footage.** Fixed word ("Keep") plus a product-style UI chip holding the variable word; cut footage and chip label on the same frame, ~1–1.5 s per variant, 4 variants, then resolve to the constant line on a clean ground.
5. **Aspect-ratio as cut accent.** Alternate 4:3, 2.39 and 16:9 mattes shot to shot to add rhythm and an archive texture without any transition effects.
6. **Lockup on a living frame.** Place the final logo and tagline over a quiet live shot where the subject still moves slightly, held 3–4 s, instead of an emptied ground.
