# Harvey — analysis (121 frames, ~60 s)

## 1. One-line concept
Harvey (legal AI workspace) sold as "Intelligence" built from chaos: hand-made paper-collage fragments (brush strokes, scraps, glyphs) assemble into order (cubes, grids, a hub diagram) that frames the product UI. The spine is **scatter → structure → product → wordmark**, and a textured cube with a black dot (the "aperture") is the object that carries every act.

## 2. Visual world
- **Background:** warm off-white paper with visible fibre/marbling (like rice paper), never flat #FFF. It stays on through the UI shots, so the product sits *on* the paper instead of on a digital void.
- **Palette:** greys and charcoal, plus dusty mauve (#8a7a7f-ish), lavender/violet (#6c6680), muted sage/teal (#9fb3ae, a stronger teal in #83), and warm brown (#6e5a55). No saturated brand colour; the teal only turns up as the Harvey project-icon colour.
- **Imagery:** analogue collage. Scanned charcoal and pencil hatching, torn paper edges, crayon-textured polygons, dry-brush ink strokes, halftone/pixel-step "staircase" motifs, letterpress type fragments, lorem-ipsum body copy used as texture (#6, #13 "occaecat"). 3D forms (cubes, extruded bars, bevelled frames) are rendered with hand-drawn textures on their faces, so they read as 3D but *drawn*.
- **Type:** a high-contrast editorial serif (Harvey's display face) for "Good morning, Maya", "Project Titan", "Intelligence." and the H monogram. Spaced-out small caps grotesk for "I N F O R M A T I O N" on paper strips (#74). The UI uses its own small sans. The final "Intelligence." takes ~35% of frame width. The H mark is ~5% of frame height, tiny.
- **UI staging:** (a) a large flat white window, ~75–80% of frame width, with a soft drop shadow, on top of a collage backdrop (#23–27, #103–112). (b) UI chips pulled apart and floated as separate cards in a radial hub around a central object (#28–32, #95–102). (c) single-line UI pills ("Your Templates", "Your Precedents", "Your Standards") pinned onto a line-drawing grid (#54–59). (d) agent-reasoning log text laid out loose around the cube with no window chrome (#91–94).
- **Depth:** small soft shadows under windows. Most depth comes from the drawn 3D objects, not from blur. There is no DOF blur anywhere.
- **Motifs:** cube with a dot/aperture; the chequer/staircase pixel step; the thin construction line (diagonal hairlines, circles, and dots at line intersections, #51–59).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Brush write-on / write-off** (#2–5, #10): the stroke's tail retracts after the head. This introduces the "hand" and motivates the cut.
- **Stop-motion replacement** (#11–18, #74–76): the whole cluster re-lays out every frame. Chaos stands for unstructured information.
- **Pop-over window** (#22→23): the UI card appears over a collage that doesn't move. Order lands on chaos.
- **In-UI text morph** (#24→25, #42→44): the greeting cross-dissolves with overlapping ghost letters, driven by a cursor click.
- **Flat→3D reinterpretation** (#46→47, #62→63): a flat square becomes a box interior/cube while the camera pushes. It turns a graphic shape into space.
- **Object merge match-cut** (#63→64): two cubes collide into one. "Your firm's knowledge + Harvey."
- **Centred-dot match cuts** (#65–73, #82–102): every shot keeps the black dot at dead centre, so hard cuts between wildly different geometry read as one continuous object.
- **Scale-down-into-frame** (#88→89): the ellipse mosaic shrinks until it becomes the square aperture.
- **Macro→wide pull-out** (#106→107): a cropped UI hero pulls back to the full window.
- **Explode-to-logo** (#116→118): the cube shatters outward and the background inverts from dark to paper.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The paper texture is the same plate through every UI shot. The UI window never covers the full frame (max ~80%), so the collage always frames it.
- In #25 the old and new greetings overlap for exactly one frame, with letters offset ~10px. It's a blur-dissolve morph, not a cut.
- The cursor path in #40→42 curves (enters bottom-right, arcs up-left) and the hover state on the row shows one frame before the click.
- A teal radial glow (~40% of frame width, very low opacity) blooms behind the chat input after the matter is chosen. It's the only "glow" in the film and it marks the AI taking context.
- The construction dots (#52–59) move *along* the hairlines before the pills appear. Motion motivates each label.
- Pills pop in on consecutive frames (#54, #55, #56): about a 0.5 s stagger between siblings, which is slow and deliberate.
- The hub reprise (#95) reuses the exact layout of #28 but swaps the centre object from the "Project Titan" card to the aperture. Same composition, new meaning.
- The cube faces in #98–102 are re-tinted pink/teal/grey versus pure charcoal in #95. It's a subtle "activated" state.
- Multiplayer cursors carry role labels ("Senior Partner", "General Counsel") instead of names. That's the legal persona targeting.
- The final H is small (~5% of frame height) with no tagline and no URL. The confidence comes from restraint.
- Collage fragments animate "on twos" (a new drawing per frame at 2 fps sampling, so probably 6–12 fps held), while the camera moves smoothly. Mixing the two rates is the core texture.

## 6. Pacing curve
0–10 s is frantic but low-stakes (stop-motion chaos, nothing to read). 10–25 s alternates UI (calm, 2.5–3.5 s holds) with abstract 3D interludes (2.5–3 s). 25–40 s is the conceptual middle: the cube/merge/kaleidoscope sequence speeds up to ~1–2 s per geometry, with a climax at "Intelligence." (#79–81, held 1.5 s on clean paper, the film's only true breath). 40–55 s is product proof (the reasoning log, the hub, multiplayer UI) at a steady ~4 s per beat. 55–60 s: dark cube → explode → H on paper, held ~1.5 s. The rhythm alternates **noise → UI → noise → UI**, and the gaps get more ordered as the film goes on.

## 7. What makes it premium
- It commits fully to an analogue medium (scanned charcoal, torn paper), which is rare in AI launches, which default to gradients and glass.
- The product UI is shown flat and honest, but always placed *inside* the art world (same paper, framed by collage), never on a stock gradient.
- One object (the dot/aperture) threads through 60 s of unrelated geometry, so a cut-heavy film stays coherent.
- The "chaos → order" metaphor is carried by the animation's frame rate (stop-motion replacement versus smooth camera) rather than by copy.
- There are almost no words: two headlines ("Intelligence.", "H"). The UI carries the messages.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Centred anchor match-cut**: keep one small graphic (dot, 3–4% of frame) at exact frame centre across 6–10 hard cuts between different geometries. Cut every 1–2 s. The anchor makes the cuts feel like morphs.
2. **Stop-motion chaos plate**: build 6–10 collage layouts and swap one per 2–4 frames (12 fps replacement) under a smooth 24/60 fps camera drift of ~3%/s. Use it for "unstructured data" beats.
3. **Brush write-on/write-off**: a trim-path stroke where start follows end with a ~0.4 s lag, on ease-in-out over 1.5–2 s. Use a textured brush, not a vector stroke.
4. **Construction-line label pins**: draw hairlines from the hero object's corners, slide a dot along each line (~0.3 s ease-out), and pop a UI pill at the landing point. Stagger siblings by 0.4–0.5 s.
5. **Hub-and-spoke UI explosion**: put the main object centre, 4 UI chips at the compass points, and spokes as hairlines. Reprise the same layout later with new chips to show state change (tasks → agent outputs).
6. **Flat-to-3D reveal**: a flat square with a bevel rotates or pushes 10–15% while its faces extrude into a cube or box interior. Works as a "look deeper" beat.
7. **Collide-and-merge**: two flat tiles each rotate ~30° into 3D cubes and slide together until they share a face, then cut to the combined form. About 1.5 s total.
8. **Explode-to-mark**: the hero cube splits into 4–6 planes that fly radially past the frame edge over ~0.5 s while the background swaps dark→paper, leaving the monogram. Hold 1.5–2 s.
