# Wabi — analysis (135 frames, ~67 s, 4:3 480×360)

## 1. One-line concept
Wabi is a "personal software platform" (describe an app and it's built on your phone). It's told as a **history-essay film**: "The Macintosh changed the world in 1984 → before it you had to be a nerd → GUI made computing accessible → the same is happening again in software → you'd just describe it → Wabi is the start of personal software." The spine is **one sentence spoken one word per cut**, over archival footage. The product appears only in the last ~20%.

## 2. Visual world
- **Format:** 4:3 frame with archival VHS/film grain throughout. It uses:
  - CRT bloom and chroma bleed (#1–3, #93–94);
  - a warm vignette on "paper" cards;
  - dust and scan lines (#49–50).
- **Two type grounds:**
  - **(a) Off-white, slightly textured paper** with a vignette. Huge black condensed grotesk numerals ("1980", "1984", "1981", "19" at 70–90% of frame height, #5–10, #25–26) and big bold words ("that was", "until", "the", "telling you", "this?" at ~12–15% height).
  - **(b) Kinetic captions over footage**: bold white grotesk with a soft drop shadow. Either a bottom caption (~5% height, "You had to", "be a") or a centred shout at 15–25% height ("GUI", "accessible", "Until today", "personal software").
- **Annotation language:** hand-placed **arrow + label** pointers ("nerd" #13, "Steve"/"Steve Jobs" #27–28, "Steve Wozniak" #29–30, "Mars" #62). Labels append in place ("Steve" → "Steve Jobs").
- **Framing devices:**
  - old photos in Mac-OS window frames with a "Wabi" title bar, stacked as a tilted deck (#35–38);
  - photo prints lying on paper with a shadow (#9, #60–61);
  - the classic Mac CRT screen used as a caption card (#77–86).
- **Modern section** (#104–133): cinematic shallow-DOF footage with **floating frosted-glass UI panels composited into real rooms**, AR-style (#109–116, #128–133); a liquid-glass lens distorting "Meet Wabi" under a finger (#107–108); iOS keyboard typing (#117–119).
- **Logo:** Wabi mark (5 black dots in a cluster), small, on off-white.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Word-per-cut edit**: the caption word changes on every cut, and the cuts are timed to words. Continuity comes entirely from the sentence (the manual's "type is the timeline", taken to its extreme).
- **Odometer year**: numerals swap in place or roll vertically, which moves through time (#5–10, #25–26, #93–94).
- **Screen-as-caption push**: a fixed CRT frame is the title card for 10 words, with a continuous push (#77–86).
- **Label append**: the arrow label grows "Steve" → "Steve Jobs" (#27→28).
- **Acronym expansion** (#43–44).
- **Rack-focus reveal** (#71→72).
- **Whip-in with motion blur** (#87): the only whip, and it marks the jump from history to "today".
- **Bookend reprise of the opening footage** with new words (#2–3 vs #122–124).
- **Liquid-glass lens** over the product name (#107–108). Compare Opera Air's magnifier (#9–13).

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The IBM monitor **types the caption itself** ("green text_", "and command_", #16–18). The caption moves into the diegesis.
- The serif (Apple Garamond) is used only for "Macintosh." (#32). The one font switch is historical citation.
- The year "1981" appears twice with different meaning: IBM PC (#10), then 'apps in "1981"', i.e. apps are where computers were in 1981 (#89–91).
- Photo prints carry real paper shadows and tilt (5–10°). The Mac-window frames have a "Wabi" title bar, so the brand sits in the archival stack (#35).
- An empty paper frame (#73) is a half-second breath before the question.
- In the final shout, "personal" appears alone first, then "software" slides beneath it: a late tail on the key noun.
- The final "personal software" dims to ~50% before the cut to the logo (#133). The line fades as an exit, not a slam.

## 6. Pacing curve
Metronomic word cadence (~2 words/s) for about 50 s, with variation coming only from the *ground* (paper vs footage) and the *size* (caption vs shout vs 90% numerals). There are breaths at #73 (empty paper) and #77–86 (a 5 s locked push on the Mac). The turn "Until today" (#87) is marked by the only whip. The payoff (product) is compressed into the last 13 s, with a short 1 s logo hold. That's shorter than the manual's 2–5 s; the ending leans on the bookend reprise for closure instead.

## 7. What makes it premium
- An essay structure with real archival research (IBM ads, Jobs/Woz, Mars rover, Wikipedia records), so the argument is earned before the product appears.
- Typography does all of the editing. Three type systems (paper numerals, captions, CRT screen) keep a one-word rhythm from going monotonous.
- The historical analogy is visual: the Mac CRT becomes the title card for the thesis about software.
- The modern section uses premium live action + AR glass UI, a deliberate contrast in texture with the grainy past.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Word-per-cut essay.** One caption word per 0.5 s cut. Alternate bottom captions (5% of frame height) with centred shouts (15–25%) on key nouns and 70–90% numerals for dates.
2. **Odometer date.** Condensed numerals at 80% of frame height. Swap single digits in place (1980 → 1984), then roll the whole number vertically out while the next era's image card rises in.
3. **Arrow-label annotation with append.** A thin arrow + bold label placed on a photo. Extend the label by a word on the next beat, then push 2× toward the second subject.
4. **Screen-as-caption.** Lock a period device and put each caption word *on its screen* while pushing in ~1.8× over 5 s.
5. **Diegetic typing.** Make the footage's own screen or keyboard type the caption (a terminal typing "green text_", an iOS field typing "You'd just describe it" mirrored by the subtitle).
6. **Acronym expansion.** Keep the letters of the acronym pinned and type the full words out from each letter.
7. **Bookend reprise.** End with the opening footage, re-captioned so the same image now means the thesis.
