# Crosscut H: Netflix, Coca-Cola, Google Pics, Frontify

Two brand/identity films (Netflix at ~97 s, Coca-Cola at ~91 s), one feature-list product launch (Google Pics, ~99 s), and one wordless teaser (Frontify, ~17.5 s).

## 1. Patterns that recur across these films
1. **The caption or claim is a repeated template, not a flowing sentence.**
   - Coca-Cola: "More ___." ×8.
   - Google Pics: "[Verb] [object] / [qualifier]" ×7.
   - Netflix: four chapter nouns (NEW NAVIGATION / NEW DESIGN / HOT TIP / MORE PERSONAL), each followed by 1–3 explanatory lines.
   - The template is the timeline. Each instance is followed by a proof block of 3–10 s.
2. **Proof-block rhythm is uniform on purpose.** Netflix chapters run 18–25 s with the same internal order (type → UI wide → macro → type). Google repeats an identical prompt ritual 5 times. Coca-Cola beats at a metronomic 0.5 s. None of them varies the tempo much. They vary the *register* instead: the colour pair, small vs huge type, packshot vs lifestyle vs OOH.
3. **Reels are everywhere, and they are faster than the manual says.**
   - Netflix badge reel: 0.45 s per item (#76–84).
   - Netflix category list: 0.5 s per item under a fixed highlight (#141–147).
   - Frontify guideline reel: 0.5 s per step (#12–14).
   - Coke localisation: 0.5 s per language (#78–81).
4. **An atom that becomes punctuation or an interface element.**
   - Coca-Cola's square logo becomes the word separator in the marquee copy (#69–70) and the full stop of "made even more ▣" (#144).
   - Frontify's dot becomes a list bullet (#11→12).
   - Netflix's pill is the nav highlight, the badge and the reaction button.
   - Google's blue is the status bloom, the placeholder, the ghost word and the marker.
5. **The UI or its vocabulary is taught abstractly before it is shown in context.** Netflix shows the badges as a free reel and then finds them in real rows (#75–97). Google's caption performs the feature (Isolate / instantly / one click / AI image circled) before the UI does it. Frontify has no UI at all; the dot and reel stand in for the AI.
6. **The final card is bigger than the manual allows, but it still subtracts.**
   - Coca-Cola: square logo ~30% of frame height, alone on white (#178–182).
   - Frontify: mark ~25% (#32–35).
   - Google: URL ~10%, shrinking to ~7% (#193–195).
   - Netflix: URL ~7%, then the ident ~45% (#190).
   - All four hold 2–3 s and then cut to black or end.
7. **The oversized object is a camera path.** Coca-Cola's words are wider than the frame and trucked across ("new chapter.", "Unmissable.", "timely.", "everywhere."). Netflix's NAVIGATION overflows the frame on its width animation. Google's collage and carousel exceed the frame.

## 2. Contradictions between the films (and why)
- **Type register:**
  - Netflix is all-shout: 12–28% of frame height and constant. Emphasis is by colour polarity and the width axis.
  - Coca-Cola alternates whisper (4–6%) and super-shout (35–60%) *every word*, so size itself is the rhythm.
  - Google is all-whisper (4–6%); emphasis is by a blue ghost, a click and a marker.
  - Frontify has no copy.
  - Why: a brand with a proprietary display face (Netflix Sans, the Coke serif) is showing the typeface *as the identity*, so size is the content. A tool launch (Google) needs the caption readable next to UI, so it stays small.
- **Colour:** Netflix uses 4 chapter duotones plus per-word flips, which is a lot of colour but always in pairs. Coke uses red and white only. Google uses one accent. Frontify uses the customer's palette with one accent. Colour-per-chapter works when the colour *is* the chapter label and the type never changes.
- **UI honesty:** Google shows the real app window 10+ times (#28, #48, #98, #116, #143, #166, #183…), a clear full-window overuse by the manual's rule. Netflix shows the TV screen whole 6 times but always as a TV card, then goes macro. Frontify shows no UI. Coke's only UI is a dock icon. For a consumer creative tool, the result image *is* the proof, so the full window stays on screen to frame the result.
- **Followed object:** Frontify follows one object (asset → dot → reel → card → folder). Coca-Cola follows a **component** (the ribbon, the square) rather than an object. Netflix and Google follow nothing continuous across chapters; they follow a repeated gesture (the pill and the prompt).
- **Endings:** Coke and Google *accumulate* just before the end (grid pull-back #175–177; carousel #187–191) and then subtract to the logo in one cut. Netflix subtracts gradually (type → URL → ident). Frontify multiplies (#26–28), subtracts to one (#30), then the logo.

## 4. vs. the manual

### (a) Techniques NOT in the manual
1. **Duotone polarity flip per word** (Netflix #49–51, #161–162, #105–106).
   - Each chapter has one colour pair.
   - On the key word of a headline the ground and ink swap for that beat (0.5 s), then swap back or stay.
   - It's emphasis without a size change, and it doubles as a chapter code.
2. **Width-breathe headline** (Netflix #13–17, #65–66).
   - Animate a variable font's width axis: condensed (~60%) → extended so it overflows the frame (~130%) → normal.
   - Takes ~2 s, ease-in-out, with a small scale-up.
3. **Echo-letter onomatopoeia** (Netflix #7–10).
   - The word's vowel repeats 4–6 times, each copy ~15% dimmer; the camera pans across the stretch (~1 s), then it collapses to the real word.
4. **Headline stack** (Netflix #69–75).
   - The previous line shrinks to ~40% and rises about one line-height to ~40% opacity while the new line ghosts in below and takes the centre.
   - It's a vertical reel made of *sentences*, not options.
5. **Vocabulary reel → found in context** (Netflix #75–97).
   - Show the UI's tags or badges as a free-floating slot reel (0.45 s each).
   - Dock the last tag onto the rising UI, then show 2–3 of those tags in real UI rows.
6. **Press → iris** (Netflix #110–116).
   - The headline collapses into an icon button (with ghost letters behind it), and a hand or cursor presses it (squash, then invert to white).
   - The button's circle expands as an iris revealing the next screen.
   - The manual has "dot grows into ground" but not a *pressed control* as the aperture.
7. **Aperture line reveal** (Netflix #122–128).
   - Each line appears through a window that widens from its centre while the text slides laterally inside it; lines are staggered ~0.3 s.
8. **Shrink-out / grow-in type swap** (Netflix #130–133).
   - The old block shrinks to ~50% and drifts off-centre.
   - The new block appears at that same small size and grows to full.
   - Continuity comes from scale, not position.
9. **List under a fixed highlight** (Netflix #141–147).
   - A slot reel built from real UI: the selection bar stays still and the list scrolls under it at 0.5 s per item.
10. **Blur-the-world reaction** (Netflix #156–160).
    - Blur the whole frame ~30 px and darken it; one pill sits sharp in front with a sparkle burst, an icon swap and a ~4° wobble.
11. **Word truck** (Coke #14–16, #31–35, #62–64, #125–127, #154–156).
    - The word is set at 40–60% of frame height, wider than the frame; the camera trucks across it over 1–1.5 s, then settles to fit or cuts.
    - The manual's A2 "scale shout" is static.
12. **Size-alternation anaphora** (Coke throughout).
    - A fixed lead word plus a variable word, where every successive word flips between whisper (5%) and super-shout (40–60%) at 0.5 s per word.
    - The last instance inverts, with "timeless." tiny, so that the small word lands as the emphasis.
13. **Atom → label → chain** (Coke #48–55).
    - The brand swoosh draws on, wraps the product as its label, then duplicates into a chain of products along the swoosh path.
14. **Logo as punctuation in marquee copy** (Coke #69–70, #78–81, #144).
    - The logomark sits at cap height as a word separator.
    - Alternate rows scroll in opposite directions.
15. **Outline → fill specimen** (Coke #74–76).
    - A hairline outline with baseline and x-height guides (1 s), then a solid fill.
16. **Self-describing type stack** (Coke #91–95).
    - Each line names a style and is set in it, pushing up one line per 0.5 s.
17. **Localized swap → in-world** (Coke #78–86).
    - The same layout in 4 languages at 0.5 s each, then the same line on a real billboard.
18. **Rack-focus into a tiny word** (Coke #150–151).
    - The macro product blurs out while a ~2% word sharpens at the centre.
19. **Pull-back asset wall** (Coke #175–177).
    - The finale grid contains every image used in the film; the camera pulls back so more cells appear, then a hard cut to the lone logo.
20. **Caption performs the feature** (Google #40–42, #58–62, #85–88, #149–152).
    - The cursor clicks a word in the caption and the word undergoes the feature: it is boxed and lifted (isolate), recoloured (change), translated in place (localize), or circled with a hand-drawn marker (control).
    - This is the most transferable new technique in the set.
21. **Prompt ritual** (Google ×5).
    - Dim the app to ~40% under a white wash.
    - Pull the prompt box forward (85% width, soft shadow).
    - The text appears; push ~3.5× to the send button; the cursor clicks.
    - A dark status pill macro with a colour bloom.
    - Pull back to a placeholder in the output's aspect ratio; the result resolves.
    - Repetition is the point: the viewer learns the product's rhythm.
22. **Status-bloom colour bridge** (Google #27→28, #128→129).
    - The "Generating…" shot's radial glow matches the colour and position of the canvas placeholder gradient in the next shot.
23. **Caption swap with fixed satellites** (Google #104–111).
    - The icons around a caption hold position while the caption text blurs out and in.
24. **Icon portal** (Google #112–113).
    - The app icon is clicked and the app UI fades up behind the icon, which dissolves into its place.
25. **Multiplayer cursors around a caption** (Google #138–142).
    - Two cursors with different hand shapes drift independently around the caption to signal collaboration.
26. **Carousel → pile → URL** (Google #187–193).
    - Outputs sweep on a curved 3D plane with parallax, converge into a stacked pile, and the pile shrinks as the URL blurs in.
27. **Reverse-build to an atom** (Frontify #2–9).
    - The layout builds in 3 steps and un-builds in 2, leaving one object that morphs into a 2% dot.
28. **Non-sequential reel** (Frontify #12–14).
    - The reel's values jump (12 → 8 → 9), which reads as the AI checking rather than browsing.
29. **Random-cell multiply** (Frontify #26–29).
    - A shuffled pop-in and pop-out of an 84-cell grid of the hero object, leaving one.
30. **Negative hold drift** (Frontify #22–25).
    - The object *shrinks* ~15% during its hold to anticipate multiplication. The manual only specifies a push.
31. **Emoji as actor** (Netflix #98–99, #112–113).
    - A tiny emoji at the centre grows while the words part around it.
    - An emoji hand replaces the cursor on a TV (there is no pointer on a remote-driven UI).

### (b) Where these films CONTRADICT the manual
- **"Captions at 1.5–3.5%; one shout per film":**
  - Netflix is 12–28% for the entire film (#5–186).
  - Coca-Cola has 15+ super-shouts at 35–60% (#14, #22–25, #31–35, #43–47, #62–64, #96, #107–108, #125–127, #154–156).
  - For brand or typeface-led films, the rule should be *registers*, not a single shout.
- **"Slot-reel swap 0.8–1 s per item":** 0.45–0.5 s here (Netflix #76–84, #141–147; Frontify #12–14; Coke #78–81). This is readable because the items are 1–3-word tags.
- **"Text readable ≥1.5 s":** Coke single words last 0.5 s (#21–25), which works for single-word cards with no reading.
- **"Show a full window 1–3 times":** Google shows the full app window 10+ times, and Netflix shows 6 TV-card wides. Consumer TV and creative-tool films need the whole screen to frame the output.
- **"End logo 4–5% of frame height":**
  - Coca-Cola ~30% (#178–182).
  - Frontify ~25% (#32–35).
  - Netflix N ident ~45% (#190).
  - For a heritage mark, the logo *is* the hero and can be big, provided it is alone on an emptied ground.
- **"No spinners / AI glow clichés":** Google makes "Thinking… / Generating image…" with a spinning spark and blue bloom a recurring hero shot (#23–27, #126–128). It works because the glow is used as a colour bridge, not decoration.
- **"At most one accent; a second hue only per act":** Netflix uses 8 colours (4 pairs), and flips within an act. That's acceptable because each chapter is a strict duotone.
- **"Vary the mechanism, not the tempo":** Google repeats the *same* mechanism 5× on purpose (the prompt ritual). For a tool whose core loop is simple, repetition teaches it.
- **"End by subtraction":** Coke and Google *accumulate* first (asset wall #175–177; carousel #187–191) and then subtract in one cut. The accumulate-then-cut ending is valid.
- **"Every cut needs continuity from object/anchor/layout":** Coca-Cola is ~150 hard cuts with no carried object between most photos. Continuity comes only from the red/white palette and the 0.5 s metronome, so for brand films "palette + tempo" suffices.

### (c) Manual claims strongly confirmed
- **Chapter coding by ground** (Netflix: teal/mint → blue/sky → aubergine/lilac; no title cards needed).
- **Slot reel with dimmed neighbours and motion blur** (Netflix #76–84; Frontify #12–14), although faster than stated.
- **An atom routes several transitions:** the Frontify dot (object → dot → bullet), the Coca-Cola square and ribbon (ground, punctuation, label, chain, connector), the Netflix pill.
- **Arrival state (ghost ~25–30%, blur-in):** Google #3, #58, #85, #149; Netflix #71. One arrival style per film is kept (Google: blue ghost; Netflix: ghost plus motion blur).
- **Late tail:** Google "Try it now **at**" (#193).
- **Push to affordance ~3–4×, then the click causes the cut:** Google send button ×5, Netflix back button.
- **Device-frame rule:** Netflix frames the UI as a TV because the platform is the point. Google shows the frameless app.
- **Riffle at 0.5 s with nothing to read:** Coke OOH and application riffles (#111–124, #157–172).
- **Logo front-loaded in long films:** Netflix wordmark at 0–2 s, Coke square at 0.5 s.
- **Final hold 2–5 s:** 2–3 s in all four films.
- **Inline icon as word, words parting:** the Google Pics icon between "Google" and "Pics" (#5); the Netflix chili between "HOT" and "TIP" (#99–100); the Coke logo mid-sentence (#78–81).
- **Show the verb:** Google shows the placeholder resolving to the result every time; Coke's outline → fill shows construction.
