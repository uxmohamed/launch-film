# Shapes — analysis (87 frames, ~43 s)

## 1. One-line concept
Shapes is an AI workspace for HR teams. It's told as a **comic "day in the life" of an HR person**, clocked in minutes. The spine is a set of timestamps:
- "Whaaat?!"
- 12:59 → 1:00 → "1:02 Knock, Knock"
- "I'm going to figure this out"
- "We are going to fly"
- "1:45 Knock Knock Knock"
- "I quit!"
- "We are losing our top talent"

It closes with "You know what? It actually is pretty damn hard. / Not anymore. / shapes". The product itself is never shown. It is a problem film, and the logo is the answer.

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** warm off-white paper (#f6f4f0), used for all type-only beats (#1–2, #40–42, #66–87). Everything else is full-bleed photography of a messy desk, or real third-party UI: Google Sheets, Gmail, macOS dock, Maps, Slack, WhatsApp, a calendar.
- **Imagery:** **cut-out product photos with no background** (a tape dispenser, sunglasses, juice bottle, retro digital clock, folder icon, notebook, matcha cup, keyboard, cable) floating on the paper at slight tilts. Stock-photo metaphors (a butterfly, a plane, a space shuttle, Earth from a porthole).
- **Type:** one neutral grotesk, regular weight.
  - Small on paper: "Whaa" at ~4% of frame height, "Not anymore" at ~2.5%.
  - Mid over footage: "We are going to fly" at ~5%, white.
  - Only emphasis beat: "hard" at ~7%.
  - Timestamp captions: white over photos, "1:02 Knock, Knock,".
- **UI staging:** real, recognisable third-party UI at macro (2–3×): a spreadsheet row highlighted, a Gmail inbox with the cursor, the dock with a file dragged to the trash, a Slack composer, a WhatsApp chat on a phone in hand, a context menu over a matcha photo. The UI is **the chaos being described**, so it's borrowed rather than rebuilt. The only "own" UI-like elements are floating frosted notification and calendar cards over photos (#7–8, #36–39) and a voice-note player (#47–54).
- **Motifs:** the clock/time (12:59, 1:00 on four devices, 1:02, 1:45, 0:00–0:04 audio timer); "Knock" repetitions; the prop cut-outs that come back in the finale collage.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Clock match cut across devices** (#3–8): the time readout is the anchor; the device changes around it.
- **Word-append escalation over changing footage** (#43–46): each new word brings a bigger image (butterfly → plane → rocket → space).
- **Pin → card pop** (#15→17): a map pin spawns the ID card.
- **Push onto an affordance** (#28–29, #60–61, #62–65): 2–3× onto the one item that matters.
- **Recall collage** (#72): every earlier element reappears at once around the frame edge. A visual "previously on".
- **Subtraction cut** (#78→79): the whole collage is removed, leaving one word on paper.
- **Attrition subtraction** (#66–71): the grid empties itself, a transition by removal.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- "Whaa" → "Whaaat?!": the letters are *elongated* by appending repeated vowels, which reads as the character's voice.
- The time readout is always centred (#3–8) even though the devices differ in scale and angle.
- The avatars go greyscale for a frame before disappearing (#68–69): a "leaving" state.
- The voice note's timer ticks 0:00 → 0:04 in real time over 4 s (#47–54).
- The escalating metaphor is a joke: "fly" lands on Earth from orbit.
- "Not anymore" is **smaller** than "hard". The resolution is quieter than the problem, which reverses the usual build-to-shout.
- The finale collage keeps the right-side "AI strategy Time" menu and the "I quit!" bubble legible, the two story beats.
- Frosted floating notification cards over photos (#7, #36) are the only "designed" UI surfaces.

## 6. Pacing curve
Frantic 0.5 s cutting for 70% of the runtime (#3–39). The film "breathes" only in the voice-note-in-space beat (#47–54, 4 s), the only long hold, placed mid-film. Then it speeds up again into the "I quit!" / chart / attrition sequence, which is the emotional climax. The collage (#72–78) is the densest frame, followed by abrupt emptiness: "hard" (1.5 s) → "Not anymore" (1.5 s) → logo (1.5 s). It's a comedy-timing ending, with the logo on screen for only ~1.5 s.

## 7. What makes it premium (vs. a normal SaaS spot)
- A comic persona and real HR micro-moments (knocks, a matcha, "I quit!") told entirely through artefacts. No actors' faces are used for emotion except in stock photos.
- The timestamps give a spine without a voiceover.
- The prop cut-outs are photographed, not illustrated, so they have real texture on flat paper.
- The finale reuses every element, which makes the chaos feel cumulative.
- The product is withheld, a confident "problem film" ending on a whisper.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Clock match-cut riffle.** The same time readout, centred at the same size, on 4–6 different devices, 0.5 s each. It establishes "a moment" and a day structure.
2. **Word-append escalation.** Keep the sentence growing one word per cut while each cut's image escalates in scale (insect → plane → rocket → space).
3. **Props as words.** Cut-out objects drop into the gaps between words on a paper ground (the words part ~1 em), each slightly tilted and drifting.
4. **Recall collage → subtraction.** Gather every earlier element around the frame edge (a 15–20% border band), put the punchline word by word in the centre, then cut to empty paper with one word.
5. **Attrition grid.** A grid of people where members go grey for 1 frame, then vanish, 1–2 per 0.5 s, until ~40% are gone.
6. **Quiet answer.** The resolution line is set *smaller* (~2.5%) than the problem word (~7%). Hold both ~1.5 s.
