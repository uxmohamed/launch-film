# Spellbook, analysis (96 frames, ≈48 s)

## 1. One-line concept
Spellbook is AI contract review inside Microsoft Word. The spine is a serif sentence split across the film: "Every deal / comes down / to a contract.docx" → (demo: review for risks → redline → apply all) → "Review entire agreements in se:co:nds" → "and enforce your standards" → "at scale" → (playbook demo → compliance score ticks 12 → 41%) → "The world runs on contracts" → the words rearrange to "contracts run on" → Spellbook. The followed object is **one construction agreement**: opened in Word, reviewed, redlined, then scored against a playbook.

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** near-black blue-grey #12161A for type cards; a **halftone dot-screen gradient** (dark teal-grey dots getting denser toward the edges, like a newspaper screen) behind floating UI (#13–#18, #53–#55, #66–#71); light grey #EFEFEF paper for macro UI (#19–#49, #72–#83). Three grounds, and the dot screen is the bridge between dark and light.
- **Type:** an elegant transitional **serif** (Tiempos/Signifier-like), regular weight, white, cap height ~4–5% of the frame, centred, always lower-case after the first word. UI type is a grotesk (Inter-like). The serif carries narrative and the grotesk carries product, strictly.
- **Imagery:** cinematic stock footage of *the economy* (a bullet train from above, looking up at skyscrapers, aerial motorway interchange, ports/containers, a stadium, a refinery), used only in the opening and in "at scale". It is always seen **through a window** (a hexagon or collage tiles), never full-bleed after #3.
- **UI staging:** real Word toolbar in a light window floating on the dot screen (#13–#16); then the Spellbook side panel cropped and enlarged at ~2–3× on grey paper; issue cards float over a blurred, washed-out contract; one extreme-macro typing shot at ~15× (#37–#39). Soft shadows (≈20 px blur, 8% opacity).
- **Colour:** monochrome, except semantic colour: red "Critical" tags and strikethrough, green insertions, blue selections and "Apply All", a red→orange→yellow progress ring. The four-colour gem logo appears only in the Spellbook button and at the end.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Full-bleed → logo-shaped window** (#3→#4): the footage contracts into a hexagon (the gem's silhouette) between two words. The brand shape is the aperture, and the aperture's geometry mutates per clip.
- **Word-gap close** (#7→#8): words start far apart and slide together as the window leaves. The empty gap stays as a "ghost" of the window.
- **Word → file chip → app** (#9→#13): "contract" gains a Word icon and ".docx", the cursor double-clicks it, and the document opens. Type-to-UI with the product's native object.
- **Click → panel push** (#16→#18).
- **Prompt → status pill with echo word** (#23→#24): a sent prompt becomes a pill; a giant outline word behind it repeats the pill's noun.
- **Card pull-back into layout** (#27→#30): a macro issue card zooms out into its slot among other cards.
- **Caret-follow extreme macro** (#36→#39): the camera dives to ~15× and trucks with the typing.
- **Button press → dark checklist** (#49→#50): "Apply All" → a full-frame list of resolved issues turning into checks as it scrolls.
- **List → card** (#52→#53): the huge checklist scales down into a small card on the dot ground.
- **Collage insertion between words** (#62→#63): footage tiles push "at" and "scale" apart.
- **Word-reorder to logo** (#86→#89): "The world runs on contracts" → "contracts run on" → Spellbook, inside rotating orbit lines.
- **Fade to black** (#94→#96): the only fade in the film.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The hexagon window's **shape is irregular and changes per clip** (#4 skewed, #5 elongated, #6 regular). It reads like a gem rotating in 3D, showing different facets.
- The giant background words during the status pills (#24–#26) are **outlined in pale grey, ~8% contrast**. They are texture, not reading material, but they match the pill: "clauses" behind "Extracting clauses…".
- "se:co:nds": the colons turn "seconds" into a **stopwatch readout**. One word is set to show time passing, and the "co" flickers (#59).
- In the checklist (#50–#52) the unchecked marks are **dashed rings** (like loading spinners) that complete into solid blue checks as they scroll to the top. Resolving is shown as loading finishing.
- The Josh comment (#45) is a **human** reviewer note, a small reminder that Spellbook works with collaborators.
- The dragged "SaaS Golden Playbook.docx" (#67–#68) has a **file-drag ghost** with its own shadow; the list opens a gap for it before the drop (#69→#70).
- The score ring's colour is **value-mapped** (red at 12–14%, orange at 29%, yellow at 41%), and the card grows ~30% as it ticks. Emphasis by scale on the proof.
- Serif lines use a **dim-the-old-line** rule: when "to a contract.docx" arrives, "comes down" drops to ~40% (#10–#12).
- The orbit lines around the thesis have **small dots riding them** (satellites), echoing the ring motif from the score.

## 6. Pacing curve
The open is cinematic and fast (0.5 s footage, #1–#6). It slows into serif type (#7–#12, 3 s). The demo runs at 1.5–2.5 s per beat, with the extreme macro typing (#37–#39) as the tactile peak. The "Apply All" → dark checklist (#49–#55) is the first payoff. Then a type breath (#56–#62, 3.5 s). The collage "at scale" re-energises (#63–#65). The second demo is calmer (#66–#83), climaxing on the ticking score. The ending is a slow thesis with a word-shuffle, the logo held 2.5 s, then a 1 s fade. It front-loads the problem, back-loads the brand.

## 7. What makes it premium
- **Two type voices with strict jobs:** a literary serif for the story and a grotesk for the product. The serif makes legal tech feel like publishing, not software.
- The footage is never allowed to be a stock-video montage after #3. It is **caged in the brand's gem shape** or collaged between words, so it stays graphic.
- The camera goes to **~15× on the text being typed**. It is the most extreme macro in the library so far and makes a legal redline feel physical.
- The status pill's loading state is **amplified by a giant background echo word**, turning a 1.5 s wait into a designed beat.
- Semantic colour only: red is risk, green is inserted text, blue is action. The logo's four colours are held back until the end.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Logo-shaped aperture between words.** Two words sit ~30% of the frame apart; between them a window in the brand mark's silhouette shows footage; each 0.5 s the clip swaps and the window's facets reshape (like a gem turning). On exit the window shrinks to zero and the words close the gap over ~0.5 s (ease-in-out).
2. **Word → native file chip.** The key noun in the line gains the app's file icon and an extension typed on (".docx"), is boxed, and the cursor double-clicks it to open the product.
3. **Echo word behind status.** While a status pill cycles verbs every 0.5 s, the pill's noun is set huge (~50% of frame height) as a pale outline behind it, drifting horizontally ~5%/s.
4. **Caret-follow extreme macro.** Push to ~15× so 2–3 words fill the frame; truck the camera with the caret at typing speed; then snap back to the card (0.5 s) to show where the edit lives.
5. **Checklist completion scroll.** A huge full-frame list of issues scrolls up at 1 row per 0.5 s; each row's dashed ring completes into a check as it passes the upper third; then the list scales down into a small card.
6. **Time-word typography.** Insert a clock/stopwatch colon into the word "seconds" ("se:co:nds") and blink it. A single glyph change carries the claim.
7. **Word-shuffle into logo.** A thesis line's words scatter and reorder into a new phrase (0.5 s per rearrangement) inside slowly rotating orbit lines, then collapse to the wordmark.
