# Crosscut L — Opera Air, Walmart, Zaro, Wabi, Shapes

Five films that span the whole range:
- a meditative product film (Opera Air, 61 s);
- a brand-identity film (Walmart, 62 s);
- a classic SaaS launch (Zaro, 69 s);
- a history-essay manifesto (Wabi, 67 s, 4:3);
- a comedic problem film (Shapes, 43 s).

## 1. Patterns that recur across them

1. **A centred anchor survives every cut.**
   - Opera Air: the bubble.
   - Walmart: the Spark at the exact centre (#21–28, #118–124).
   - Shapes: the clock readout centred across 5 devices (#3–8).
   - Zaro: the dot that opens and closes the logo (#18, #132).
   - Wabi: a locked Mac CRT for 10 words (#77–86).

   Four of five films do this. The anchor is always *centred* and *the same size*, whatever the context.
2. **Couplet type as the film's skeleton.** Two-line or two-part statements whose second half arrives later, typed or appended:
   - Opera Air: "Create focus / with Binaural Beats".
   - Zaro: "Build an app. / With a sentence." (×7).
   - Walmart: "Save Money. / Live Better.".
   - Shapes: "It actually is pretty damn / hard. / Not anymore".
   - Wabi's sentence is a long single chain, the extreme form.
3. **Inline objects inside sentences.**
   - Zaro: app card and agent pill between words (#3), app card in "You didn't just [card] build an app" (#119).
   - Wabi: iFood icon tossed in after "one" (#100).
   - Shapes: props as words, "figure [matcha] this [cable] out." (#41–42).

   The words part to admit the object, and the object is always slightly tilted (5–15°) and photographic or real-UI.
4. **Word-append over hard cuts.** The line grows one word per 0.5 s cut while the image underneath changes each time (Wabi throughout; Shapes #43–46; Walmart "Welcome to your Walmart" #114–117). The sentence holds continuity while the picture riffles.
5. **Real-time tickers as proof of life.** Opera Air's player clock 00:34→00:36 (#57–60); Shapes' voice note 0:00→0:04 (#47–54); Zaro's chart values (#55–57); Wabi's odometer (#65). These are **real-time** second counters, not the manual's decelerating counters.
6. **Push onto the one affordance.** 2–3× pushes onto a single menu item, chat bubble, chart point or card (Shapes #28, #60, #63; Zaro #80–86; Wabi #29). Every film does this at least once; none uses a 10×+ zoom-through.
7. **Ending on a dot or mark on quiet paper.** Zaro (dot → mark), Walmart (Spark), Wabi (5-dot mark), Shapes (icon + wordmark), Opera Air (O inside the bubble). All are small (4–15% of frame height) and centred on the film's lightest ground.

## 2. Contradictions (opposite choices, and why)
| Axis | Choice A | Choice B | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cut rate | Opera Air: 4–6.5 s text beats, almost no cuts | Walmart, Wabi and Shapes: 0.5 s cuts for 70–90% of runtime | Opera Air sells calm, so the *edit rate is the product promise*. Brand, essay and comedy films use tempo as energy |
| UI source | Opera Air: UI re-modelled as 3D glass hardware | Shapes: real third-party UI (Gmail, Sheets, WhatsApp) shown as-is, at macro | Shapes shows *the problem* (others' tools = chaos), so borrowed UI is correct. Opera shows *the product*, so it's idealised |
| Product visibility | Zaro: product on screen ~60% of the film | Shapes: 0% product; Wabi: ~15%, only in the last 13 s | Problem and manifesto films withhold the product to make the logo the answer |
| Emphasis direction | Walmart "Bol" at 80% height; Wabi "personal" at 20% | Shapes: the answer "Not anymore" is *smaller* (2.5%) than the problem ("hard", 7%) | A quiet answer reads as confidence in a comedy register |
| Cursor | Zaro and Shapes: cursor present in almost every UI shot | Opera Air: no cursor at all, selection by highlight | A cursor implies effort, and Opera's promise is effortlessness |
| Logo timing | Zaro: logo at ~7 s (#14–17), front-loaded | Wabi and Shapes: logo only in the final 1–1.5 s | Zaro is a launch (name recall). Essay and comedy films need the payoff at the end |
| Frame format | Wabi: 4:3 with archival grain | the others: 16:9 clean | Wabi's format *is* the historical argument |

## 3. Numbers inferred from these five films
- **Cut cadence in montage films:** 0.5 s per shot (1 frame at 2 fps) is the dominant unit in Walmart, Wabi and Shapes. Runs of 6–20 consecutive 0.5 s cuts are common (Wabi #54–72: 19 cuts).
- **Word-per-cut rate:** ~2 words/s (Wabi). Two-word captions per cut are also fine ("the world", "in their").
- **Couplet timing (Zaro):**
  - line 1 appears at once;
  - line 2 is typed at ~5 characters per frame (~10 chars/s);
  - the full couplet is held 0.5–1 s;
  - total 2–2.5 s per couplet.
- **Opera Air text beats:** 4–6.5 s including a ~1.5–3 s hold. Letter typing ~3 chars/s; the blur-in of a word takes ~2 s.
- **Push onto an affordance:** 2–3× over 0.5–1 s (Shapes #28–29, #60–61, #62–65).
- **Pull-backs:**
  - Zaro orbit field ~4× over ~2.5 s (#121–126);
  - node graph ~2.5× (#63–66);
  - Walmart scene → grid tile ~2.5× in 1 frame;
  - Walmart crop-shout reveal ~5× in 1 frame (#13→14);
  - end-card mark scale-out ~8× in 1 frame (#118→119).
- **Big numerals:** Wabi's years at 70–90% of frame height; Walmart's crop at ~80%. Chant words at ~15%.
- **Captions:** Wabi bottom captions ~5%; Zaro couplets ~5%; Opera Air ~2.5%; Shapes ~2.5–7%.
- **Final hold:** Zaro 2.5 s, Opera Air 3–3.5 s, Walmart 3 s + 0.5 s black, Shapes 1.5 s, Wabi 1 s. Range **1–3.5 s**.
- **Breath rings:** ~2.5 s expand + ~2.5 s contract (Opera Air #94–105).
- **Status log (Zaro):** ~2 lines/s append rate; older lines at ~40% opacity.

## 4. vs. the manual

### (a) Techniques NOT in the manual
1. **Soft-body atom stage** (Opera Air). A simulated, constantly deforming bubble (±3–6% rim wobble per frame) holds all copy inside it for the whole film and refracts the per-act ground colour. This goes past "atom as transition engine": the atom is a *stage* that never leaves. Recipe: bubble at 60–75% of frame height, text at 2.5% inside it, ground hue swapped per act behind it; cut only UI interludes.
2. **Semantic arrival state** (Opera Air #28–37). The arrival effect is chosen per *word meaning*: "focus" blurs into focus, "Create" types. This contradicts "one arrival state per film"; the rule becomes "one per meaning".
3. **Backspace slot reel** (Zaro #67–75). The variable word is deleted and retyped with the caret instead of rolled. The background prop set re-populates per word (files vs. tool logos).
4. **Agent-as-cursor character** (Zaro #3, #41, #52). Each AI agent gets a coloured pointer + name flag. It is introduced as pixel-art "characters" floating round the headline, then the same-coloured cursor performs a real UI action. The manual's "cursor as actor" covers one human cursor, not multiple AI actors.
5. **Status-log column → headline** (Zaro #107–111). Process verbs append at 2 lines/s with older lines fading to 40%. The last item ("Read Slack") stays put while the words around it change into the next headline.
6. **Glow-card generation** (Zaro #27). A blank card with a soft outer glow for ~0.5 s = "generating", with no shimmer or spinner. Add it to the spinner-replacement list.
7. **Dot iris → dot → logo** (Zaro #131–133). The scene irises into a single dot on a new ground, and the dot splits into the mark. The reverse of "dot grows into ground".
8. **Word-per-cut essay** (Wabi). Captions change on *every* cut at ~2 words/s for 50 s. Variety comes only from switching the type system (bottom caption / centred shout / 80% numerals on paper / on-screen CRT).
9. **Odometer date** (Wabi #4–10, #93–94). Huge numerals swap digits in place, then roll vertically out while the next era's photo card rises in. RGB split on the "digital era" numerals.
10. **Arrow-label with append** (Wabi #13, #27–30, #62). A documentary annotation: thin arrow + bold label, label grows by a word, then a 2× push to the next subject.
11. **Screen-as-caption push** (Wabi #77–86). A period device's screen is the title card for 10 successive words during one continuous 1.8× push.
12. **Diegetic typing of the subtitle** (Wabi #16–18, #117–119). The footage's own screen types the caption words.
13. **Acronym expansion** (Wabi #43–44). "GUI" letters stay pinned while the full words type out around them.
14. **Bookend footage reprise with new words** (Wabi #2 → #122). The same archival shot re-captioned with the thesis.
15. **Centred-mark placement riffle** (Walmart #24–28) and **clock match-cut riffle** (Shapes #3–8). Riffle real-world contexts while one element stays at an identical centre position and size. This is related to the manual's "centred anchor" but motivated by *proof of ubiquity or time* rather than geometry.
16. **Palette-stripe wipe** (Walmart #38–41). Brand colour bars sweep across and the last bar becomes the ground.
17. **Inset card system with a visible gutter** (Walmart #16–19, #48). Chapter cards are rounded rectangles with a ~2% white margin, pushed sideways like a carousel.
18. **Scene → grid tile → other tile expands** (Walmart #75–83). A two-way nest through a collage grid.
19. **Crop-to-word shout** (Walmart #13→14). Open on a letter crop at 80% of frame height, then reveal the word with a ~5× pull-back in one frame.
20. **Recall collage → subtraction** (Shapes #72–79). Every earlier prop and UI crop is gathered around the frame border for the punchline, then all removed at once to leave one word.
21. **Attrition grid** (Shapes #66–71). People tiles go grey for a frame, then vanish 1–2 at a time. The data story is told by subtraction.
22. **Word-append escalation** (Shapes #43–46). Each appended word cuts to a bigger-scale image (butterfly → plane → shuttle → orbit).
23. **Props as words** (Shapes #40–42). Photographic cut-out objects inserted between words (the manual only has product *icons* as words).
24. **Real-time ticker** (Opera Air #57–60, Shapes #47–54). A media timer advancing at real speed during a hold, as life and proof. This differs from the manual's decelerating counters.
25. **Icon-as-object pull-back** (Opera Air #9–13). Open inside a giant glyph (a magnifier lens magnifying UI) and pull back until it reads as the search icon. Wabi's liquid-glass lens over "Meet Wabi" (#107–108) is the same family.
26. **Glass-hardware UI** (Opera Air #14–27, #79–84). Components re-modelled as frosted-glass physical objects on a tilted plate, with icons stacking in one by one along the diagonal truck.

### (b) Contradictions of the manual's rules or numbers
- **"Pace for reading; speed only where not reading"**: Wabi and Shapes put readable caption words on 0.5 s cuts for most of the film (Wabi #11–72; Shapes #3–39). Single words of 1–2 syllables are readable in one 0.5 s beat, so the manual's "line readable 1.5–2.5 s" applies to *lines*, not to word-per-cut captions.
- **Final logo hold 2–5 s**: Wabi holds ~1 s (#134–135) and Shapes ~1.5 s (#85–87). Both lean on a bookend or punchline for closure instead. The lower bound should be ~1 s for manifesto and comedy films.
- **"Never screen-record / rebuild the UI"**: Shapes deliberately uses real third-party UI (Gmail, Sheets, WhatsApp, macOS dock, #9–13, #58–61). The rule should be "rebuild *your* UI; borrow *theirs* when it represents the problem".
- **"One arrival state per film"**: Opera Air uses two in the same line on purpose (per-letter typing for "Create", blur-in for "focus", #28–37). Zaro uses lavender-on-arrival *and* a second lavender pulse after completion (#8).
- **"End by subtraction; nothing slams"**: Walmart ends with a one-frame ~8× scale-out from a macro Spark (#118→119), a snap, not a fade. It still reads as premium because the ground is emptied first.
- **"Scale shout once per film, at 25–40% height"**: Walmart shouts at ~80% (#13) and chants at ~15% for five words. Wabi uses 70–90% numerals at least four times (#5–10, #25–26, #93–94). In brand and essay films, huge type is a *system*, not a single shout.
- **Zoom-through 10–12× as the climax move**: none of the five uses it. The climaxes are the pull-back (Zaro ~4× orbit field), the breath hold (Opera Air) and subtraction (Shapes).
- **Device-frame rule (a bezel = someone else's world)**: Wabi shows its own product in an iPhone in hand (#106, #120, #127), and Walmart shows its own app on phones and laptops (#64–68, #88–90, #96). For consumer mobile products, the device is the product's home, not someone else's world.

### (c) Manual claims these films strongly confirm
- **The film is a sentence; type is the timeline.** Every film here has one: Zaro's seven couplets, Wabi's single chained sentence, Walmart's adjective chant, Shapes' timestamps, Opera Air's bubble lines.
- **Chapter coding by ground.** Opera Air (blue → lilac → teal → blue), Zaro (brown for type, greige for UI), Walmart (blue, yellow and navy per chant word).
- **Brand atom as transition engine.** Zaro's dot and caret, Walmart's Spark, Opera Air's bubble. These go further than the manual: the atom holds position through dozens of cuts.
- **Life during holds.** Opera Air's bubble wobble never stops; Shapes' props drift; Zaro's pixel cursors float.
- **Bookend the film.** Opera Air (bubble on blue, #5 ↔ #121), Wabi (the same Macintosh footage, #2 ↔ #122), Zaro (the pixel field at the bottom-left, #7 ↔ #134, plus the dot at #18 ↔ #132).
- **Follow one object.** Zaro's Deal Tracker app and FluxCo question; Shapes' one HR person's day, told through clock times.
- **Replace spinners with texture.** Zaro's glow card, "Planning…" shimmer and status log.
- **Inline icons as words** (Aside in the manual). Confirmed and extended by Zaro, Wabi and Shapes.
- **Nesting scenes into cards and grid cells.** Walmart's scene → tile → tile expands; Zaro's app card → orbit hub.
