# Shopify (Shopify Design site reel), analysis (69 frames, ≈34.5 s)

*A brand and studio showcase rather than a product demo. It appears to be a reel of Shopify's design website (#7 shows a site header with "design" top-left and a nav top-right). Read it for how a website's own interactions become film transitions.*

## 1. One-line concept
Shopify's design team presents its site and culture. The spine is the site's own scroll order: hero "Make the new normal" → a masonry of projects (Editions, Renaissance, GT Racing car, Shop Pay "$500.00", team Codex cards) → people photo decks → a clock dial showing "Every 26 seconds, a merchant makes their first sale" → "Remote by design. Together in Toronto / Ottawa / New York / Montreal / Bellevue" → a dive into a dark 3D point-cloud space of work fragments → a yellow 3D object. The followed thread is **the page itself**: each section is entered the way you'd scroll or click into it, but at film speed with cinematic effects between sections.

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** mostly **pure white** (#1–#3, #6–#10, #16–#25, #30–#55), punctuated by black: a light-burst (#4), a dark 3D racing world (#13–#15), a final dark 3D grid space (#56–#69). Colour cards for the people decks: pale periwinkle (#26), Shopify red-orange #E8573A (#27–#28), deep green #22382F (#29).
- **Type:** a heavy grotesk (Shopify's "GT Standard"-like bold), black, tight leading. The hero is ~10% cap height, and the city names reach ~12%. Accent type is in **red-orange** ("Remote by design."). There is a signature **small solid square bullet** before words ("■Make", "■Remote", "■design", "■New York") and a **full-stop dot** after them ("the.", "Together●", "Montreal."). The square and the dot are the brand punctuation atoms.
- **Imagery:** an eclectic collage: pink "NEW INVENTORY" repeated-type posters, Renaissance paintings (a figure in billowing robes with a pink skateboard, #11–#12), a green/blue livery racing car in a dark 3D void with floating merch (T-shirts, caps) in glass boxes (#13–#15), phones with Shop Pay, portrait photo cards, claymation-like 3D product objects (roller skate, handbag, toy, Polaroid camera, #44–#47), pixel-sorted/glitch point clouds (#56–#66).
- **Staging:** the site is shown **full-bleed and flat** (the web page *is* the frame; no browser chrome except a hint in #7) and then elements are pulled out of it into floating cards and 3D spaces. Phones use a real bezel (#16–#19).

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Converging words** (#2→#3): two small words at the frame's opposite sides fly together and scale up into the hero lockup.
- **Light-burst flash** (#3→#4): a radial white explosion on black, one frame, then a glitch settle (#5–#6). A reset "boom" used once at the top and once near the end (#55).
- **Glitch decay** (#5→#6): RGB/pixel-sort blocks at the edges clear in 2 frames.
- **Site scroll** (#6→#10): the film scrolls the actual page; the masonry has parallax.
- **Tile → full-bleed** (#10→#11): a project tile becomes the full frame (its content: a painting).
- **Hard cut to a 3D world** (#12→#13): the flat GT Racing tile becomes a real 3D scene.
- **Frosted-to-sharp focus pull** (#16→#17).
- **Card row slide-in** (#20→#25), **ground colour cuts under a fixed deck** (#26→#29): the chapter-coding-by-ground idea at 2/s.
- **3D ticks → flat dial** (#32→#39): floating 3D pills settle into the minute marks of a clock. Dimensional collapse as a transition.
- **Outline → solid numeral** (#42→#43), then a **push into the numeral** (#43→#44) and a **tick-down 26 → 25** (#45→#46).
- **Inline image in a word gap** (#52→#54): "New ▭ York", the photo opens between words.
- **Particle disintegration → 3D fly-through** (#55→#56): the flat lockup explodes, and its words reappear as extruded 3D letters the camera flies through.
- **Pixel-sort streak space** (#58→#66): all imagery is rendered as streaked point clouds in a dark grid, so the ending is atmosphere rather than information.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The **square bullet and the period** are the typographic atoms: "■Make … the." (#2), "■Remote", "■design", "Together●", "■New York", "Montreal." (#48–#51). Tiny dots also mark the corners of an invisible frame in #2 and #48–#50 (registration marks).
- The two words in #2 are placed **at the extreme left and right thirds** before converging. The layout starts as a spread and closes like a curtain.
- The glitch in #5 is **colour-channel-split rainbow** only on "new", which is the word being claimed ("new normal").
- On the phone (#16) the frosted glass blur is ~15 px and clears in one frame; the "$500.00" is the only large text.
- In the photo decks the **card fan angles stay constant** while the ground colour and photos change: a fixed layout slideshow at 2 changes per second.
- The dial's orange hand is the same orange as "Remote by design." Orange = time and place.
- The number counts **down** (26 → 25), echoing the copy about a sale every 26 seconds. A live clock rather than a stat tick-up.
- City names carry **tiny mono metadata** (coordinates or time zones) right-aligned beside them (#52), ~0.6% height, texture-level.
- In the 3D space the city letters retain the **red accent** on some glyphs (#56 red "d", "n"), so the colour code survives the dimensional change.

## 6. Pacing curve
It is fast from the first second: 0.5 s beats for the hero (#1–#6), a 1 s scroll, then ~1–1.5 s per project. The team cards and photo decks (#20–#31) are the steadiest passage (~5 s). The clock sequence (#32–#47, ~8 s) is the centrepiece and has the only real "reading" beat (the "26" with copy). "Remote by design" (#48–#55) is a 4 s typographic peak, then the particle burst and a 7 s dark 3D fly-through as a coda. There's no quiet logo end in the sampled frames: it closes on atmosphere (the yellow object), so the ending adds rather than subtracts.

## 7. What makes it premium
- The website's own layout (square bullets, staggered city column, masonry) is the storyboard; the film only adds cinematic joins between sections.
- **Dimensional switching:** flat web page → 3D racing void → 3D ticks → flat dial → 3D extruded type → point cloud. Each switch is motivated by the section's content.
- One meaningful number, animated as a **live clock** with a countdown, instead of a counter.
- A disciplined accent (Shopify orange-red) used for time, place and the one headline.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Spread-and-converge headline.** Place the first and last words of the headline at the far left/right, small, with corner registration dots; converge them to centre and scale ~3× in 0.5 s (expo-out).
2. **Flash + glitch decay.** A single-frame radial white burst on black, then return to the scene with RGB-split blocks on the key word that decay over 2 frames. Use once to open, once to close.
3. **Dimensional collapse to a dial.** 3D floating pills fly in along a curve (1 s), rotate flat and snap into the minute marks of a clock bezel (1 s); then an accent hand sweeps; then the outline numeral fills solid.
4. **Countdown numeral.** For a "every N seconds" claim, push into the numeral and tick it **down** by one while the hand passes vertical.
5. **Ground-cut deck.** Keep a fanned 3-card deck fixed at centre; hard-cut the ground colour and the photos every 0.5 s.
6. **Word-gap photo.** In a big type lockup, open a photo window between two words of one name ("New ▭ York"), growing from 0 to ~2 em over 0.5 s.
7. **Explode into fly-through.** Disintegrate the flat lockup into particles from its centre (0.5 s), then cut to a dark perspective grid where the same words are 3D extruded letters streaming past the camera.
