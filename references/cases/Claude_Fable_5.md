# Claude Fable 5: analysis (137 frames, ~68 s, square 1:1)

## 1. One-line concept
A model-launch teaser with no UI and no copy until the end. It's a **found-object numeral riffle**: ~45 archival and scientific images, each arranged so its contents form the digit **"5"**. It starts with unreadable macro crops and slow holds and speeds up to one image per 0.5 s. Then the last "5" (butterflies) flies apart into the title "Fable 5", followed by "Claude". The spine is **the number is hidden in everything humans have catalogued → the number → the name.**

## 2. Visual world
- **Ground:** there's no single plate. Each image brings its own paper: aged cream (#EDE3D3-ish), kraft tan, grey card, blueprint blue, black lab backgrounds. The ground only stops changing at the end, where it becomes Anthropic clay orange (#D9775A-ish) **with a faint graph-paper grid** (#109–124), then off-white graph paper (#125–135). The grid ground is the only "designed" surface in the film. It reads as a lab notebook, a place where the specimens get recorded.
- **Imagery:** everything is scanned or photographed as a specimen. The media run in roughly chronological order:
  1. chromolithograph natural-history plates: moths (#2–10), a jug and a portrait medallion (#19–24), raspberry (#25–29);
  2. engravings: a floor plan (#30–32), a barn cutaway (#33–35), crystals (#41–42);
  3. text as texture: a music score (#46), Latin blackletter (#51), an incunabulum (#54), Chinese woodblock (#55);
  4. cultural objects: flags, coins, stamps, playing cards (#47–59);
  5. cartography: a map coastline (#64), a Venice canal (#65);
  6. modern science: a cyanotype (#70, #86), fluorescence microscopy (#72, #81), diatoms (#74–75), X-ray of electronics (#78, #83), X-ray of shells (#82), petri-dish colonies (#80, #89–93).

  The collection moves from archive to lab.
- **Type:** only two words, in Anthropic's serif (a Tiempos/Copernicus-like display serif, regular weight). "Fable 5" is white on orange, cap height ~16% of frame height, ~65% of frame width, and optically centred. "Claude" is near-black on off-white at the same size. There's no grotesk, no mono, and no body copy.
- **Staging:** a flat top-down "copy stand" view of every image, with no perspective or shadows. The only depth effect is **motion-blur and defocus on objects that fly toward the lens** (#2, #106–108, #136).
- **Motif:** the numeral is the atom. The 5 is built from the objects themselves (coins in a 5, a snake in a 5, bacteria colonies in a 5, red dots on graph paper, #73). It's never drawn as a glyph until the title.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Hard cut in a fixed composition** (#46–88). Every image centres a 5 at the same scale, so the cuts read as one object changing material. This is the manual's "fixed-layout slideshow" or "centred anchor", except the anchor is a *shape* made of different content.
- **Decelerating-to-accelerating hold ladder** (#2–45). Holds of 9 → 8 → 6 → 5 → 3 → 3 → 3 → 2 → 2 → 1 → 2 → 1 frames. The macro crops hide the digit, so the viewer is never told what to look for. The first unambiguous 5 (#49) is the pay-off of ~24 s of set-up.
- **Reprise cut** (#84 repeats #68, #85 repeats #59, #94 repeats #61, #100 recalls #2's species). This lets the riffle loop back on itself before the exit.
- **Subject flies at the lens → wing wipe** (#105–109). The last specimen literally leaves the page. A foreground object crosses the lens and uncovers the title ground behind it. It's motivated because butterflies fly.
- **Hard cut, colour inversion** (#124→125): white-on-orange becomes black-on-white. The same serif, size and centre make it a lock-up swap, not a new scene.
- **Loop wrap** (#135→137→#2). The last frame is a butterfly macro, like the first, so the file can loop as a social post.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The opening **motion blur on a "printed" moth** (#2) tells you instantly that these plates are alive. It's 1 frame of blur on the wings only.
- Early holds have a **~1–3% drift**. The riffle frames have **zero** motion: the stillness is what makes 0.5 s cuts readable.
- **Negative-space 5s** alternate with **positive 5s**. The toile (#50), stamps (#58), cards (#59/#85) and blackletter (#51) carve the digit *out* of a field. The coins, snake and petri build it *from* objects. The alternation keeps the eye working.
- The digit's stance subtly matches between shots. The top bar is always at ~15–20% from the top and the bowl opens bottom-left, so hard cuts feel like morphs.
- **The media progression is an argument:** hand-coloured prints → text → maps → blueprints → microscopes → X-ray. It's the history of how humans make knowledge, ending in the lab. The title ground is graph paper, the lab's own surface.
- **Petal fall** (#96–99) is a secondary, delayed motion that signals "this composition is about to come apart" one shot before the butterflies actually do it.
- Butterflies leaving the title card **one at a time over 5 s** (#110–120) are the title's hold life. Nothing else moves.
- The title has **no reveal animation at all**. It's there fully formed the moment the wing clears (#110). Let the wipe do the reveal.

## 6. Pacing curve
Slow meditative open (4–4.5 s holds on unreadable macro). A 12-step deceleration of hold length over ~22 s. A ~21 s machine-gun riffle at a steady 2 cuts/s, with no acceleration inside it. One **2.5 s breath** (petri, #89–93) → a 3 s "decay" (petals) → a 4.5 s climax where the collection comes to life and flies at the camera → 7.5 s title hold → 5.5 s brand hold → loop. About 40% of the runtime is title and end-card holds (#109–135), far more than a SaaS film.

## 7. What makes it premium
- The number is **never typeset until the end**. Everything before the title is proof that the idea "5" can be found in any medium, which is a quiet metaphor for a general model.
- The curation is real (museum and science-archive imagery), and there's no stock-photo feel or generated imagery.
- It's restrained: no text, no logo, no music-video tricks during the riffle, only cuts. The single camera move of the second half is saved for the butterflies.
- The title ground is brand orange plus graph paper, so the title card is itself a specimen sheet.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Found-glyph riffle.** Build the key character (a version number, initial or logo) out of real objects in 20–40 different media. Centre each at an identical scale and stance, and hard-cut every 12–13 frames at 25 fps. Alternate positive and negative construction.
2. **Hidden-subject hold ladder.** Open on macro crops of the same compositions, too close to read, holding 4.5 s and then shrinking holds geometrically (≈ ×0.8 per shot) down to 0.5 s. The first legible instance is the reveal.
3. **Specimen flies at the lens.** The last composition's elements lift off in a stagger (1–2 per frame), scale 1 → 3× with growing motion blur, and one element crosses the full frame as a wipe, uncovering the title ground. Leave stragglers in the foreground to exit over 3–5 s as hold life.
4. **Reprise before release.** Repeat 2–4 earlier riffle images near the end (#84, #85, #94, #100). It signals the loop is closing, and the final image rhymes with the first.
5. **Pre-exit decay.** One shot before the big exit, let small parts of the composition fall away (petals, 1–3 per frame) as a secondary tell.
6. **Lock-up swap by inversion.** Product name on the brand colour → hard cut → company name on neutral, in the same face, size and centre. No animation.
7. **Loopable tail.** End on a defocused macro of the opening subject passing over the fading end card, so the social file loops seamlessly.
