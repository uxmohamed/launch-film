# Index by Parallel — manifesto film (187 sheet frames, source idx up to 192 ≈ 96 s)

Note: sheet labels are `#n (source idx)`; duplicate frames were dropped from #117 on, so timings below use the source index (t ≈ idx × 0.5 s).

## 1. One-line concept
Index (by Parallel) is a marketplace where creators get paid when AI agents use their content. The spine is a history-of-the-web essay told almost entirely with **one dot**: "The Internet started small. / GET / HTTP/1.0 / Vague but exciting… / It was nothing. / Freedom. Creativity. Connections. Research. / Open and limitless. / And then something interesting happened. / A new user showed up and changed how we used the web. / The old business models weren't built for this. / Opportunity is shrinking. / And the open internet is closing up. / Cures we couldn't see. Connections we couldn't make. Breakthroughs just beyond our reach. / But the economics that got us here won't get us there. / It's time for a new model. And that's why we built Index. / For the first time, creators can see what their content is worth to millions of agents and get paid accordingly. / The open web is a miracle. That miracle doesn't end here." Zero product UI. It's a manifesto with a logo.

## 2. Visual world
- **Two grounds, used as acts:** (a) warm near-black (#1c1a17-ish) with fine grain for "the early web / the void" (#1–46, #104–111); (b) **continuous-form printer paper**: light grey stock with tractor-feed sprocket holes on both edges (~1.5% of frame width in from each side), a horizontal perforation/crease line near the bottom, smudges and scanner dirt (#47–103, #124–187). (c) One **saturated orange** act (#112–122) for "what we're losing". The paper is the brand world: literally a print-out of the web.
- **Type:** one monospace (typewriter / terminal) at about **1.2–1.5% of frame height**, which is tiny even by this manual's standard. It is always set on the horizontal centre line, often *split into two halves either side of the dot* ("The Internet ● started small."). The only other type is the wordmark "INDEX BY PARALLEL" in a heavy grotesk caps (#142–151, #180), at ~4% height in the cascade and ~3% in the end lockup.
- **Carets:** a block caret `▌` on the dark ground (terminal, #10–12), an **I-beam `I`** on the paper (word processor, #49, #125–130, #167–173). The caret type changes with the era.
- **Emphasis:** hand-drawn pencil marks under or over single words: a wavy underline under "interesting happened" (#49–52), a scribble over "economics" (#125–128), an underline under "there" (#130–131). The handwritten annotation "Vague but exciting…" (Tim Berners-Lee's note on the 1989 CERN proposal) is set in handwriting over a ghosted scan of that proposal at ~10% opacity (#14–19).
- **Palette:** black, paper grey, one orange (#e8622a-ish) accent that is the "user/agent" colour, plus muted blue, sage green, beige and grey used only on the particle/dot populations.
- **Imagery:** 100% flat 2D dots, lines, circles, grids, particle fields. No photos, no UI, no 3D except an implied sphere (the polar grid).
- **Logomark:** a circle built from horizontal stripes whose left ends extend as speed-lines (#133–141), like a sphere passing at speed. The stripes shift horizontally frame to frame (#134→#136), so the mark is always moving.

## 4. Transition inventory
- **Backspace exit** (#9, #20–23, #41, #60, #129, #170): text leaves the way it came, deleted right-to-left, and the next line types into the same spot. It is the film's default text exit, used about 7 times. Motivated by "the web is written".
- **Dot as sentence hinge, then as actor:** the dot sits between the two halves of every key sentence (#5–9, #47–52, #113–121, #152–166), and between sentences it *becomes* the scene: it grows into a node (#13), collapses to a point and explodes (#24→#28), falls and splashes into a ground (#107→#112), and becomes the stripe logo (#133).
- **Collapse-to-point → big bang** (#24–28): shrink in 3 steps (~0.5 s each), one empty frame, then a full-frame particle burst.
- **Hard cut dark → paper** (#46→#47): the era change, with the dot carrying continuity.
- **Gravity collapse** (#78): the grid of dots (the old model) loses structure and falls. Physics as a verb transition for "broken".
- **Iris-close on the verb** (#98–103): the paper ground itself contracts into a shrinking disc exactly while the text says "closing up", and dark ground shows at the corners. This is the inverse of "dot grows into ground" and is word-motivated.
- **Drop → splash flood** (#107–112): a falling dot draws a thread, a liquid crown rises from the bottom edge, and orange floods the frame.
- **In-place disc swap** (#113–121): a fixed-layout slideshow, where only the disc's content changes between clauses.
- **Zoom-through the polar grid** (#121–123) into grey, then a cut to paper.
- **One-frame ink splatter** (#132): a rhythmic accent before the logo.
- **Typing-history cascade collapse** (#142–151).
- **Density-ramp pull-back** (#157–166).
- **Paper-feed advance** (#177): the sheet scrolls up like a printer to "print" the end card. It's the only move of the paper ground in the film.

## 5. Easy-to-miss details
- The **dot grows with the sentence** (#3→#9: 1 px → ~16 px), so the dot is a progress bar for the line.
- In the split layout the left half is right-aligned and the right half left-aligned to the dot, and the gap is **not** fixed: when the right half deletes, the left half doesn't move (#9).
- **Caret type encodes era:** block `▌` for the 1991 terminal, I-beam for today's editor. The handwritten ellipsis grows one dot per frame (#16–18).
- **The pill label in the particle field is dark and opaque**, so the white mono text stays readable over a busy field. The pill widens per word (#31–35) like the manual's word-append line, but boxed.
- **Particle parallax:** foreground dots are 10–20× larger than background dots and move faster; the explosion centre clears first (#36–40) so text can live there.
- **Pencil annotation as emphasis** instead of colour or weight: a wavy underline, scribble or plain underline draws on 0.5–1 s after the phrase finishes.
- **Colour shimmer on a dot matrix** (#69–77): 3–5 random cells per frame flip to orange/grey. It keeps a static grid alive.
- The fallen pill lands **tilted** and stays tilted under the next line (#79–82), so the aftermath persists.
- In the iris-close, the text *types inside* the shrinking disc and stays centred, and the disc's edge carries the paper's grain.
- The orange act has a **soft radial glow** (#112, lighter bottom-centre) where the splash landed.
- The cascade rows **mirror symmetrically** above and below the full row, reading as an audio waveform or a Rolodex of spellings.
- The end lockup rises from below-centre (#178 → #180), so it arrives from the direction the paper just fed.

## 6. Pacing curve
Slow, even and essay-like: nearly every beat is 2.5–5 s and text is on screen for 2–4 s per line. The open is silent (1 s empty, 3 s of the dot growing). The first energy spike is the particle explosion at ~14 s. There's a long, calm paper act (24–52 s) with medium spikes on the network burst and the gravity collapse. The dark trough is the iris close → 1.5 s black (~51–55 s): the film's breath. The climax is the colour flood into orange (~56 s), followed by the zoom-through, the splatter flash (one frame) and the logo at ~67 s (70% of runtime). Then a second, product-level act (creators → agents density ramp), a quiet coda line, and a 3.5 s end-card hold. The logo appears twice: as a mark mid-film (with the claim) and as the lockup at the end.

## 7. What makes it premium
- **One primitive does everything:** the dot is a word gap, a node, a star, a user, a drop, a creator and a logo. The film has no stock transitions at all.
- **The ground is a metaphor** (continuous-form printer paper = the web as a document), and it's persistent. Even the final move is the paper feeding.
- **Text has a lifecycle:** typed with a period-correct caret, annotated in pencil, and deleted by backspace. The film never fades text out.
- **Restraint in type size** (~1.3% of frame height) gives the abstract graphics all the scale; the only large type is the name cascade.
- **Verb-literal geometry:** "closing up" closes an iris, "weren't built for this" collapses under gravity, "shrinking" drains colour, "millions of agents" multiplies dots.

## 8. Reusable techniques
1. **Hinge dot sentence.** Split each key line into two halves with the brand dot at frame centre. Left half right-aligned, right half left-aligned, gap ~3–4% of frame width. The dot grows with the line. Change only the dot's contents between clauses (spots → network → polar grid) for a triptych. About 2.5 s per clause.
2. **Backspace exit.** Delete text right-to-left at ~2× typing speed (~6 chars per 0.5 s), stop on a shared prefix, and type the next line from there ("It was nothing." → "It was not" → next). Linear timing, with the caret visible throughout.
3. **Iris-close on the word.** When the copy says shrink/close/narrow, turn the ground into a disc and scale it from 130% to 40% of frame height over ~3 s (ease-out, ~×0.85 per 0.5 s) with the line typing inside, then cut to black for 1.5 s.
4. **Gravity collapse.** A structured grid (the old model) drops under rigid-body physics in ~1 s and piles along the bottom edge. The label pill tumbles and lands tilted. The next line types above the wreckage.
5. **Typing-history cascade.** For the name reveal, stack 12–16 rows of the name at every typing stage, mirror them around a complete centre row, scroll vertically for ~3 s, then collapse to the centre row in ~1 s.
6. **Pencil emphasis.** A hand-drawn underline or scribble under one word, drawn on in 0.3–0.5 s about 0.5 s after the phrase completes. Use it instead of colour or bold.
7. **Collapse → big bang.** Shrink the atom in 3 steps (0.5 s each), hold one empty frame, then burst a particle field with 10–20× size parallax; clear the centre within 2 s for type.
8. **Paper-feed end.** Advance the ground sheet upward (perforation line travelling up) to deliver the end card from below.
