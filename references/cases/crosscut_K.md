# Crosscut K: Claude Fable 5, Claude Fable 5.1, Claude keynote intro, Instagram Brand System, Reve

Three of these five are Anthropic/Claude films with no UI at all (Fable 5, Fable 5.1, keynote intro). One is a brand-system film (Instagram), and one is a short feature teaser (Reve). Together they show how top studios make launch films **when there is no product screen to show**, or when they refuse to show one.

## 1. Patterns that recur

1. **The 2 fps riffle is the backbone.** Four of the five use a sustained run of one image per 0.5 s:
   - Fable 5: ~21 s, #46–88;
   - Fable 5.1: ~17 s in the eyepiece, #41–71;
   - keynote: ~5.5 s of costumes, #76–86;
   - Instagram: 2/s gradient and photo flicks, #85–86 and #91–93;
   - Reve: 3.5 s of tools, #1–7.

   It's always held together by **one invariant**: a centred digit shape, a circular aperture, a centred mascot body, a fixed tool-tip point, or a fixed bar position. The content changes and the silhouette or anchor doesn't.
2. **The anchor is a shape, not a dot.** The manual's "centred anchor" is a 3–4% dot. Here the anchor is a *silhouette* (the digit 5), a *vignette* (the eyepiece circle), a *character* (the mascot) or a *tip point* (the pens). Any constant geometry turns hard cuts into morphs.
3. **Bookend loops for social.** Both Fable films end on a frame that re-enters their first frame: a butterfly macro in Fable 5 (#136–137 → #2), and the same branch and bird in Fable 5.1 (#125–138 → #1). The keynote bookends caret → caret (#2–4 / #92). Instagram bookends glyph → wordmark and glyph (#1 / #122). The loop *is* the ending.
4. **The type layer is minimal and arrives late.** Neither Fable film shows a single word for the first ~75% of its runtime (the Fable 5 title at 80%, #110; the Fable 5.1 title at 67%, #92). The keynote has no words until its last 1 s. Titles are small and quiet: Fable 5.1's cap height is ~7% of frame height.
5. **Hand layer over clean system.** Instagram has ovals, underlines, a strike-through and a handwritten slot word. Reve has a marker-stroke "annotations", a sun/moon doodle and a handwritten "draw". Fable 5 has hand-coloured plates. The keynote has hand-pixelled sprites. The human mark is always on a pristine ground.
6. **Blur as the dominant "soft" transition.** Fable 5.1 uses a blur-in title (3 frames), fades by occlusion under defocused clouds and branches, and defocuses on the eyepiece exit. Reve's generate is a blur-bloom (~40 px → 0). In Fable 5, objects flying at the lens motion-blur into a wipe.
7. **Life during end holds comes from the world, not from keyframes.** In Fable 5, butterflies leave the title one by one over 5 s. In Fable 5.1, the camera tilt never stops under the title and logo. In the keynote, the sun rises during the lock-up. There's no 3–5% push on any title.
8. **Dimensional chapter coding without title cards.** Chapters are coded by ground: Instagram alternates black and cream, Fable 5 goes from specimen paper to an orange grid to a white grid, and Fable 5.1 goes from outside to inside the instrument to outside.

## 2. Contradictions between the films

- **Continuous camera vs. pure cuts.** Fable 5.1 is essentially one camera move (push in, scroll, pull out, tilt) that also contains cuts. Fable 5 has *zero* camera motion during its riffle. Why: 5.1 is "look closer", so the camera *is* the verb. Fable 5 is "find the 5", so stillness is needed to read each composition in 0.5 s.
- **Title reveal.** Fable 5's title has *no* animation and is simply uncovered by a wing wipe. Fable 5.1 blur-ins its title. Instagram writes its wordmark on with a mask. Reve's "draw" is written by hand. All four end marks are different, but each matches that film's verb (uncover / observe / write / draw).
- **End hold.** Fable 5 holds 13 s of title and brand (#110–135). Instagram's lock-up gets ~1.5 s, and the keynote's ~0.5 s before cutting to the live stage. Social-loop teasers hold long, while intros and system films hand off quickly.
- **Real vs. redrawn.** The keynote and Reve redraw everything (a sprite world, stylised UI tools). The Fable films use only real archival or scientific imagery and never draw anything.

## 4. vs. the manual

### (a) Techniques NOT in the manual (name + recipe)
1. **Found-glyph riffle** (Fable 5). Build the key glyph (a version number) *out of* real objects in 20–40 media, positive and negative. Use an identical centre, scale and stance, and hard-cut at 2/s with zero camera motion. It's a riffle that carries a message instead of "taste". The manual's A12 montage riffle says "nothing to read". This one has exactly one thing to read, and it's the same in every frame.
2. **Hidden-subject hold ladder** (Fable 5 #2–45). Open on unreadably tight macro crops of the same compositions and shrink the holds geometrically (×0.8) from 4.5 s to 0.5 s. The viewer discovers the pattern at the first legible frame (~24 s in).
3. **Instrument portal** (Fable 5.1 #6–8, #76–79). Push through a physical aperture (an eyepiece) in 1–2 blurred frames. Keep the ring as a permanent vignette for 30+ s, and exit by pull-back plus rim defocus, landing on the *real* version of the last thing seen (a lunar photo → the Moon in the sky at the same frame position).
4. **Shape-rhyme chain** (Fable 5.1 #37–71). Order hard cuts by *line structure* (leaf veins → rivers → PCB traces) or by *radial form* (circle inside circle), not by colour or subject.
5. **Passenger type on a moving world** (Fable 5.1 #92–124). The title and logo are screen-locked while the camera tilts continuously behind them. They exit by being occluded and faded under passing foreground (clouds, defocused branches), not by an animation of their own.
6. **Specimen flies at the lens → wipe** (Fable 5 #100–109). The last composition's parts take off in a stagger and fly toward camera. One fills the frame and uncovers the title ground, and stragglers drift out over 5 s as hold life.
7. **Pre-exit decay** (Fable 5 #96–99). One shot before the collection comes apart, small pieces (petals) fall off. It's a secondary tell for the coming release.
8. **Caret-born mascot** (keynote #2–7). The terminal caret blinks, squashes to an underscore, then inflates into the character. The manual has "punctuation → logo", but not "caret → character". In reverse, the caret types the lock-up at the end.
9. **Costume riffle / fixed-star world** (keynote). The character is pixel-identical and centred, and ground, sets, rings and props are built around it. Then hard-cut costumes at 2/s on an almost empty ground.
10. **Physical-to-UI tool match** (Reve #1–10). Real photographed tools are anchored at one point, then 2 frames of white, then the stylised UI tool rises into the same point at ~3× scale.
11. **Blur-bloom generate** (Reve #38–41). On submit, the frame floods with a defocused, zoomed version of the reference image for ~1 s, then focus-pulls to the result. It's a non-spinner "thinking" state.
12. **Self-editing title cards** (Reve #51–59). The product's UI (a chip "Invert this graphic", a marquee) acts on the film's own kinetic type, so the type card becomes the demo canvas.
13. **Doodle → render** (Reve #44–45, #48). A crude hand-drawn symbol is replaced by its photoreal effect in one frame.
14. **Annotation arrival state** (Instagram #9, #25, #116). A hand-drawn oval or underline drawn in ~2 frames marks the key word. This is a 6th arrival-state option alongside blur, ghost, accent, inverted block and scramble.
15. **Split-mask identity comparison** (Instagram #13–16). A vertical divider with the old form as a ghost outline on one side and the new solid form on the other, while the camera trucks across the word at ~2.5×.
16. **Handwritten slot word with strike-through** (Instagram #105–109). A fixed sans phrase with one handwritten variable word. Options change by being struck out by hand rather than by rolling.
17. **Imperfect typing** (Instagram #100–103). A deliberate typo and backspace inside a typed message, used when the message is about imperfection.
18. **Material flip** (Reve #26–27). The same 3D title word swaps material in 1 frame (rainbow chrome → silver).

### (b) Where these films CONTRADICT the manual
- **"Hold final card 2–5 s."** Fable 5 holds the title plus brand for ~13 s (#110–135, ~20% of runtime). Instagram and the keynote hold ≤1.5 s. The rule should depend on the format: social loops hold long, and stage intros and brand-system films cut out fast.
- **"Holds breathe (3–5% push)."** Fable 5's 21 s riffle frames have *zero* motion (#46–88), and its title is static (#110–123). The life comes from **world elements leaving** (butterflies), not from camera drift. Stillness is correct when each frame is on screen for only 0.5 s.
- **"Accelerate by removing reading" / "A12 riffle: nothing to read."** Fable 5's riffle *is* the reading: every frame holds a readable digit for 0.5 s, and it works because the thing to read is the same in every frame.
- **"Speed up toward a climax."** Fable 5's riffle has constant cadence for ~21 s, and its climax is a *slowdown* (2.5 s petri, 3 s geranium, 4.5 s butterflies). The climax of energy comes from the first camera move after a long stillness, not from faster cuts.
- **"Every transition has a cause (click, keystroke, product verb)."** In the Fable films the causes are natural events (a bird takes off, a butterfly flies at the lens, clouds occlude). For model launches with no UI, **nature's own motion is the cause**.
- **"Front-load proof / logo by 20–35% in long films."** Both ~68 s Fable films hide *all* branding until 67–80% of the runtime. Brand teasers save the name even at 60+ s.
- **"Type is small (1.5–3.5%)."** Instagram's captions sit at 4–5% and Reve's shouts at ~22%. Brand and feature films run bigger than the manual's SaaS numbers, but they still keep one small/large contrast.

### (c) Manual claims these films strongly confirm
- **Brand atom as the transition engine:** the keynote caret (open, birth, lock-up), the Instagram glyph plus corner brackets plus bezier handles, the Fable 5.1 circle aperture, and the Fable 5 digit.
- **Centred anchor makes hard cuts read as morphs:** Reve's tool tips, the keynote mascot, the Fable 5 digit and the Instagram glyph (#1–5).
- **Deconstructed UI, no screenshots:** Instagram shows icons, stickers, pills and a viewfinder but never an app screen. Reve shows the tool palette built piece by piece, and only overlays chips and marquees on photos.
- **Text → UI / type is the timeline:** Instagram's sentence introduces each system element in the face being introduced. Reve's title cards become canvases.
- **One accent colour means something:** the keynote mascot is the only orange object in 46 s, Instagram's gradient is used only on the bar, and the Fable titles use only brand clay orange as a ground.
- **Ground becomes card / scene → card:** Instagram #66–68 (cream full frame → bordered card → layout canvas).
- **End by subtraction:** Reve ends on a tiny pixel butterfly on black, the keynote dims its world to 30% under the caret, and Fable 5 clears the butterflies before the title settles.
- **Slot reel for variants:** Instagram "post / share / make it anyway", keeping the phrase fixed and swapping one word.
